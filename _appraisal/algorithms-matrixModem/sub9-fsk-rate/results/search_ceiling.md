# Sub-9 kHz FSK rate and spacing: affordable against needed hypotheses, by tone length

Generated by `search_ceiling.py` from the measured decode times in `results/decode_bench.md` and
`results/gpu/`. Carrier 8,270 Hz, span 12.5 Hz. One hypothesis is one decode of all 2^k messages on the
full window ladder. Counting rules of calculation 18 of `../fsk-lattice` plus the 1/(8 x span) timing rule.
Theoretical apart from the measured times. GPU figures for 16 and 10 bits are scaled from its 24-bit
measurement by the message count; every CPU figure is measured for its own block size.

## 1. What one decode costs, and how many fit into the block's own duration

An 87.4-minute block holds 32,768 / 8,192 / 2,048 / 512 / 128 tones of 0.16 / 0.64 / 2.56 / 10.24 / 40.96 s,
the tone counts that were timed. The full ladder costs one lookup per message and tone, so a longer block
at the same tone length takes longer in proportion and the number that fits into it stays the same.

## 2. Hypotheses needed

Frequency bins x drift-rate bins x timing offsets for a whole-block coherent window. The sample clock is
taken from the same reference as the carrier, so its rate follows the frequency hypothesis. Timing offsets
are the larger of the timing uncertainty over an eighth of the tone (per-tone reception) and over
1/(8 x span) = 10 ms for the 12.5 Hz span (coherent sums across positions). The second rule governs for
every tone length here, so the count does not depend on the tone.

timing uncertainty times the span. A span of 25 or 50 Hz doubles or quadruples the timing offsets.

## 4. The same margin if the frequency bins come from a transform across tone sums

Derived, not measured. A transform across a message's tone (or short-window) sums gives every frequency
bin inside +-1/(4 T) of the tone length T at once, at a cost taken here as 20 plain decodes (an assumption).
Frequency then needs ceil(4 F T) such decodes in place of 4 F D plain ones; drift and timing are unchanged.
