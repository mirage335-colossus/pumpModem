## 3. Trails that deliver one tone's worth of energy, and how often

A burst is counted as usable when its whole energy over the noise density reaches 12 dB, about what one
256-value tone needs. Burst energy = peak C/N0 x (decay time / 2). `q needed` is the line density that
reaches it; `none` means it exceeds 2e14, so no underdense trail does. Rate: 450 trails an hour above
q = 3.8e+12 on the anchor link, scaled as 1/q; the anchor's 17 dBi beams and 1300 km path are kept for every row,

## 4. Average signal energy from all underdense trails

With N(q > q0) = K/q0, the energy arriving per second is K (E14 / 1e28) (q_max - q_min), where E14 is the
burst energy over N0 at q = 1e14. It is dominated by the strongest trails. `equivalent C/N0` is that
energy rate with q_max = 2e14: the level of a steady signal delivering the same energy. A receiver that
adds tones as energies gets less than this from the many weak bursts. 50 MHz, 1300 km.
