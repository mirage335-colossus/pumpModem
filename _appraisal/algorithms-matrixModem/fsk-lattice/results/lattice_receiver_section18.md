# Matrix Modem FSK lattice: receiver calculations

Generated by `lattice_receiver.cpp`. Complex baseband at 3200 Hz, cell = 512 samples = 160 ms, bin = 6.25 Hz.
Dwell period D with nc whole active cells starting 40 ms after the dwell start: A 0.4 s / 2 cells,
B 4 s / 24 cells, C 40 s / 249 cells. Phase and fading are independent from dwell to dwell. Known timing
and Doppler centre, Gaussian noise, no interference, ideal coding. Theoretical calculation.

## 18. Below 9 kHz: how many hypotheses coherent reception has to try

Carrier 9 kHz, FHSS off, one coherent window of length T. Counted per window: frequency bins, drift-rate
bins and timing offsets, each combination one block decode. The sample clock is taken from the same
reference as the carrier, so its rate follows the frequency hypothesis; the last column of 18c is the
extra factor if it does not. Theoretical; the oscillator classes are assumptions, not measurements.

### 18a. Spacing rules

Frequency: a tone off by x bins of 1/T keeps sinc^2(x).

| bin spacing | worst loss (dB) | mean loss (dB) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 / T | 3.92 | 1.11 |
| 0.5 / T | 0.91 | 0.29 |
| 0.25 / T | 0.22 | 0.07 |

Drift: a frequency that moves by d bins of 1/T across the window, linearly, keeps the value below after the
best fixed frequency is fitted.

| residual drift across the window (bins) | loss (dB) |
|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | 0.06 |
| 1.0 | 0.24 |
| 2.0 | 0.97 |
| 4.0 | 4.04 |

Drift hypotheses 3.05 bins apart lose at most 0.56 dB (the timing rule's worst case); 3.88 apart at most 0.91 dB
(the worst case of half-bin frequency spacing). The tables use half-bin frequency spacing and the 3.88 rule.

decodes a second as a direct kernel; about 65 a second for the coherent ladder by extrapolation. `x` marks
