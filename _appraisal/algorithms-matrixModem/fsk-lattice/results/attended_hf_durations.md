## 20. HF per-tone sizing with whole blocks and hops

Use the large-alphabet spread-matched per-tone metric and bin span of section 13b.
Compute each rectangular aperture's bin powers at all 3,200-Hz sample lags. The test template
has the correct Doppler centre and spread. A = 400 is the assumed count of searched
nuisance alternatives per word. Count all 1,024 ten-bit words. The three per-tone
family allowances are 1e-6 (historical), 1e-4 (full attended budget) and 5e-5
(half of 1e-4, as in the two-branch experiment). At true-template miss 1e-3 their
Gaussian deflections are 99.9835, 86.7330 and 88.7529. The other branch receives the remainder
in the half-budget case. This table sizes reception by the per-tone branch alone.

For B flat tone slots per hop and d² = 2 × per-tone GMI, use
L = max(B, ceil(S / (32 d²))) repeats per symbol, N = 32L transmitted tones and
H = ceil(N / B) occupied hops. Each new block starts at a hop boundary. Charge the
whole last period if only partially filled. Any unused slots there contribute zero.
The floor supplies 32 full hops' worth of data in both profiles. One-cell tones span
0.16 s, and a held tone fills both or all 24 flat cells of a 0.4 s or 4 s hop.
The edges carry no scored energy. Width is the full two-standard-deviation width of
a Gaussian Doppler spectrum. The steady row uses a line at zero offset.

The additive d² proxy assumes equally useful independent tone observations. Its Gaussian
tail and the substitution d² = 2 GMI are weak-tone approximations. Strong tones (the
historical screen flags d² > 0.5), few independent looks, shared within-hop fading,
finite codes and score quantization can change the required duration. No interference,
erased hops, decoding margin, competing-word argmax or quiet-slot completion is modeled.
Times are wall seconds for a scheduled data block at fixed flat-interval received power.
