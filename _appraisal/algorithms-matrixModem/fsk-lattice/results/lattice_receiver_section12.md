# Matrix Modem FSK lattice: receiver calculations

Generated by `lattice_receiver.cpp`. Complex baseband at 3200 Hz, cell = 512 samples = 160 ms, bin = 6.25 Hz.
Dwell period D with nc whole active cells starting 40 ms after the dwell start: A 0.4 s / 2 cells,
B 4 s / 24 cells, C 40 s / 249 cells. Phase and fading are independent from dwell to dwell. Known timing
and Doppler centre, Gaussian noise, no interference, ideal coding. Theoretical calculation.

## 12. A keyed phase in every cell of a dwell

Each 160 ms cell of a dwell is transmitted with a keyed phase of 0, 90, 180 or 270 degrees. The receiver
removes it by an exact quarter turn of each cell's values before it combines the cells of a dwell. The
rotation has unit modulus, so with exact timing the tones stay orthogonal over whole cells, the noise
statistics are unchanged, and every earlier figure for the keyed receiver holds.

### 12a. What the keyed receiver pays: timing

A receive window displaced by tau holds tau of the neighbouring cell, whose phase is unrelated, so the
coherent amplitude is 1 - tau/160 ms. A plain tone is not free of this either: its window then holds tau
of ramp, quiet or the next dwell, so its amplitude is 1 - tau/dwell (section 12e). An earlier version of
this section said a plain tone loses nothing inside the guard and compared against a 40 ms grid; that
holds only for the 0.4 s dwell.

| timing error (ms) | 2.5 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 | 80 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| loss (dB) | 0.14 | 0.28 | 0.56 | 1.16 | 2.50 | 6.02 |

| timing grid step (ms) | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| worst loss (dB) | 0.14 | 0.28 | 0.56 | 1.16 |
| mean loss (dB) | 0.07 | 0.14 | 0.27 | 0.55 |

A propagation path delayed by d against the timing reference loses the same fraction of its own power:
0.03 dB at 0.5 ms, 0.11 dB at 2 ms, 0.39 dB at 7 ms.

### 12b. What an observer without the key loses

Link: the 32-dwell block of section 11 on a steady path (E/N0 = 20.12 dB, 3.21 per dwell). The observer
knows the lattice, the band and the cell and dwell timing. Criterion as in 11d: detect the block with
probability 0.9 at a false-alarm rate of 1e-2. `margin` is the extra C/N0 it needs over the link.
Observer statistics, all computed with exact chi-square tails unless marked:

- `line`: plain tone. The largest dwell-long bin over all cells and dwells. Section 11d found the best
  statistic 0.6 to 1.7 dB better than this largest-bin detector (1.6, 3.8 and 5.7 dB).
- `blob`: keyed phase. Cell energies added over a dwell for each position (2m degrees of freedom for m
  cells), then the largest over positions and dwells.
- `energy`: keyed phase. All cell energies of the block added (Gaussian deflection).
- `square`: the phase key is binary. Cell values are squared and added over the dwell, which restores a
  line (Gaussian sum over cells, then the `line` requirement).
- `fourth`: the phase key is quaternary. The same with fourth powers.

`square` and `fourth` need many cells per dwell for their Gaussian sum and are not given for 2 cells.

### 12d. Few positions without FHSS: the line a plain tone leaves

Without FHSS and without a phase key, every visit to a position continues the same carrier, so each of P
positions holds a steady spectral line of 1/P^2 of the power. An observer that integrates one line
coherently for the block time, with no knowledge of key or timing, needs 20 log10(P) dB more C/N0 than a
receiver that is coherent over the block. A quaternary phase key leaves no line.

| positions P | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 352 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| line power (dB below the signal) | 6.0 | 12.0 | 18.1 | 24.1 | 50.9 |

### 12e. Timing search: what sets the spacing, clock drift, and the phase interval

A timing error tau costs (1 - tau/T)^2 of the power, where T is the stretch over which the receiver is
coherent with one known phase: the flat part of the dwell for a plain tone, the interval between phase
steps with the phase key. Hypotheses spaced T/8 lose 0.56 dB at worst and 0.27 dB on average; T/16 loses
0.28 and 0.14 dB. The spacing is therefore a fraction of T, not a fixed time. All figures below use T/8.

A tone that changes position every 160 ms with no guard (one cell per dwell) already has T = 160 ms, and
a phase key adds nothing to its search.

Spacing, and timing hypotheses per search dimension against the plain tone:

| dwell | plain tone | phase step every cell | every 2 cells | every 4 cells | every 8 cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4 s | 40 ms | 20 ms, 2x | - | - | - |
| 4 s | 480 ms | 20 ms, 24x | 40 ms, 12x | 80 ms, 6x | 160 ms, 3x |
| 40 s | 5.0 s | 20 ms, 249x | 40 ms, 124x | 80 ms, 62x | 160 ms, 31x |
| 400 s | 50 s | 20 ms, 2499x | 40 ms, 1250x | 80 ms, 625x | 160 ms, 312x |
| 4000 s | 8 min | 20 ms, 24999x | 40 ms, 12500x | 80 ms, 6250x | 160 ms, 3125x |

Frequency error and drift of the oscillators add nothing: the phase key is removed cell by cell before
the frequency search. Sample-clock error does: a clock off by e slips e x (block time) over the block, and
one timing hypothesis has to hold over the block, so the timing rate is a second search dimension with
about 2 e (block time) / spacing hypotheses. With offset and rate both uncertain the factors multiply.
