# Intact Matrix FSK under antenna pointing loss

A constant loss of transmitting antenna gain attenuates every tone equally. This test varies the received energy of an intact, correctly oriented 10-bit emission while holding receiver noise fixed. It models a narrowband link with constant gain over a block. A physical angle would require an antenna pattern. [Waveform and receiver geometry](README.md#coherent-branch-and-protected-per-tone-fallback)

## Protocol fixed before sampling

Use a single stable path, 3,200 complex samples/s, 64 contiguous 160 ms tones on the 352-position HF grid and eight configured public schedules with FHSS disabled. The experiment knows the ordinal and nominal slot start, searches all 1,024 possible words, and gets neither the transmitted word nor an exact timing or carrier correction. Actual offsets are randomly ±32 samples and ±0.25 cycles per 10.24 s block. Unknown initial phase is uniform. The ordinary weak-signal decision combines the single-orientation per-tone and coherent branches, with per-tone admission first. The historical no-argument regression exercises per-tone energy alone. Neither experiment is a production Matrix receiver. [Native ordinary decision](block_reception.cpp)

Relative to the 30 dB reference these are pointing losses of 18, 15, 13.5, 12, 9, 6, 4.5, 3 and 0 dB. Receiver noise and the transmitted word stay the same across a pair. Score the corresponding noise-only buffer before adding any signal.

Each rectangular 512-sample row lasts 0.16 s with bin width 6.25 Hz. The ordinary display cutoff is 9.9517550479 (nominal 1% indication in Gaussian quiet). Also report a more stringent indication at 10.431464751805 (the maximum of 39,999 training noise blocks). All thresholds are strict. The display has no receiver decision authority. Descriptive intervals at other grid points are pointwise. Stop after the fixed grid and report any adverse result. Do not estimate a continuous dB threshold from the grid.
4. Run 64 fresh five-block streams at 18 dB (seed `0x706f6903`) with two adjacent transmitted words, two physical quiet blocks containing noise and a new word on the continuing configured calendar. At 16.5 dB, the receiver recovered 157/160 (pointwise 95% Wilson interval 94.6–99.4%) and the ordinary waterfall flagged 0/160 (0–2.3%). At 24 dB, the receiver recovered 160/160 (97.7–100%) and the ordinary waterfall flagged 104/160 (57.3–72.0%). At 25.5 dB the ordinary waterfall flagged 158/160 (95.6–99.7%). The first tested levels reaching 90% were 16.5 dB for correct words and 25.5 dB for the ordinary waterfall. This nine-decibel spacing describes the sampled grid. At 27 dB, 0/160 visible records failed to yield the correct word, giving an exact one-sided 95% upper bound of **1.855%** for the primary conditional failure probability. With just two visible at 18 dB the corresponding bound is 77.6%. Pairing across levels precludes treating their counts as independent trials.

Each noise-only twin stayed below both display cutoffs and produced a scored absence. These 160 twins add little precision about rare events. On the earlier independent 30,000 quiet holdouts, the ordinary single-orientation receiver using the original full-precision cutoffs admitted **1** false word (exact one-sided 95% upper bound 1.58 × 10⁻⁴, above the 10⁻⁴ target), and the ordinary waterfall flagged **267** records. The present run uses the published decimal cutoffs from that calibration. The waterfall statistic measures a schedule-blind feature of this particular display. Human recognition, displays with other integration times and off-axis antenna patterns were unmeasured. [Original quiet thresholds and holdout](fault-tests.md#false-words-at-the-attended-target)

At 18 dB all **64/64** adjacent-word episodes returned their exact five decisions (pointwise 95% Wilson interval 94.3–100% for an exact episode). The ordinary receiver returned all 192 active words, admitted zero false words in the 128 quiet slots, closed on the second quiet in all 64 episodes and reopened on the next scheduled word in all 64. Within each episode these outcomes share a calendar, channel and overlapping observation guards. This tests delivery and closure on an existing public time and frequency grid.
