2026-10-07 / Codex: oscillator-aware Robust search and long-symbol efficiency
==========================================================================

Improve the Robust Modem receiver so its actual bounded iterative search uses
the selected LF/audio and RF oscillator models and a conservative uncertainty
margin. Remove unnecessary dependence of the expensive encrypted pattern search
on the audio carrier frequency or the way an equivalent hardware-radio carrier
is specified. This is a future implementation prompt, not a claim that the
changes below are implemented, measured or qualified. Recheck the current source
and preserve the development contract before beginning implementation.

Scope and exclusions
--------------------
Target stationary links with sufficiently stable oscillators and bounded residual
frequency/timing uncertainty over the complete symbol. Do not add Doppler drift
following, adaptive real-time synchronization loops, drift-rate/acceleration
hypotheses, or a broad iterative search over Doppler trajectories. Real-time
following can diverge, and comprehensive trajectory searches can be prohibitively
expensive; neither is an acceptable solution for this work. Do not disguise such
a search expansion as oscillator margin. Preserve existing pattern-evidence
acquisition/refinement and detector behavior; report unsupported stability or
coverage honestly rather than chasing a changing signal or claiming useful
dynamic-Doppler coverage.

Controls and physical meaning
-----------------------------
Add an "RF frequency shift" dropdown, default 0 Hz, with suitable frequency
choices and validated custom entry. It describes the known frequency translation
between the delivered real-sample passband and the on-air spectrum, whether in
an external converter or the radio itself. It is not residual tuning error,
signal bandwidth or an instruction to retune hardware. Zero covers direct audio,
VLF/sub-9 kHz and untranslated operation. An HF radio/converter can supply a shift
in megahertz while the modem still generates and receives a real waveform.
Define the sign/sideband convention and distinguish the radio's LO/tuning
frequency from the actual on-air modem carrier. Never substitute either RF
quantity for the delivered modem carrier in the PCM sample-rate planner.

Add an "Oscillator search margin" dropdown, default 3x, including 2x and 3x
choices. Validate finite multipliers of at least 1x. Show the assumed accuracy,
requested frequency half-width in Hz, sample-clock allowance in ppm, and actual
covered region. Persist these controls and both oscillator selections through
the existing configuration and CLI/GUI command import/export paths. Apply them
to real reception as well as simulation and planning; they must not be
simulation-only presentation settings.

Provide separate "LF / audio oscillator model" and "RF conversion oscillator
model" selections. Each describes its own frequency accuracy and phase stability
over the intended observation interval; do not use one oscillator-quality choice
to silently improve both stages. The LF model represents the actual audio
carrier/timebase and ADC/DAC sampling behavior, while the RF model represents the
radio/conversion references and synthesizers at the translated frequency.
For untranslated audio with no hardware RF stage, the extra RF contribution is
inactive. In shared radio-reference mode the RF model also accounts for that
radio's ADC/DAC clock; an LF=0 indicator disables only an additional independent
LF oscillator contribution, not the sample-clock uncertainty or actual tone.
Support an explicitly declared shared reference without assuming it by default
or double-counting its correlated errors. Retain effective relative TX/RX model
semantics, or explicitly combine separately specified endpoint models.

Ideal upconversion followed by downconversion removes the nominal RF carrier,
but independent TX/RX oscillator phase variations do not cancel by symmetry.
The remaining RF phase is their difference (at the appropriate delayed times),
apart from constant propagation phase. Unknown constant phase is already fitted;
a constant frequency difference belongs in the bounded frequency search. Model
residual phase variation after those fits separately from the search half-width.
Do not reduce RF phase fluctuations by the audio/RF frequency ratio on
downconversion. Combine independent phase-error variances consistently and retain
shared-reference correlations. A sufficiently stable RF stage may contribute
negligible error compared with audio, but establish that over the actual symbol
duration rather than assuming GPS lock provides unlimited phase coherence.

Distinguish GPS time tagging of the computer, GPS-based audio-rate calibration,
and hardware disciplining of the sound card's sample clock. A GPS receiver/PPS
can support a hobbyist clock reference or calibration system; GPS synchronization
of system time alone does not discipline an independent ADC/DAC oscillator.
Hardware clock discipline requires a compatible clock input/control path. Where
that is impractical, investigate measured sample-rate calibration and resampling
against an independently observed local GPS reference, with trustworthy mapping
from reference events to audio sample positions. State the remaining accuracy,
phase noise and jitter; software correction cannot remove unobserved jitter or
restore missing samples. Keep this local-reference calibration distinct from
the excluded weak-signal/Doppler tracking. GPS PPS quantization/jitter and the
disciplining loop must not be mistaken for the oscillator's phase stability.

Derive the search from justified oscillator accuracy bounds for the intended
integration duration. Distinguish static accuracy, stability and phase noise;
do not treat a phase-noise or Allan-deviation value as a frequency-error bound.
The present GPSDO residual presets are illustrative effective-link assumptions,
not measured device guarantees. A 3x multiplier does not turn an unjustified
nominal value into a conservative specification. State whether accuracy values
already represent relative TX/RX error; combine per-end bounds conservatively
before applying the margin, without counting an effective-link allowance twice.

For a common reference controlling conversion and sampling, the starting model is
  RF frequency = physical on-air center from the declared conversion chain
  frequency half-width = margin * RF frequency * relative_accuracy_ppm * 1e-6
For an upper-sideband chain, RF frequency is RF LO/shift plus the actual modem
carrier in the real stream. Use the corresponding signed mapping for other
conversion orientations. The actual modem carrier is never replaced by an LF=0
reference-topology indicator in this calculation.
For independent audio and external RF references, use separate bounds, for example
  frequency half-width = margin * 1e-6 *
      (abs(audio carrier) * audio_accuracy_ppm + abs(RF shift) * RF_accuracy_ppm)
  sample-clock half-width in ppm = margin * audio_accuracy_ppm
Do not assume an OCXO/GPSDO controlling the radio also disciplines an independent
sound card. RF translation error contributes an additive frequency offset in Hz;
sample-clock error changes chip/symbol timing. Never infer timing error by
dividing the total RF-derived offset by the audio carrier. Allow coupling only
when the declared reference relationship justifies it.

Native radios, shared clocks and equivalent carrier settings
----------------------------------------------------------
Data Pump accepts and produces ordinary real ADC/DAC sample streams. Do not add
a complex I/Q input/output format or require the user to supply quadrature data.
The delivered receiver modem carrier is not literally at DC. A radio can deliver
a real stream spanning, for example, 0-20 kHz or 0-1 MHz above its RF tuning/LO
frequency in upper-sideband operation. These ranges describe usable passband,
not sample rate; use the actual supplied sample rate and passband limits.

Represent the RF LO/tuning frequency, actual modem carrier and occupied band in
the delivered real stream, real sample rate, and reference topology separately.
Interpret the proposed LF=0 / RF=10 MHz setting as metadata for a radio whose
ADC/DAC and synchronous mixer LOs share the radio reference, with no independent
PC sound-card oscillator. It does not place the modem tone at zero or remove
timing uncertainty. Resolve this shorthand to an explicit shared-reference mode
in configuration and display its meaning clearly. Keep the actual modem carrier
available separately for templates, filtering and physical RF calculations.

For example, if RF=10 MHz identifies the LO, a 1.5 kHz modem carrier in the
delivered upper-sideband stream corresponds to an on-air carrier of 10.0015 MHz.
Label LO frequency versus total on-air carrier so the offset is added exactly
once. Independent sound-card operation retains its separate LF oscillator model.
In shared-reference mode, do not force two independent oscillator models onto
the same clock. Separate reference sharing within each endpoint from any sharing
between TX and RX; two GPSDO radios need not have identical residual phase noise.

Normalize equivalent user descriptions of the same physical waveform, input
interface and reference chain into one configuration before planning the search.
An explicitly declared total RF carrier and an equivalent LO-plus-modem-offset
description must give the same physical carrier and uncertainty. Record sideband
orientation at the real-stream interface. Direct conversion with subsequent
processing and superheterodyne conversion with synchronous LOs require no separate
decoder configuration when they present the same stream and reference behavior.
Model their effective frequency/phase performance, not the internal mixer chain.
Neither LF=0 metadata nor an RF carrier value may overwrite the actual PCM tone
or make the software sample-rate planner scale with RF frequency.

For static fractional reference errors e_TX and e_RX, with each endpoint's RF
synthesis and sampling sharing its own reference, use the normalized model
  r = (1 + e_TX) / (1 + e_RX)
  residual frequency offset = physical_RF_frequency * (r - 1)
  symbol duration in receiver nominal time = nominal_symbol_duration / r
For upper-sideband reception this makes the delivered modem carrier
  received_modem_carrier = nominal_modem_carrier + residual_frequency_offset
Use the corresponding mapping for other conversion orientations. Search paired
frequency/rate hypotheses on this relation, not every independent combination.
Use the actual physical RF frequency for the coupling, never divide by the LF=0
metadata indicator. Combine endpoint bounds or use the effective relative-link
bound, then apply the margin once. Do not count the common reference again as an
independent LF error plus an independent RF error. Add independent dimensions
only for justified residual errors, such as a separately referenced converter.
Shared references can still feed different PLLs with additional phase noise;
retain those residual impairments and correlations without double-counting them.

Fit unknown constant phase using the existing internal phase treatment, preserving
encrypted stream/epoch and symbol-timing searches. A high RF center does not require a new
grid over carrier phase. Assess residual time-varying phase after constant-phase
and fixed-frequency fits over the full symbol. If that coherence is inadequate,
report the limitation or use a justified shorter integration; do not pretend a
wider static-frequency bank restores coherence or add the excluded tracking.

Downconvert, filter and decimate once before the repeated pattern search, using
the radio's hardware facilities where available. At equal waveform bandwidth,
symbol duration and actual uncertainty, equivalent carrier representations must
produce the same hypotheses and comparable dominant decoder cost and memory.
A wider or higher-rate delivered real stream can incur greater front-end cost;
select the modem's occupied band plus justified uncertainty before repeating
expensive correlations. Preserve valid sampling and noise statistics.
A physically higher RF frequency at the same fractional accuracy may legitimately
require more frequency hypotheses. A different way of entering the same RF
frequency must not. Keep encryption work independent of nominal RF cycles.

One search policy throughout the application
-------------------------------------------
Resolve both oscillator models, RF shift and margin into one bounded policy used
by live and offline receivers, receiver resets, simulation, probability/CPU estimates
and planner target fitting. At fixed waveform settings, a better declared
oscillator must reduce unnecessary hypotheses in the real receiver, not merely
move the modeled signal inside an unchanged broad bank or change a graph label.
Keep a finite frequency lattice with suitable integration-dependent spacing;
round coverage outward without retaining an arbitrary broad minimum region that
negates the improvement. Keep explicit residual offset distinct from RF shift.

Represent frequency and sample-rate hypotheses correctly in every backend.
At the inspected source, the FFT receiver validates clock_errors_ppm but its rate
comes from carrier coupling, whereas the compact correlator actually enumerates
the independent rates. Audit and fix this discrepancy before advertising separate
RF/audio clock coverage; merely passing another configuration field is insufficient.
Preserve compatible behavior for low-level callers without the new policy.

Retain finite passband, hypothesis, memory and numeric limits. Report requested
versus covered ranges, including any narrower fallback; never silently clamp and
advertise complete margin coverage. The current GPSDO-XO/TCXO/OCXO presets share
a frequency residual and differ in phase diffusion: equal accuracy assumptions
should produce equal search geometry at otherwise identical settings. Do not
invent wider frequency bounds merely from the oscillator class name.

Long-symbol computational efficiency
-----------------------------------
Make the expensive pattern correlation primarily depend on bandwidth, complete
symbol duration and actual hypothesis count, rather than audio carrier cycles.
Use a suitable common internal representation and reusable pulse/template work
where equivalence can be established. Internal complex arithmetic is an
implementation choice and must not introduce an I/Q stream interface.
Encryption/keystream generation scales with chips, positions and key hypotheses;
RF translation adds no cipher work by
itself. A wider justified uncertainty in Hz can legitimately add hypotheses.

The existing FFT path already mixes and bins the input: at 100 Hz nominal Rate,
aligned half-chip bins can give 100 complex observations/s for both a 50 Hz
carrier with 400 PCM samples/s and a 1.5 kHz carrier with 6,000 PCM samples/s.
The compact shaped correlator still evaluates both bit templates at every raw
sample per hypothesis. Investigate shared pulse/chip calculations and a validated
baseband projection for that long-symbol path. Simply discarding samples or
changing the CPU estimate is not an optimization with equivalent reception.

Any filtering/decimation must retain the occupied signal and the entire intended
offset range, account for image/quadrature and noise covariance, and preserve
correct energy, Gram matrices, trial penalties and confidence thresholds. Preserve
exact symbol/epoch/chip addresses, sample-coordinate rounding, bounded memory,
cancellation, next-poll bit progress and physical absence detection. Do not add
transmitted synchronization markers, change the encrypted waveform or wire format,
or end a stream using EOF, successful decoding or partial-symbol silence.

Validation and delivery for the future implementation
----------------------------------------------------
Test default 0 Hz/3x controls and command round trips, translated HF cases, separate
LF/RF model selections, shared and independent reference assumptions, both signs
of residual error, uncertainty edges, invalid values, numeric limits, and explicit
incomplete-coverage reporting.
Changing the RF model must not silently change an independent audio-clock bound;
GPS computer timestamps alone must not select a disciplined audio-clock model.
Test inactive extra RF error for untranslated audio, active RF and sample-clock
error for radio-reference LF=0, GPS-calibrated versus free-running audio,
constant unknown phase, and residual phase noise in each stage separately.
Verify actual evaluated frequency/rate hypotheses against the planner, including
FFT, compact and fallback paths. Narrower searches must not conceal loss of the
declared coverage or use simulated ground truth unavailable to a real receiver.

Test real-stream radio cases at 10 MHz and up to 30 MHz, the LF=0 shared-reference
indicator with a nonzero actual modem carrier, LO versus total RF carrier entry,
upper-sideband offset mapping, and equivalent direct-conversion/superheterodyne
interfaces. Include real 0-20 kHz and 0-1 MHz input passbands with appropriate
sample rates; no complex I/Q interface is in scope.
Hold the physical waveform, delivered interface, clocks,
uncertainty and symbol settings fixed; require identical canonical search bounds,
paired hypotheses, phase assumptions and comparable core search cost. Cover
shared-clock timing ratios on both sides of unity and genuine independent-clock
cases. Separately measure wide-stream front-end work and narrowband search work.

Benchmark encrypted 100 Hz Rate reception at 50 Hz and 1.5 kHz audio carriers with
identical symbol duration, keys/epochs, timing/frequency coverage and acceptance
rules. Include very long symbols, matched signal and noise-only cases, memory
limits, cancellation and incremental progress. Report front-end conversion/mixing
separately from correlation, actual CPU time, wall time and memory. Compare against
the current batched implementation; do not claim speedup from fewer required
observations, weakened detection gates or omitted hypotheses. Distinguish savings
from a legitimately narrower oscillator search from DSP savings at equal coverage.
Target comparable dominant search cost, not an unsupported promise of identical
whole-application CPU use. Qualify detection/false-accept behavior and normal
regressions before claiming measured efficiency or improved usable sensitivity.

2026-10-07 / Codex: superseding Carrier/Shift oscillator control contract

The implemented controls are Baseband Osc, Shift Osc and Margin, beside Carrier
and Shift on the main Robust screen. No separate Clock dropdown is needed.
Carrier and Shift accept case-insensitive Hz, kHz, MHz, GHz and THz suffixes,
optional spaces and scientific notation. Their positive difference is the real
USB stream tone; USB remains fixed.

At Shift 0 Hz, Shift Osc is disabled as N/A. Exclude every Shift-model frequency,
phase and linked-drift contribution, including legacy shared-reference settings;
only the Baseband model is active. Keep the chosen Shift option for reactivation.
At a positive Shift, an independent Shift profile adds conversion uncertainty,
or Baseband clock uses the selected Baseband profile once for shared ADC/DAC and
synchronous mixers, with linked frequency/rate search and no duplicate drift.
The old LF=0 setting was shared-reference metadata, not Shift=0. Preserve legacy
CLI imports by mapping an active shared model to visible Baseband Osc and
Shift Osc=Baseband clock; an inactive Shift must never replace Baseband's model.

Put numerical oscillator assumptions and requested/covered search information
under the Link planner's hideable Model limits and references. Preserve tiny
nonzero search bounds in formatting. These control refinements supersede the
LF/RF/Clock terminology above; remaining computational-efficiency and validation
requirements still apply. See docs/oscillator-models.md for the current contract.
