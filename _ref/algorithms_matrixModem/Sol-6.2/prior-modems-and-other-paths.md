# Prior modems and the distinct propagation paths

Research note for Matrix Modem, prepared 24 November 2026. This report reconciles the user's design history with repository records and the maintained HF, VLF, groundwave, and Earth–Moon–Earth research. It does not qualify a Matrix implementation or establish a field success probability. The prior findings are useful constraints and counterexamples, not proof that one family is best.

## Evidence map and interpretation

The user’s account is the starting design history. It contains informed observations from development, but several numerical or causal parts are not independently established by that account alone. The repository offers stronger, bounded evidence for the acoustic Fast path and recent three-carrier Robust studies. Single-carrier Robust is principally a historical implementation record. In each case, distinguish the observation from an inferred general property.

| Modem or evidence | Supported observation | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Fast acoustic OFDM | Several exact speaker/microphone transfers and held-out channel measurements are documented, including a 5 MB transfer. The study explicitly computes its own conditional Gaussian-equivalent capacity and says Shannon capacity was not reached. | A weak-signal HF sensitivity limit, performance below a calibrated noise spectral density, arbitrary acoustic paths, and the claim “near Shannon” as a general property. |
| Historical single-carrier Robust | Git history retains the earlier modem, documentation, and validation artifacts. The family's core concept used a keyed long tone/pattern and searched its cumulative matched evidence. | A current independently reproducible single-carrier sensitivity curve, a broad quantitative compute ceiling, or natural ionospheric success probability. Much of the old experimental stack was removed. |
| Three-carrier Robust | Current native code and extensive contained appraisal receipts demonstrate reception for selected constructed and recorded conditions, alongside numerous adverse failures, material duration costs, and a slower matched CUDA path on short workloads. | General HF reliability, complete practical real-time capacity, large-message probability of success, a broad measured GPU ceiling, or that three carriers are the best way to share a hypothesis across frequency. |
| Literature and standards | They support materially different path classes, broad HF variability, finite link budgets, and specific propagation prediction methods. | Interchangeability of HF, MF/LF groundwave, sub-9 kHz waveguide/near-field and lunar radar channels. |

The evidence chain for Matrix should retain provenance: a simulation is evidence about its specified law, not a natural-path frequency; a complete receiver trial is evidence for its realization and configuration, not a probability estimate absent a predeclared sampling design; an executed CUDA path is not a speedup; receiver computation is not a propagation limit.

## Fast Modem: an acoustic strength and a boundary

The strongest concrete evidence located for Fast is the physical acoustic study. A 5,000,000-byte file transferred exactly over the default internal speaker/microphone path in 746.471 seconds including startup and silence. All 840 LDPC frames converged, there were no FIFO overflows or audio errors, and observed physical completion followed the emitted stream. A separate normal CLI trial transferred 100,000 bytes exactly in 47.976 seconds. A 500,000-byte 1024-QAM / LDPC 1/2 configuration also transferred exactly, but its much shorter trial did not qualify long-file reliability. These are completed transfers on one room/device/mixer setup, not channel-independent success rates.

The same study's 130.86 kbit/s estimate is a frequency-integrated, conditional Gaussian-equivalent capacity from a held-out residual and quiet-noise model. The selected earlier Fast configuration achieved 56.04 kbit/s source rate, around 43% of that sounder model. The study explicitly states Shannon capacity had not been reached and lists cyclic prefix, pilots, refresh, coding and mapper-fill losses. “Near the Shannon limit” therefore overstates this repository evidence. Fast is impressive because it converts a challenging, echo-rich but strong/structured acoustic channel into reliable, high-rate delivery on tested hardware. No source located here demonstrates Fast at the intended weak HF regime where received carrier power sits below the noise power in a wide receiver bandwidth.

This distinction does not imply that Fast's constituent tools are intrinsically unsuitable for weak signals. Coding, pilot-assisted equalization, OFDM, and frequency-selective bit loading can all be used at low SNR. The system's tested modulation, acquisition, power, background, observation time and receiver workload determine the result. If Matrix reuses any Fast encoder or DSP primitives, it needs an identical-input weak-signal reference, and its own complete observation/false-alarm contract. The prior acoustic result supplies no automatic sensitivity transfer.

Repository evidence: [Fast acoustic study](../../../docs/fast-acoustic-live-study.md), [physical low-SNR reception correction](../../../docs/fast-acoustic-low-snr-recovery.md), [Fast mode behavior](../../../docs/fast-mode.md). The study itself links the archived 2026-09-20 evidence and explicitly labels long-file forecasts as projections.

## Historical single-carrier Robust: useful mechanism, weak continuity

The user's description is consistent with the older repository history: one carrier communicated through key-dependent phase/amplitude patterning, and the receiver searched accumulated tone evidence under noise. The modem and heavy tests were removed in commit [`419e6bae`](https://github.com/mirage335-colossus/pumpModem/commit/419e6bae) (“Remove obsolete Robust modem code and heavy test infrastructure”). History proves that the implementation existed and was removed; it does not independently validate the user's assessment that its usable SNR was impressive or compute ceiling uncomfortable. The historical branch's detailed reports are sparse, and retaining every removed campaign would conflict with the repository's material lifecycle contract.

The family still suggests useful Matrix questions. Long matched integrations can detect a known pattern below the instantaneous noise level. They pay for observation time, nuisance search and false-alarm calibration. A single carrier also samples one narrow portion of a selective fading channel. Several frequencies can provide diversity, but only if their fades are sufficiently decorrelated and the receiver has a principled joint statistic. A fixed carrier count is not a guarantee of independent diversity. Adjacent HF carriers separated by a few hundred hertz may be highly correlated in a narrow-delay channel; some longer-delay or differently polarized modes can decorrelate over smaller separations. Conversely, frequency movement can change the relative channel phase and make a nominally coherent sum worse.

Thus neither the single-tone history nor the three-carrier successor fundamentally rules out a simpler one-carrier or small-bank design. A useful comparison would match transmitted energy, information, path/noise realization, false-alarm budget, complete search and acquisition assumptions. It should include a flat-channel control, frequency-selective slow fading, and at least one evolving multipath case, with diversity gain measured against the same transmitted energy.

Historical repository references include [`docs/robust-alternate-receiver.md` at the last pre-removal tree](https://github.com/mirage335-colossus/pumpModem/blob/419e6bae%5E/docs/robust-alternate-receiver.md), [`docs/robust-cpu-costs.md` at that tree](https://github.com/mirage335-colossus/pumpModem/blob/419e6bae%5E/docs/robust-cpu-costs.md), and the pinned acquisition and spectrum reports under `docs/validation-data/robust/` at the same parent revision. These are source-bound historical records, not current appraisal contracts. In particular, a measured implementation cost from that single-tone stack should not calibrate Matrix's native batched algorithm.

## Three-carrier Robust: proof of possibility and evidence of unfinished work

The three-carrier design addressed two real pressures. Joint evidence across separated carriers may survive a fade affecting one carrier, and the public search work has more independent candidate calculations that are potentially batchable. The current channel literature finds that a three-carrier decision cannot be modeled as “three independent votes” by default. Correlation bandwidth varies with path class, range, frequency, polarization, antenna pattern and the exact observable. HF paths can contain dominant modes, correlated changes and extended weak intervals. A third carrier also consumes transmitted energy, spectral span, transmitter linearity and search/storage work.

The repository's recent validation is much richer than the original proof-of-concept simulations, and it has a mixed outcome. The native appraisal has completed bounded integrations and selected native receives, and has reproduced real algorithmic improvements on specific frozen inputs. At the same time, the latest HF continuation preserves errors in timing acquisition, wrong-bit decisions, false DATA, physical completion, fading, clock/channel mismatch and long weak observations. Its own summary lists completed desired-message failures and a remaining highest-priority adverse case. Some repairs recover selected prior failures but fail fresh or more general integrity checks. These are actual contained-receiver limitations, not proof that three carriers cannot work.

Several adjacent results are especially relevant to a new design:

* Stable two-ray offset, random Doppler width, stochastic per-ray gain, phase wandering and a time-varying ionosphere are different channel laws. Simulations matched to a receiver's exact assumed two-ray parameters can be much easier than the natural problem. The detailed reconciliation records examples in which a flexible dynamic likelihood helps under random fading but loses on stable channels, and can admit more wrong keys if gain freedom absorbs the payload pattern.
* A propagation model may be numerically credible yet still fail to yield decoded content through the complete public receiver. Conversely, receiver misses may arise from a coarse proposal omitting a supported fine-search hypothesis, not from insufficient received energy. Private oracles and known-channel projections diagnose information or implementation loss; they are not public receiver outcomes.
* Adding observation length or coding can recover isolated erasures, but cannot correct a physically premature completion or persistent wrong bits without a trustworthy stream boundary and integrity mechanism. The complete-message evidence includes cases where one policy recovers selected payloads at material increases in transmit energy and receiver service, and cases where it accepts truncation or still fails a fresh control.
* The user's concern that the development process and computation were difficult is supported qualitatively by the breadth of research and qualification now maintained. It is not a quantitative comparison against a settled alternative Matrix implementation.

### GPU evidence is narrower than the performance concern

There is one relevant direct comparison in current validation. A bounded six-episode paired CPU/CUDA campaign ran the same frozen inputs, with actual CUDA execution on a full RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Outcomes matched. Aggregate episode wall times were faster on CPU in all three paired classes: 0.957 vs 2.472 seconds for AWGN, 5.209 vs 5.966 seconds for disturbed HF, and 8.513 vs 11.820 seconds for noise-only Helper acquisition. In the disturbed-HF cell an FP16 FFT ran, while INT8 and FP16 matrix-contraction call counters were zero. A separate corrected-horizon CUDA receive was a functional check, not a throughput comparison. A utilization monitor sampled too sparsely to establish Tensor Core use or duty cycle.

These numbers show that this older workload, its launch/preparation/transfer costs and tested CUDA route did not improve complete-process wall time on these short episodes. They do not show that the RTX PRO 6000 was executing “a few TFLOPS of matrix arithmetic,” that the device's theoretical peak is below the 32-core CPU, or that an INT4-friendly dense matrix formulation cannot be faster. The user-reported dozen-TFLOPS CPU versus few-TFLOPS GPU figures lack an accessible workload definition and instrumentation here, so they remain a development observation to remeasure.

The relevant current records are [complete CUDA comparison](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/validation.md#matched-cpucuda-runpod-episodes), [current appraisal findings](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/hf-validation.md), [coverage and limits](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/coverage.md), [GPU optimization protocol](../../../docs/triad-gpu-optimization.md) in the historical tree, and the current [GPU assisted development contract](../../../docs/triad-performance.md#gpu-selection-for-accelerated-development). Full source, hardware and allocation information must accompany any later result. The existing workload calls do not qualify Matrix throughput.

The denominator for Matrix GPU results should be useful fully evaluated hypotheses and complete-receiver progress per elapsed second, reported alongside GPU busy time, Tensor Core active time, achieved arithmetic, memory traffic, launched kernel count, transfer/preparation time, workspace, CPU fallback and exact operand/accumulator arithmetic. “Near 100% busy” by itself can describe useless kernels or synthetic padding. High utilization matters because it may raise completed search per second; it is not itself an acceptance outcome.

## Propagation families are separate operating problems

The current appraisal covers five target families. They make different physical assumptions and cannot share one undifferentiated “worst channel” setting.

| Path family | Main propagation and receiver consequences | What HF evidence transfers | Research anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sub-9 kHz / 200 ft-class antenna | A 61 m radiator at 8.27 kHz is only λ/594. It is not a conventional efficient half-wave radiator. Coupling and radiation depend strongly on antenna circuit, grounding, electric/magnetic receiver geometry, near-field terms at short range, and Earth–ionosphere waveguide modes at long range. There are mode cutoffs, absorption bands, day/night variation and geomagnetic direction effects. | Detection/coding mathematics and stream integrity can transfer. HF ionospheric delay/Doppler presets, path loss assumptions and antenna noise do not. | Existing [sub-9 kHz propagation review](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/10-sub9khz-propagation.md), [antenna/link review](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/11-sub9khz-antennas-links-and-practice.md), and ITU-R [P.684](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.684/en) distinguish near-field, wave-hop and waveguide regimes. |
| 1 MHz groundwave | A surface wave over real ground or sea. Conductivity, permittivity, polarization, antenna efficiency and distance dominate; groundwave may coexist with nighttime MF skywave. Atmospheric and man-made noise can be very high. | Narrowband detection and measured noise handling transfer. HF F-layer multi-hop/ray spread is not the channel. | ITU-R [P.368 ground-wave method](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.368/en) covers 10 kHz–30 MHz; ITU-R [P.1147](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.1147/en) is for skywave field strength around 150–1700 kHz. |
| 30 MHz groundwave | At this upper edge of HF, groundwave over land can be short-range and strongly ground-dependent. “30 MHz” alone does not define groundwave range; antenna height, terrain, polarization, ground parameters, and any direct/tropospheric component matter. | General detector/coding and receiver implementation transfer. A skywave HF distribution cannot be assigned to a groundwave test. | ITU-R [P.368](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.368/en) includes 30 MHz at its upper boundary. Model land/sea and finite geometry explicitly. |
| 1–30 MHz skywave | Refraction/scattering in a changing ionosphere; multiple modes, Doppler/frequency spread, multipath delay, polarization rotation, fading, noise and availability vary with geography, time, season and solar/geomagnetic state. | This is the priority challenge for Matrix. Standards and measurements can bound representative test strata, but not guarantee all links or outage duration. | ITU-R [P.533](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.533/en) predicts HF circuit performance including time/frequency spread and reliability from 2–30 MHz; [P.372](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.372/en) supplies radio-noise methods and reference data. |
| Earth–Moon–Earth | A roughly 384,000 km two-way radar path with inverse fourth-power distance in the monostatic radar equation, lunar scattering, a delay distribution to about 11.6 ms and geometry-driven Doppler across the lunar disc. It is far more than free-space fading on a point-to-point terrestrial path. | Generic hypothesis testing, coherent/noncoherent detection, coding and resource accounting transfer. Ionospheric HF spread numbers and ordinary terrestrial Doppler presets do not. | Existing [lunar scattering/geometry analysis](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/40-eme-lunar-scattering-and-geometry.md) and [EME station/environment study](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/41-eme-systems-practice-and-environment.md) derive the geometry and map the evidence limits. |

For HF specifically, the [ITU-R P.533 method](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) treats time and frequency spreading as part of predicted digital circuit performance, while its scope is 2–30 MHz. Its monthly prediction method is not a substitute for a physical-channel record of the actual message duration. The Fable reviews show why one chosen impairment preset cannot stand in for broad conditions: measured spread definitions differ, sparse campaign percentiles are path- and era-specific, auroral and equatorial tails can exceed ordinary midlatitude cases, and natural outages are temporally correlated. Existing standards provide representative modeled cases rather than universal worst-case bounds.

### Bandwidth and “6.25 Hz” are not a single tolerance knob

For context, the user suggests a default near 6.25 Hz bandwidth and symbol rate. That pair does not establish a 6.25 Hz Doppler spread, channel coherence time, delay-spread tolerance, required tone spacing or occupied RF bandwidth. At 6.25 baud a symbol is 160 ms. This duration is much longer than many HF differential path delays (often sub-millisecond to several milliseconds, with adverse tails larger) and may avoid symbol-to-symbol disruption from short delay spread if the receiver is noncoherent or handles the delay tails. It does not make the channel static for 160 ms when the instantaneous Doppler/fading law varies; nor does it average away a fade whose coherence time is seconds or minutes. Tone spacing and observation length should be chosen from separate measured distributions and a clear likelihood model.

The maintained HF research reports path-specific distinctions, not one universal numerical “tolerance.” For example, quiet midlatitude widths can be well below 1 Hz, disturbed auroral/equatorial cases can reach several to tens of hertz, and delay spreads measured on ordinary paths often fall below a few milliseconds with adverse tails extending beyond. Measurement conventions (Gaussian 2σ, 80%-power width, half width) vary, so raw values cannot be combined without conversion and caveats. The detailed source table and literature limitations live in the linked [midlatitude/NVIS](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/31-skywave-dispersion-midlatitude-nvis-longhaul.md) and [high-latitude/equatorial](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/32-skywave-dispersion-highlatitude-equatorial.md) reports.

## Design consequences and discriminating evidence

The prior record supports a research direction, not a proven implementation. A Matrix candidate should be expressed as a likelihood/statistic whose expensive core is an exact batched matrix operation over public hypotheses and a corresponding null/noise model. Then measure whether increased matrix utilization advances complete correct decisions under the same false-admission rule and the entire same-stream hypothesis set.

The simplest informative comparison is a single narrowband tone bank versus two or three frequency-separated tones, all using the same code and identical information/total transmitted energy. Use at least three frozen channel strata: (1) stationary AWGN/flat fading as a numerical control, (2) measured or source-reproduced moderate HF multipath with explicit delay/Doppler law, and (3) a changing/adverse path with burst fades or correlated outages. Pair each with noise-only and wrong-key controls. Keep timing/CFO/clock acquisition, complete physical quiet completion and coding in the measured path. Report bit, complete-message and false-DATA outcomes separately. Run a smaller CPU reference first to make high-confidence reduced experiments affordable, then compare actual GPU work and complete simulation speed on the same native renderer and receiver.

For simulation speed, batch independent channel realizations only after preserving each realization's continuous path, oscillator, filter, hop and noise state across the complete stream. Repeated reset short blocks can grossly overstate the number of independent fading conditions or suppress long fades. Reuse computed deterministic waveforms/templates across SNR sweeps when mathematically valid, and separate source generation, propagation, receive filtering, hypothesis products and decision work in profiles. A simulation that speeds only noise replicas while repeatedly restarting channel state is not a faster test of long-message availability.

Do not infer that better alternatives have been ruled out. Credible families remaining for direct comparison include noncoherent orthogonal/near-orthogonal FSK with coded symbol decisions, short coherent tone blocks with noncoherent accumulation, keyed hop schedules with noncoherent evidence, single-tone matched histories, and dense template projections whose columns represent nuisance hypotheses. They have different energy, bandwidth, acquisition, fading, false-alarm, accumulator and GPU-tiling costs. The external [waveform options research](waveform-options.md) and [information limits](information-limits.md) should decide which survive quantitative comparison.
