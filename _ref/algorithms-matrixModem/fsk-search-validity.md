# Coherent FSK search on a finite observation

The sub-9 kHz transform-across-tones design shares substantial work across candidate frequencies. Three quantities determine whether its result is meaningful for a given oscillator range: the bandwidth admitted by each tone sum, the physical coordinates of the frequency/drift templates, and the samples retained at tone boundaries. The historical kernel experiment measured useful throughput on preformed tone values. A matched error in its generator and templates reduced the physical drift range by half. Historical tables and their original programs at revision `396b47e1` remain evidence for that earlier geometry. The corrected code, independent calculation, and device experiment are linked below. ## Define the reference at the middle of the observation

Let `T=5242.88 s`, `x=(t−T/2)/T`, `q=f_mid T` and `g=fdot T²`. A linear physical frequency gives phase `2πqx+πgx²` up to an irrelevant constant. Its instantaneous frequency changes by `g/T` hertz over the block. The corrected kernels use tone midpoints, symmetric half-bin frequency centres and physical drift centres −5.82, −1.94, 1.94, 5.82. Previously the quadratic term was `πgx²/2` in both generation and removal, with frequency referenced to the start of the block. This gave physical centres −2.91, −0.97, 0.97, 2.91. A correct planted winner in that experiment therefore tested an internally consistent, narrower bank. Its historical 10-bit error rates and search penalty attach to that bank. The independent control integrates a chirp specified in hertz per second and recovers unit coherent power at both new outer centres. At a true drift of 5.82, fitting the historical outer drift 2.91 while aligning the mean frequency loses 2.083 dB. The new 174-bin frequency grid covers a symmetric span by offsetting the even-sized zero-padded FFT grid by a quarter of an ordinary Fourier bin. For a rectangular whole-block aperture, the separate mid-cell losses for 0.25 cycles of frequency residual and 1.94 bins of end-to-end drift are 0.912 and 0.909 dB. The joint corner gives 1.752 dB even with exact timing and uncompressed samples. These are deterministic changes in coherent signal power. They do not set a block-error threshold. Frequency/drift mismatch can also be integrated directly for a different grid, window or weighted occupancy. Ransom, Eikenberry and Middleditch distinguish finite data integration from frequency interpolation and acceleration matching in their original Fourier analysis. [Primary paper, 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)

The oscillator table uses the *relative* bound between emitted and sampled waveforms. For 8,270 Hz, a relative ±1 ppm and ±0.1 ppm/hour gives `|f_mid|T=43.359` and `|g|=6.315`. At half-bin frequency spacing and 3.88-bin drift spacing this needs 174 × 4 cells. If each independent endpoint is allowed that full error, opposing signs give 347 × 7 cells before adding path shifts, clock-rate uncertainty or propagation delay. Common references or measured correlations can reduce this envelope when evidence supports them. These are configured supports. An estimated standard deviation does not establish a deterministic limit.

## What one complex value per tone retains

For a rectangular tone sum of duration `U`, a frequency residual `δf` relative to the centre of the **first** projection leaves the power fraction `sinc²(δf U)`, with `sinc(v)=sin(πv)/(πv)`. At constant offset the sequence of tone sums is sampled every `U` seconds. The *outer* FFT response repeats after `1/U` hertz. Frequencies separated by this amount give the same phase progression, up to a constant. The discarded samples supplied the amplitude envelope and any information needed to break that ambiguity for an unknown received gain. Post-rotation cannot undo an aperture null.

For 512 × 10.24 s tones, the following are losses at the edges of a centred **span of half-bin cells**, before error in choosing the final grid point. Recurring calibration must charge every separately mixed first-stage band. Sub-block summation changes `U` to the duration actually summed before the frequency-dependent rotation. For drift, budget for the instantaneous frequency throughout each sub-block and any within-sub-block curvature.

Within a tone, holding instantaneous frequency at its midpoint leaves a quadratic phase error of at most `π|g|/(4×512²)` radians, below 0.000018 for the simulated rows. The reference comparison with sampled chirps includes both immediate neighbours. This calculation assumes a phase-matched rectangular aperture, unit path gain and white complex Gaussian noise. It does not synthesize antenna filtering, sample-level conditioning or quantization before the projection. The simulation quantizes complex bin values. ## What a phase slope and a tilted bin path can correct

The [wide-band timing study](fsk-timing-coverage.md) applies these limits to the
native ten-bit sample receiver. Its earlier successful arrivals matched the
coherent search grid; fractional arrivals additionally require signed physical
bin frequencies and coverage between the old timing candidates.

A delay contributes a known phase slope across stationary frequencies for a fixed aperture. Translating a *finite* integration window additionally exchanges samples at its two ends. On the default rectangular waveform consider two adjacent tones at the same position. A window that is late by `U/16` retains unit power if their phases agree. If the second is reversed, the amplitude is `1−2/16`, and power falls by 1.160 dB after perfect slope correction. At `U/8` the loss is 2.499 dB. A random/zero-mean neighbouring contribution can justify a mean coherent-amplitude model, but `(1−τ/U)²` is not a worst-case power guarantee. Transition ramps and multipath require their own calculations. Reuse aligned bin values with a documented approximation or account for boundary samples explicitly, including their work and memory. Majoral, Arribas and Fernández-Prades measured a Galileo receiver using longer coherent integration and discuss the separate transition, drift and code-alignment constraints. Their signal-specific results supply no threshold for this waveform. [Primary receiver experiment, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24051416)

For a rung of `W` equal windows, the frequency moves by `|g|/W²` ordinary Fourier bins *within each window*. The first-to-last window-centre shift is `2|g|(W−1)/W²` half bins of that rung. A tilted path through window spectra corrects the latter between-window motion. The former still attenuates each local projection, even on the correct tilt. At `W=2` the relative TCXO, crystal and sound-card model extremes produce respectively 1.58, 15.79 and 78.93 ordinary bins of intra-window drift. At `W=8` the sound-card case still has 4.93 bins per window. The two-rung correction described for a small drift therefore does not generalize to the wider classes. Hold the data long enough to correct the required drift before projection, use suitably shorter chunks and combine them, or quantify the sensitivity envelope of an approximation. A screening stage that can discard a weak coherent signal requires its own miss probability at the target workload. A 2025 GPU pulsar study illustrates this distinction by reporting unmatched-filter sensitivity on a simulated population alongside execution times. Its work and signal distribution differ from a coded FSK receiver. [White et al., Pulscan](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/adc89e)

## Which times the old experiment actually measured

The historical 1,643-s laptop figure expands 393,216 sampled candidate evaluations with 20 synthetic phase rotations to 16,777,216 candidates. The 11.38-s GPU figure multiplies per-stage timings from a 131,072-message batch and one timing offset by 128 batches and 20 offsets. The TFLOP/s figure describes the matrix product inside that batch. Neither figure times a full receiver block. Both support comparing the fitted transforms with the earlier repeated-decode implementation at that kernel shape. The source deliberately omits physical window formation, presence decisions and drift-correct shorter rungs. GPU figures for other clock classes or message sizes add scaling assumptions. Full-receiver backlog, memory and correct ordered completion need measurements of all admitted work at the configured uncertainties, including unsuccessful search and quiet slots. Retain the synthetic kernel timings as benchmark data and use the corrected geometry in new experiments.
