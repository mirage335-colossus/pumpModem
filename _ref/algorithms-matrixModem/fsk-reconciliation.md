# Matrix FSK receiver design review

The original brief asks for a one-way modem that can keep up when reception is difficult. Keep the one-tone lattice as an experimental reference. A fast transform, a high score at the true frequency, and successful decoding on a Gaussian channel answer different questions. For a configuration to be usable, the receiver must also score absent blocks, search its full public uncertainty, and finish processing before input accumulates. The following results change how to select and test the design. ## Receiver evidence

At 18 dB of block energy over noise density, the six-hypothesis, 10-bit receiver admitted the correct word on 125/160 one-path blocks and 12/160 two-path blocks. The second fixture splits the same nominal power between a direct ray and an echo delayed 2.5 ms and shifted by 12.5 Hz. It retained the same samples across timing and spectral hypotheses. On a fresh paired holdout, adding two-bin apertures raised the count from 15 to 40 of 160 on that echo. The expanded search lost ten single-path admissions, and the counts were 20 and 19 on a 9.375 Hz echo. Both thresholds were trained for a nominal 1% false-presence budget. Five of 400 independent noise-only blocks crossed the original threshold. These are decisions on a specified configured calendar with public test schedules and nominal transmitted energy, including both frontend and decoder losses. [Test protocol and counts](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/block-reception/README.md)

A second comparison gave a more transferable gain on these fixtures. The receiver tried a six-rung coherent sum over all 1,024 candidate words and reserved half the nominal 1% false-admission budget for its per-tone fallback. At 18 dB, correct two-ray admissions rose from 15/120 with the per-tone baseline to 115/120 with the joint decision. At 15 dB they reached 39/120 on the two-ray path. On a path that reset phase every tone, allocating the smaller per-tone budget reduced correct admissions at 18 dB from 105/120 to 91/120. The joint rule had 3/400 false admissions on its noise holdout. This supports preserving the fallback, calibrating the two branches together, and choosing their budget against the weakest required path. [Coherent and per-tone comparison](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/block-reception/README.md#coherent-branch-and-protected-per-tone-fallback)

The same receiver closed a message when two transmitted blocks were completely extinguished. Their samples had the same law as two quiet blocks. One interior extinguished block followed by a good block remained an erasure. A stronger code can help with partial evidence. A different receiver cannot determine the transmitter's state from genuinely indistinguishable inputs. This reproduces the important distinction from the older Robust work with Matrix samples and the Matrix decision. [Historical identical-input control](../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/hf-fragment-integrity.md#exact-native-input-ambiguity-under-an-outage)

The attended target has a direct sizing and receiver comparison. At 400 nuisance hypotheses per word, ten bits at 10⁻⁴ cost 5.9% more modeled weak-tone airtime than one bit at 10⁻⁶, or 27.5% more than one bit at 10⁻⁴. Both calculations hold the true-template miss probability at 10⁻³. In the native one-cell experiment, the thresholds trained for 10⁻⁴ admitted one of 30,000 fresh quiet blocks. The exact one-sided 95% upper bound of 1.58 × 10⁻⁴ missed the design target. At fixed airtime, relaxing further to 1% recovered 43 additional correct words in 120 slow-fading trials at 18 dB. In 120 fast-fading trials at the same energy it gained two correct words and admitted two wrong ones. The trials counted receiver decisions before operator interpretation. [Modeled airtime](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/fsk-lattice/results/admission_tradeoff.md) · [Measured outcomes and reproduction](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/block-reception/README.md#one-false-admission-per-ten-thousand-quiet-blocks)

The corrected sub-9 kHz search also ran on a full RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition. All four tested routes recovered the planted parameters within the tested candidate batch. At 13 dB block energy the 10-bit word error counts were 91/8,192 at the known oscillator hypothesis and 1,257/8,192 searching 696 possibilities. At 15 dB the counts were 0 and 49. The trials used one timing offset, signal present and on-grid offsets. They establish a payload-selection cost of searching this grid. They do not calibrate a quiet-block threshold or quote service for a full 24-bit stream. [Actual device and bounded run](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/search-validity/device-validation.md)

## Operating assumptions

The bandwidth of the tone-position grid, the main lobe of one rectangular tone, the occupied bandwidth of the radiated waveform and the allowable bandwidth under a particular rule are separate quantities. Where the US definition in §2.202(a) applies, occupied bandwidth integrates power and leaves 0.5% in each tail. A pointwise 26 dB spectral mask needs its own reference power and measurement conditions. For specified RTTY/data operation, §97.307(f)(3) gives the 2.8 kHz limit with exceptions. [47 CFR §2.202](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-2.202) · [47 CFR §97.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.307)

A published all-zero schedule lets others run the same receiver. US amateur operation has separate rules for one-way traffic, obscuring meaning, station identification and allowable emission types on each band. A private encrypted payload intended to obscure meaning cannot be assumed usable for ordinary amateur traffic. The 8.27 kHz example is outside the US amateur allocations. The absence of a listed limit below 9 kHz in the general Part 15 field-strength table provides no determination for a particular site or transmitter. Its modeled 10 kW is antenna-system input. The cited radiated power of 5.5 mW to about 0.3 W depends on antenna geometry and voltage. ## What reducing the work can buy

The earlier 0.4 s calculation has a specific premise. In weak independent energy observations, the per-tone mean excess is proportional to received power and the noise standard deviation of a sum grows as the square root of the count. Keeping a fraction `f` of equal-quality tones therefore costs approximately `−5 log10(f)` dB of received power at fixed duration. In a coherent matched filter the output SNR is proportional to retained signal energy. Equal-quality samples then cost `−10 log10(f)` dB, even with the correct timing, channel and frequency. [Original geometry and results](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/fsk-lattice/results/lattice_receiver_section8.md) · [reproduce the bounds](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/calc/retained_work.py)

| Retained tones | Weak independent energy | Coherent reception | Speedup if half the serial cost scales with tone count |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 63% | 1.00 dB | 2.01 dB | 1.23× |
| 50% | 1.51 dB | 3.01 dB | 1.33× |
| 40% | 1.99 dB | 3.98 dB | 1.43× |

For serial cost with tone-dependent share `s`, the ideal speedup from that reduction is `1 / (1−s+s f)`. A parallel pipeline needs a measured change in its limiting stage, including preparation and memory traffic. The default per-tone decoder can still require 32 table lookups per message per hypothesis after thinning. A fixed thinning mask also has no worst-case sensitivity guarantee. A burst channel can deliver all the usable tones on the discarded part. The included 32-tone constructed example gives zero retained signal energy after keeping half the tones. A randomized mask independently chosen from the channel law can support a probabilistic claim with a stated outage risk. Qualification needs the joint mask, schedule, channel and decision rule. [Original fixed-work argument](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~18742/papers/Amdahl1967.pdf) · [earlier scheduling measurements](Fable-5.2/computation-and-simulation-speed.md)

Reusing work can itself reduce the maximum size of a search if it requires retaining an oversized table. For a 24-bit block with 32 symbols, storing a byte for every symbol of every candidate takes 512 MiB before indexing positions, timing hypotheses or windows. The affine symbol map permits split tables indexed by the upper and lower 12 bits of a message. Two byte tables per symbol then require 256 KiB and one XOR per lookup, as in the measured candidate kernels. With 420,000 tones, a single tone-by-position array of packed complex four-bit values over 352 positions takes 147,840,000 bytes per alignment. Admit bounded tiles and compare recomputation with retaining each array over its actual lifetime. Both routes must evaluate the same candidates. [Existing split-table benchmark](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/sub9-fsk-rate/decode_gpu.cu)

Operand precision and accumulation range need separate declarations. With `N=32L` tones, additive evidence in 0…15 gives a per-symbol sum as large as `15L`. At the draft's approximately 420,000-tone scale this is 196,875, requiring 18 unsigned bits. After exact quarter-turn rotations of bin components in −7…7, the largest whole-block squared magnitude is `98N²`, requiring 44 unsigned bits for 420,000 tones. Fractional phase rotations and other scales need their own bound. A kernel timed with 16-bit tables or an FP16 output on short blocks does not establish an exact accumulator or even sufficient range on long blocks. Implementations can partition, widen or explicitly rescale, with score ordering and threshold calibration checked on the resulting representation. [Integer calculation](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/calc/retained_work.py) · [existing precision review](Opus-5.5/07-matrix-hardware-and-precision.md)

## Choosing tone length and power constraints

The current duration calculation uses the attended target on a block the transmitter can
actually schedule. In the HF per-tone approximation at 400 nuisance hypotheses per word,
reserving half of 10⁻⁴ for the per-tone family gives a deflection requirement of 88.75.
The isolated historical 10⁻⁶ calculation gives 99.98. This is an 11.2% difference before
rounding. At +10 dB-Hz the 4 s hop still takes at least 128 s, even where the older
one-cell table quoted 13–14 s. At −10 dB-Hz and 0.1 Hz full Doppler width, the same hop
takes 58,428 s with one-cell tones and 3,712 s with held tones in this model. The 32-hop
floor, actual active samples and exactly 32L transmitted tones enter both comparisons.
These are estimated transmit periods for threshold crossing. An isolated word requires
another two scored quiet periods for physical closure. [Current specification](../../_appraisal/matrixModem-FSK.md#10-sizing-and-expected-duration) · [Repetition, periods and per-tone metric](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/fsk-lattice/results/attended_hf_durations.md)

On the sixteen-position, 10.24 s tone profile below 9 kHz, ideal whole-block coherence at
−37 dB-Hz gives 3.17 days for transmission. Combining for at most 128 tones at a time
gives 29.0 days on the same transmitted format. Finite coherence therefore matters even
when the search finds the correct offset. Each partial final window includes only the
energy actually sent and contributes its own noise to the test. The theoretical model uses
the central and noncentral chi-square laws of normalized squared magnitudes in independent
complex Gaussian noise. Gil, Segura and Temme treat inversion of the corresponding
noncentral tails. The empirical admission experiment has estimated noise, quantization
and competing word decisions and needs separate calibration. [Window and hop calculations](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/fsk-lattice/results/attended_coherent_duration.md) · [Gil, Segura and Temme](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0302) · [Receiver calibration](fsk-presence-calibration.md)

A fixed-duration comparison used the same 10.24 s of diffuse channel and noise for both waveforms, 10 payload bits, 352 positions and equal block energy. The 160 ms format sent 64 tones with two looks per code symbol. The 320 ms format sent 32 tones with one look per symbol. At 18 dB block energy and 0.5 Hz Doppler spread, longer tones raised correct per-tone admissions from 81 to 98 of 120 (24 paired gains, seven losses), and correct joint admissions from 90 to 101. At 20 Hz and the same energy, the joint rules admitted only 0 and 3 correct words. At 22 dB they reached 81 and 85. These results compare two whole profiles in a single frequency-flat Gaussian fading model with 32 Doppler components. Both lack adequate sensitivity on the wider spread at 18 dB. [Shared-input comparison and calibration](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/block-reception/README.md#one-cell-versus-two-cell-hf-tones)

The 32-component approximation matters more on the fast path. With block power normalized to mean one, its reciprocal variance measures how much independent fading is averaged over a block. At 0.5 Hz this measure was 9.30 for the fixture and 9.40 for a continuous Gaussian Doppler spectrum. At 20 Hz it was 31.96 versus 363.32. The finite model therefore has substantially more block-to-block power variation at 20 Hz. Its recovery counts apply to this channel realization model. [Channel calculation and experiment geometry](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/block-reception/README.md#one-cell-versus-two-cell-hf-tones)

Numerical resolution changed a spacing comparison. On the 0.098 Hz-spaced, 10.24 s tone fixture, a 16-point within-cell rule folded Doppler energy from one edge of the 16-position grid to the other. Analytically integrating the 48 modeled rays reduced the 44 dB error count from 10 to 5 on the same 200 blocks. Independent sample integration reproduced the complex projections to within 2.56 × 10⁻⁹. The residual errors and finite energy grid support a warning about tight spacing on this channel. The high-energy asymptote remains unknown. [Projection and decoder convergence](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/sub9-fsk-rate/quadrature-check.md)

Keep the 160 ms cell as a common comparison and choose a transmit configuration for the credible channel and link budget before transmission. In the existing 4 s hop model at −10 dB-Hz and 0.1 Hz spread, one-cell tones require 14.2 dB more time than the model bound and held tones require 2.2 dB more. The 12 dB difference is about sixteen times the airtime. A receiver cannot obtain that energy retrospectively when the per-tone contributions cease to combine coherently. On faster-changing or stronger links long tones can instead impose an unnecessarily long minimum block or lose frequency diversity. Publish both the slow and fast limits of each profile, the deadline, and the fallback duration. [Retained tone-length study](Fable-5.2/fsk-lattice-design.md#tone-length-one-cell-per-tone-is-the-default)

A fixed peak power, fixed transmit time, and a single continuously active tone maximize emitted energy within the flat part of a hop. They do not establish capacity optimality or fix sensitivity under arbitrary fading. Gursoy, Verdú and Poor derive different low-SNR behavior for on/off FSK under bounded peak-to-average ratio and under an absolute peak limit with vanishing average duty. Their energy-efficiency objective allows average power and duty to change. For this modem a comparison must also hold the deadline, peak and actual radiated energy constraints explicit. It would be a different experiment to raise the instantaneous power on the surviving tones. [On-Off Frequency-Shift-Keying for Wideband Fading Channels](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0510075) · [prior peak-limited Matrix calculation](Sol-6.2/fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md)
