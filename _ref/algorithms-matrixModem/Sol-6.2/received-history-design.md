# Received-history correlation for Matrix Modem

24 November 2026. The design target is a simple weak-signal waveform whose receiver can issue large, useful Tensor Core batches when the channel and public search require them. The prior Blackwell run used FP32 cuFFT as a regular-grid control. It demonstrated a faster projection stage for that grid, and the hardware counter request was denied. **Develop the format around FSK soft evidence and a small fixed code, with received history available as an extra statistic.** A repeated pattern deserves a direct equal-airtime comparison. Begin by evaluating it in a narrow channelized representation, with a raw correlation reference. The low-SNR penalty and channel memory rule out requiring raw periodicity as the only admission test across the intended HF envelope. The least complicated Matrix candidate with a genuine dense multiply is a batch of bounded timing/frequency hypotheses scored against a short codebook. Use history correlations where they add independently calibrated evidence, then measure whether enough such work is ready to keep Tensor Cores busy. ## When a received reference is useful

A pair of repeats can preserve delayed echoes, filter distortion and other structure that a nominal tone template omits. It can also share an interfering signal, and the received reference contributes its own additive noise. If a fixed waveform `s` is observed as `x=s+n₁` and `y=s+n₂` with independent proper complex noise of variance `ν` per component, energy `E=‖s‖²`, and `N` independent complex components, then

`E[xᴴy]=E`, `Var₀(xᴴy)=Nν²`, and `Var₁(xᴴy)=Nν²+2νE`.

The squared mean shift divided by null variance is `(E/ν)²/N`. At `C/N₀=−10 dB-Hz`, a 160 ms repeat has `E/ν=0.016` before losses under this normalization. With `N=512` white sampled dimensions the ratio is `5.0×10⁻⁷`, and with `N=352` independent dimensions, a 2.2 kHz × 160 ms ideal time-bandwidth count, it is `7.27×10⁻⁷`. A known one-dimensional projection has `2.56×10⁻⁴`. This is a noise-product sanity check with identical energy per look, perfectly aligned replicas and known phase when using the complex mean. It is neither a receiver sensitivity estimate nor an applicable degree count for arbitrary colored/overlapping audio. When the tone is unknown, computing and testing many projections incurs a whole-search false-alarm cost. The positive case for using raw history is when useful unknown distortion really occupies a larger subspace and persists strongly between the two windows. [Quek, Win and Dardari on received-reference autocorrelation and interference](https://winslab.lids.mit.edu/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/quewindar-j07.pdf).

A simple channelized version keeps the complex tone projections and their diagonal energies. For an already-associated label, let a vector of `R` disjoint noise-whitened projections have proper complex covariance `I+K` under the signal hypothesis. Compute `zᴴ[I−(I+K)⁻¹]z−log det(I+K)` using a small frozen covariance model. This includes ordinary energy and any off-diagonal evidence supported by relative phase and fading persistence. When `K` is diagonal the score reduces to weighted energies. Compare a few model families with calibrated weights. A freely fit covariance for every arrival can overfit interference, noise, and adjacent labels.

For fixed total received energy `e=tr K`, the Gaussian divergence from the null is `sum_j [λ_j−ln(1+λ_j)]` for covariance eigenvalues `λ_j`. At low signal power this is approximately `tr(K²)/2`. If energy is equally distributed over `R` independent fades the divergence is about `e²/(2R)`. If a known relative phase and a common fade put all projected energy into one mode, it is about `e²/2`. For `e=0.096` (the mean upper energy budget of a 960 ms record at −10 dB-Hz), the exact values are 0.004333 nats for one equal-energy mode, 0.002233 for two and 0.000760 for six. This is a local test of signal presence under known covariance with fixed energy. Independent fades can still reduce message outage and help FEC and interleaving. An `R×R` Gram matrix contains `R(R−1)/2` unique off-diagonal products of the *same* `R` observations. Their shared inputs and the signal covariance govern their joint calibration. More pairs add computation without increasing the original energy or observation count. [Matched subspace detection](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.301849), [two-look experiment](../../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/repetition-screen/README.md).

Phase-invariant history remains testable. One simple statistic compares background-centered per-tile powers `qᵢ=|zᵢ|²−1`. If `z₁,z₂` are properly Gaussian with independent unit-variance noise and equal projected signal variance `ρ`, then `E[q₁q₂]=ρ²(1+|γ|²)` under signal, with `γ` the signal correlation. Its extra repeat evidence is second order at weak `ρ`. The ordinary energy shift is first order, and the expected complex cross-product `ργ` is first order if the correlation and phase are usable. Include centered-power history, ordinary soft energies, and complex history in the same comparison. A persistent narrowband interferer, noise covariance error or overlapping windows can raise history scores systematically. Calibrate their maxima across the public search using noise and interferer records that preserve those dependencies. [Narrowband-interference analysis of autocorrelation synchronization](https://telin.ugent.be/~hs/full/j27.pdf), [observer models](observer-models-review.md).

## Channel limits that shape the repeats

- **Doppler spread and oscillator offset.** For aligned repeated samples, a constant common offset `f₀` rotates a lag `Δ` product by `2πf₀Δ`. Its phase can estimate offset modulo `1/Δ` when the transmitting phase relation is known and the channel permits it. At the exact `1/T` FSK grid, every tone's lag-`T` phase is the same modulo `2π`, even in a noiseless channel. An unknown common offset and repeat boundaries make identification harder. The classic OFDM repeat method uses designed synchronization and separate acquisition steps. Timing plateaus and narrowband-interference effects transfer as counterexamples to relying on one peak. [Schmidl and Cox, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1109/26.650240), [Minn et al.](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~hxm025000/CLJul00.pdf).
- **The useful lag can be short.** For a stationary Gaussian tap with full width `w=2σ`, the point-sample half-correlation lag is `sqrt(2 ln 2)/(πw)`. It is about 750 ms at 0.5 Hz, 60 ms at 6.25 Hz, and 12.5 ms at 30 Hz. The correlation of projections uses window-integrated covariance. At 6.25 Hz, two adjacent 160 ms projected signal components correlate about 0.17 and a two-symbol lag about 0.000098. A receiver that needs shorter raw repetition intervals has to account for lost per-look energy, wider tone separation and delayed tails whose extent can be comparable to the interval. [Aperture and leakage calculations](receiver-and-evaluation-review.md#channel-memory-at-the-receivers-actual-aperture), [F.1487 channel scenarios](channel-envelope-review.md#what-established-channel-models-cover).
- **Hopping changes the channel seen by the reference.** Repeating a motif at another frequency retains its labels while changing the channel coefficients. In an equal-power, two-uncorrelated-path model with differential delay `τ`, the normalized cross-frequency covariance magnitude is `|cos(π δf τ)|`. At `τ=2 ms` and `δf=250 Hz` it is zero. This is a concrete counterexample even when the taps are stationary in time over the repeat. A hop can supply useful diversity to noncoherent coding in the same situation. Research on frequency-hopped differential GFSK similarly finds errors across hop boundaries when the previous reference is decorrelated, under its own land-mobile model. [Liu et al., ICC 2003](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~hliu/papers/FHGFSK-ICC03.pdf).

Recent FSK work optimizes soft metrics and coding with unknown Doppler frequency in a non-fading space-link model. It motivates using soft evidence and comparing practical search coverage. It supplies no quantitative tolerance for HF stochastic spread. Recent joint synchronization work shows that channel inference and payload decisions can be studied together at finite blocklength. Its waveform and memoryless block-fading assumptions differ from this link. The first design can use explicit small covariance families and fixed code scoring without requiring a learned receiver. [Gómez-Vilardebó et al., JSAC 2024](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10436069), [Chen, Durisi and Häger, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41580).

## A format comparison small enough to falsify

Use shaped, continuous-phase binary FSK initially. Hold the tone grid and transitions, receive band and known reference plane fixed. Use 160 ms symbols with spacing 12.5 or 18.75 Hz as first comparisons, and retain 6.25 Hz as the exact ideal-grid stress. For the 160 ms transmission compare 80/40 ms receiver apertures or subspace looks on the same samples. Separately test a shorter transmitted-symbol geometry with an appropriate guard, spectral mask and spacing. Short analysis looks and shorter transmission symbols change different things. Keep room for a long-integration profile for exceptionally weak and slow-fading cases, since the earlier model shows a large cost for forcing 160 ms in those cases. [Duration and sensitivity audit](information-limits-review.md#audit-of-the-repositorys-97998-calculation), [tone spacing review](waveform-and-repetition-review.md).

For a finite matched test, use three information bits `(a,b,c)` within six fixed slots. Equalizing count, transmit power, and slot duration fixes the nominal signal energy and elapsed time. If smoothing, guards or synchronizers differ, count them and normalize at the waveform boundary. The calendar and the allowed placements are public configuration. Compare these formats:

| Format | Slot labels | What the comparison can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeat each label adjacent | `a,a,b,b,c,c` | Shortest supported repeat lag. Uses same-frequency phase/covariance when present, with ordinary noncoherent soft combining on the identical waveform as the control. A long single tone or an interferer can also create a strong lag. |
| Repeat a three-slot motif | `a,b,c,a,b,c` | The whole motif can offer distinctive transitions for timing, after accounting for phase continuity and the samples affected by previous slots. Lag is `3T`, with lower expected channel persistence. The all-equal codewords and false candidates require separate controls. |
| Short parity code | `a,b,a⊕c,c,a⊕b,b⊕c` | Eight known length-six words with minimum Hamming distance three. This compares soft codeword scoring at the same payload, duration and energy. The slot order also handles three consecutive erasures within the block. Retain the same synchronization assistance for all formats. |

Enumeration of the eight words and their coordinate erasures confirms a concrete trade-off in this geometry. Given correctly known erasure positions and perfect surviving bits, both repetition orders recover 12 of 15 two-erasure patterns. The parity code recovers all 15. Of the four placements of a three-slot contiguous gap, adjacent repeats recover zero, the repeated motif recovers four, and the indicated parity order recovers four. The naive systematic order `a,b,c,a⊕b,a⊕c,b⊕c` recovers only three because its last three labels contain just two independent equations. Under general three-erasure patterns the indicated parity order recovers 16 of 20 and both repetition formats recover eight. This supports testing time ordering alongside tone/hop choices. Real deep fades may cause wrong decisions or miss the whole codeword, and reliable erasure flags require receiver evidence.

First score each format with the same calibrated tone energy bank, including a full search over public timing/frequency alternatives. Then test raw and channelized history on the repeated formats for acquisition and incremental decision gains. A repeat-based nomination may change scoring order. Treat using it to exclude any candidate as a separate hypothesis requiring evidence on misses. Run same-frequency and hopping conditions separately at predeclared positions, including reset, boundary transients, a stable interferer and structured wrong-label histories. A two-hop reference can remain in the comparison even if its calibrated weight is near zero. Extend to a second payload/control stratum before generalizing a result from this small code. This frame is a research fixture. Its coding length or metadata never controls physical completion. [Stream and completion rules](stream-keys-and-spectrum.md), [native physical completion contract](../../../docs/robust-manual-capacity.md#physical-completion-and-security).

## Where the matrix arithmetic can pay

There are two distinct useful matrix forms. First, gather recent candidates `X` and historical candidates `Y` as row matrices of `A` and `B` windows with `N` useful complex samples or whitened features. The correlation bank is `X Yᴴ`. A real layout can concatenate I and Q, place signed/conjugate columns in the other operand, and use one real GEMM. With real multiplies and adds counted separately that is approximately `8ABN` operations before packing, alignment and scaling. A time or frequency transform per hypothesis belongs in the cost. If the format fixes only a few legal lags `d`, a running sum of `x[n]conj(x[n−d])` computes the same rectangular lag score with one new product per sample per lag and an add/remove update. For sliding cross-correlation or frequency-compensated grids, compare known FFT or grouped projections when they preserve required hypotheses. Reserve a dense history GEMM comparison for enough genuine cross-pairs, independent geometry, or simulation work to reuse both matrices.

For `N=512`, `A=16`, `B=256`, the raw dense tile contains 16.8 million real operations and 32 KiB of complex FP32 outputs. At 125 new windows/s it processes about 0.131 GOP/s for 256 histories per start, with up to 120 ms to fill 16 new windows from one stream. With `A=B=4096`, the tile contains 68.7 billion operations and 128 MiB of output, before packing or workspace. At 125 starts/s the live fill can take more than 32 seconds. Processing independent simulation trials can fill those batches immediately and directly improve iteration time. A live receiver needs a reusable ring, fused or bounded on-device reductions, and a measured ready-work rate consistent with the allowed wait. Report Tensor activity during the contraction, unique useful scores per second including producer/epilogue, and complete single-stream service under the expensive public geometry. [Tensor evidence and receipts](tensor-execution-evidence.md).

Second, accumulate candidate codeword evidence from the same calibrated per-slot soft features. With `ℓ(b,t,+)` and `ℓ(b,t,−)` from two tone labels, define `L(b,t)=ℓ(b,t,+)−ℓ(b,t,−)`. For sign-valued codewords `C(t,m)∈{−1,+1}`, multiplying `L C` gives twice the codeword log score up to a batch-row constant, when the symbol model factorizes. This is a dense product for a binary alphabet. An `M`-ary one-hot codebook has a sparse gather/sum baseline, and a structured code may admit a faster transform or trellis. The eight-word test deliberately has low matrix work and can establish the likelihood and full-search reference first. Scale to heavier but still legitimate code/subspace/timing hypotheses when a channel or allowable message geometry justifies them. Optimize device feeding and fused reduction for those shapes. A code with memory or a shared fade needs an explicit joint likelihood or a documented mismatch test before using this additive rule. [Existing receiver factorization proposals](receiver-architecture.md), [GPU scheduling and workspace contract](../../../docs/development.md#receiver-cpu-allocation).

The precision choice must state whether wide accumulators and scales are part of the research branch. The original strict INT4 wording and NVFP4 operands with FP32 accumulation describe different experiments. Preserve complete public coverage and keep decision/track arithmetic calibrated. Use FP32 as a small oracle, then test low precision after Helper/DATA isolation with adverse nulls and rare near ties. Given source-matched useful work, qualification requires confirmed matrix instructions/counters where available and matched end-to-end work. Simpler rolling/FFT methods remain valid exact references and emergency CPU routes. [Precision contract and available hardware](int4-and-accelerators.md), [GPU arithmetic controls](../../../docs/gpu-precision-controls.md).

## Decision before implementation

The best current starting pair is **short coded FSK with a shared soft-evidence front end**, and **the same signal budget spent on short FSK repeats, scored with optional history**. Keep the direct raw correlator as a control for stable but mismodeled multipath. On matched noise/fading and search support, demand evidence that the repeat branch improves timing, wrong-label or erasure outcomes after accounting for its redundant slots. A nonstationary phase slip or a narrowband interferer can falsify a design that depends on a coherent repeat. Compare mild, disturbed and delay-heavy fixtures plus one mode-change or outage holdout. Report wanted and search-wide false-alarm decisions, time/energy to correct completion, and worst valid runtime. A portfolio of fixed configured profiles can cover distinct coherence regimes while keeping receiver state simple. A new high-utilization kernel only earns a place when it accelerates useful, sensitivity-qualified work.
