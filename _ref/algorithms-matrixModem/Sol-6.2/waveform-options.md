# Matrix Modem waveform and receiver options

This is a literature synthesis for choosing what to prototype, not a selected or qualified modem design. It concentrates on waveform/receiver alternatives and their channel assumptions. The theoretical energy and four-bit hardware questions are treated in the adjacent Matrix Modem research chapters. No candidate below has been tested as a Matrix Modem.

## Findings in brief

The most directly supported starting family is coded orthogonal or near-orthogonal frequency signaling with noncoherent or short-block coherent soft detection, followed by frequency/time interleaving where channel variation supplies diversity. The appeal is a transparent signal model, simple tone generation, phase-insensitive detection, and a natural bank of matched-filter hypotheses. Published coding studies show that orthogonal noncoherent FSK can approach its own channel capacity under stated fading/CSI assumptions. HF field data also show a real, conditional trade: DSSS can sustain low-rate links when the forecast is poor, while OFDM can send faster bursts when conditions are favorable. Neither result establishes an optimum under Matrix Modem's sub-noise-floor received signal and no-feedback operation.

No evidence found makes the matrix-multiplication formulation itself a reason to select a waveform. The receiver must first approximate a valid likelihood or score. Batched template correlation can be used for tone banks, chirp banks, code-phase banks, or short channel templates. Efficient tensor use depends on the workload geometry and the nonlinear/post-correlation work as well as on the waveform.

Six variables should remain distinct in experiments: symbol duration, tone spacing, occupied bandwidth, hop interval, delay spread, and Doppler spread. For ordinary orthogonal tones, a useful baseline is spacing approximately `1/T`; therefore a 160 ms symbol naturally suggests about 6.25 Hz tone spacing. This does not imply that 6.25 Hz is also the channel's Doppler spread, the total occupied bandwidth, or an independently adjustable robustness threshold. A longer `T` improves frequency discrimination and energy per symbol but lowers raw rate and increases exposure to within-symbol channel evolution.

## Evidence from HF rather than adjacent bands

The most relevant direct modem comparison located is Bergadà et al.'s long-haul Antarctica–Spain HF study. It uses measurements from the 2010/11 and 2011/12 campaigns and concludes that direct-sequence spread spectrum is useful for low-rate transmission when the channel forecast is poor, whereas multicarrier techniques send sporadic higher-rate bursts when the forecast is good. Its result is conditional on one five-hop path, its sounder history, power/antenna setup, and its tested DSSS and OFDM implementations. It does not show that DSSS is universally better in deep weak-signal detection, nor does it test a no-feedback fixed-rate receiver under all conditions. The earlier group result also reports DSSS lower-rate but better BER than OFDM on that path. [Bergadà et al. 2014, *Radio Science*](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005203)

HF dispersion measurements make clear why a single generic fading model cannot decide the waveform. A disturbed wideband 15.5 MHz measurement reported about 100 μs rms multipath spread, while its full data set had median rms Doppler shift magnitude around 0.5 Hz and 90th percentile just below 3 Hz. Other channel-probe data from high latitudes include multi-millisecond delay spreads and Doppler widths in tens of hertz, with measurement windows and detected-signal selection limiting their interpretation. One long Antarctica–Spain path reported maximum Doppler spread 2–2.4 Hz over a stated observation period. These are different geometries, estimators, and strata. They should not be collapsed into one supposedly representative “HF worst” number. The established project survey chapters contain the source-by-source conditions and caveats. [Nissen and Bello 2003](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002RS002746), [Ads et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012RS005074), [Angling et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1029/97RS02206)

Older ionospheric sounding measured one-hop polar-cap, auroral, and equatorial HF signals with delay/range distortions of hundreds of microseconds and Doppler-domain distortions of tens of hertz. This is strong evidence that severe propagation can escape the comfortable assumptions of one constant complex gain or a quiet-ionosphere Watterson realization. It is not a probability distribution for ordinary mid-latitude amateur contacts. [Basler et al. 1988](https://doi.org/10.1029/RS023i004p00569)

The project-local synthesis reports typical median mid-latitude trough Doppler widths near instrument resolution, but conditional 95th-percentile widths of roughly 1.5–2.5 Hz in many conditions and 5–13 Hz in selected winter/equinox nighttime intervals at sunspot maximum. It also records NVIS and short-path multi-mode delays of several milliseconds and selected cases near 10 ms. These are research leads with measurement-window, path, season, frequency, and selection limits, not a universal guarantee. See [mid-latitude/NVIS/long-haul dispersion](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/31-skywave-dispersion-midlatitude-nvis-longhaul.md), [high/low latitude dispersion](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/32-skywave-dispersion-highlatitude-equatorial.md), and [HF channel models](../../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/33-hf-channel-models-and-simulators.md).

## Noncoherent scoring and the matrix-product connection

For received samples `y[n]` and candidate template `s_h[n]`, a coherent score is the complex correlation `c_h = sum_n y[n] conj(s_h[n])`. Under unknown constant phase and a specified noise model, a common noncoherent statistic is `|c_h|^2`, optionally normalized by a local noise estimate. For MFSK, the templates can be tone vectors, producing the familiar bank of tone energies. If `Y` contains sample windows and `S` contains templates, correlations have dense form `Y S^H` for complex data or paired real matrix products for I/Q. This is a reasonable tensor-core target only if preparation, layout conversion, magnitude/square, noise calibration, maxima/null tests, and decoding do not dominate. It also remains necessary to define whether the template bank spans CFO, clock drift, timing, channel taps, and missing observations. More hypotheses increase both arithmetic and the null distribution's multiple-comparison penalty.

The zero/blank null should be a real hypothesis or a calibrated statistical comparison, not simply the least-valued member of an always-present tone bank. In unknown noise, correlated bins and channel coloration make a nominal independent exponential-bin threshold wrong. Predeclare receiver false-alarm probability and test it on noise-only data, nearby occupied-band controls, wrong keys, and realistic impulsive/interference records. If the transmitted symbols are all nonzero, a continuous receiver still needs code/synchronization evidence and a physical completion rule independent of a packet length. Scoring one expected tone does not prove message completeness.

Low-SNR code design results do not say “use maximum order.” Guillén i Fàbregas and Grant optimize bit-interleaved coded modulation for orthogonal noncoherent FSK under fading models and report near-capacity decoding for their tested orders and assumptions. Their own discussion makes the order-versus-energy/spectral-efficiency tradeoff explicit. At high modulation orders, the tone bank consumes more bandwidth and receiver work; binary or low-order alphabets may provide better complete service once spectrum, acquisition, coding, and channel uncertainty are counted. The channel's effective tone distinguishability matters more than the nominal alphabet size. [Guillén i Fàbregas & Grant 2007](https://guillen-it.github.io/files/journals/paper_fsk_final.pdf)

## Duration, spacing, guards, and spectral shaping

For a rectangular symbol of duration `T`, frequency bins separated by `1/T` are orthogonal in the ideal flat, synchronized channel. Abruptly gated tones have sinc sidelobes. Smooth amplitude shaping or phase-continuous transitions can reduce out-of-band energy, but change the matched templates and exact cross-symbol orthogonality. A transmitter should measure the actual generated waveform after interpolation and filtering, then through a representative transceiver audio passband. A nominal tone-bank width inside 450–2650 Hz does not guarantee FCC mask compliance or survival of every radio filter.

Frequency spacing should be related to the channel's Doppler width and the receiver's CFO uncertainty. If neighboring tones are closer than the effective time-varying spectral blur, their channel outputs overlap. Simply widening the spacing lowers the number of tones supportable in a fixed channel; lengthening symbols narrows nominal bin width and can improve Eb/symbol but reduces rate and can span more channel evolution. There is no universal rule that tone spacing only needs to exceed “typical Doppler.” The relevant criterion is a measured error/false-alarm surface over Doppler spectrum, delay profile, frequency-selective gain, oscillator errors, and the chosen detector.

Delay spread acts differently. A long symbol makes a few milliseconds a smaller fraction of `T`, but an echo can still cross symbol boundaries, create frequency-selective nulls, and cause ISI. Candidate controls include a guard interval, overlap-aware matched filtering, a small multi-tap model, or a longer error-control code/interleaver. A guard wastes transmitted time and may be inadequate for a rare long echo. A longer code can correct erasures and scattered errors but increases the time over which a persistent fade can erase all evidence. Interleaving helps only when its depth spans enough independent channel states; it cannot manufacture diversity in a fade lasting the whole codeword.

Frequency interleaving/hopping should be compared against the measured coherence bandwidth rather than an assumed “frequency diversity” benefit. A rough reciprocal delay-spread scale is useful only as an order-of-magnitude guide, and its exact value depends on the delay-power shape and chosen correlation threshold. At 2.2 kHz, the available diversity order is finite. Full-band hopping may also create wider instantaneous spectra, amplifier/filter settling issues, or spurious emissions. Record both desired-signal energy and maximum/average spectral occupancy.

## Coding and stream-level framing

Soft symbol or tone likelihoods are preferable to hard tone decisions when computational cost permits; decoder feedback may improve low-SNR performance but adds iterations. Coded noncoherent FSK papers study BICM with iterative decoding, turbo codes, repeat diversity, and related concatenations. For an initial transparent baseline, compare a convolutional or LDPC family with soft input, with and without a modest frequency/time interleaver. Avoid choosing a code based only on asymptotic threshold. The useful metric is complete bits delivered per transmit second at a declared message failure probability and fixed radiated energy, including synchronization, pilots, guards, puncturing/termination, acquisition and quiet completion.

Because the Data Pump receiver accepts a continuous stream and must not infer DSP geometry or successful completion from transmitted length, coding/framing should preserve bounded memory and allow indefinite reception. Useful designs can use periodically synchronized fixed-size coding blocks, a stream code with bounded decoder state, or independently recoverable chunks with fixed geometry and strong integrity checks. A quiet interval can serve as one physical observation, but message completion requires a defined scored quiet-slot rule and delayed-tail allowance. Interior missing blocks remain erasures if later signal resumes. No CRC or decoder success alone proves the transmitter has finished. For this task, compare coding on long streams with inserted quiet slots, partial fades, missed blocks, and continuous key/schedule state.

## Low intercept and observable waveform structure

Encryption of the data and a secret hopping/order schedule can remove direct semantic structure from the payload, but they cannot conceal received energy from an observer whose integration bandwidth and observation duration are sufficient. Randomized-looking constant-envelope FSK or hopping can reduce stable spectral lines or make a single narrowband monitor less effective, while spreading energy across frequency can make energy detection easier for a sufficiently wide receiver. Receiver-site LPI therefore requires an explicitly bounded observer, bandwidth, false-alarm rate, observation period, noise uncertainty, propagation channel, and detection test. The actual-versus-noise experiment proposed by the user is the appropriate empirical question; simulation should first compare known-noise energy detection, unknown-noise energy detection, cyclostationary/periodic structure, and keyless matched-pattern detection. Do not present encryption as a physical detectability gain.

## Recommended comparison sequence and falsifiers

1. **Establish an idealized score baseline.** Generate a flat-channel tone alphabet and matched AWGN/noise-only cases. Verify the predicted correlation distribution and false-alarm calibration. Compare coherent and unknown-phase energy detectors at equal energy. This isolates detector correctness from HF.
2. **Add measured channel families separately.** Use source-grounded quiet midlatitude, selected disturbed midlatitude/NVIS, low-latitude spread-F, and high-latitude auroral records/models from the current HF research set. Preserve original time/frequency/delay scaling, complete fades and channel state. Treat public paths as conditional examples, not as occurrence frequencies.
3. **Pair candidate waveforms on identical channel innovations.** Compare MFSK, segmented correlations, DSSS, chirp and OFDM at equal in-band energy, allocated bandwidth, message payload, synchronization burden, and fixed configuration. Include Doppler ramp/spread, several discrete echo modes, frequency-selective gain, filter skirts, and the actual waveform audio chain. First compare against the simplest robust noncoherent baseline.
4. **Add schedule diversity and soft coding.** Compare fixed tones against keyed hopping and a no-hop interleaved code. Include no-feedback fixed settings, long correlated outages, erasures, impulsive bursts, wrong-key admission, and message-integrity failures.
5. **Consider OTFS only if a measured channel feature points there.** Its discriminator is whether its 2-D representation/equalizer improves complete service or produces a more GPU-efficient likelihood calculation on the same records. If accuracy gains require fragile channel estimates or much more iterative work, it has not solved the stated problem.
6. **Measure the actual observer question.** At receiver-site signal/noise levels after channel and transceiver filtering, publish ROC curves for energy and structure detectors versus observation length. Repeat under noise uncertainty and another receiver bandwidth. There is no single LPI duration independent of observer and background.

For every comparison, report bit error/erasure and complete-message success, wrong-key/false-completion rates, onset/detection delay, bits per second, joules radiated, emission spectrum, required signal energy per bit, exact receiver hypotheses, and complete receiver work. A waveform can win isolated BER and lose because it costs more acquisition time, transmit time, energy, or compute. No “fundamentally ruled out” conclusion follows from an earlier GPU implementation's poor utilization.
