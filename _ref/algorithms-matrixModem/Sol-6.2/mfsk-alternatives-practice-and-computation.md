# MFSK alternatives: field practice and receiver computation

25 November 2026 · Literature review with cross-checks against new calculations

This note answers the practical half of the question “has anything been shown to be substantially better than MFSK, in receiver computation, sensitivity or both, for weak signals on disturbed channels?” It surveys fielded weak-signal systems, military HF, planetary entry links, GNSS weak-signal acquisition, low-power HF field trials and the computational literature. The theoretical half, and the calculations, are in [the fading-limit study](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md). Earlier comparisons already in this directory are summarized in [Alternatives to MFSK](weak-signal-mfsk-alternatives.md) and are not repeated here.

“Substantially better” is taken to mean more than about 2 dB of sensitivity at matched rate, bandwidth, energy and channel, or an order of magnitude less complete-receiver computation. Access is marked **[full]** (primary text read), **[abs]** (abstract or metadata), **[sec]** (secondary source) or **[derived]** (arithmetic or simulation done for this review). Thresholds are converted with `C/N₀ = SNR + 33.98 dB` for a 2500 Hz reference and `+34.77 dB` for 3000 Hz, and `Eb/N₀ = C/N₀ − 10·log₁₀(R_b)`.

## Findings in brief

1. **No study found shows a gain of more than about 2 dB over well-implemented coded noncoherent MFSK on Doppler-spread or multipath channels at matched conditions.** The one large sensitivity gain found, Turbo-FSK at about 3 dB below practical noncoherent MFSK, requires a phase reference across repeated symbols and ideal synchronization in AWGN. That condition does not hold on Doppler-spread HF.
2. **The strongest fielded weak-signal receiver for spread channels is itself an MFSK estimator-correlator.** Q65 weights each tone's bins by `g·Es/N₀/(1+g·Es/N₀)` from Gaussian or Lorentzian spread tables, tests spread hypotheses in 9% steps, and decodes with a GF(64) repeat-accumulate code. Its published threshold degrades smoothly up to 100 Hz of spread. This is a working precedent for the receiver form that the [new calculations](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#practical-receivers-against-the-bound) find to approach the bound.
3. **The most robust military HF waveforms are coded orthogonal signals.** STANAG 4415 and the 3G ALE burst waveforms use 32-chip Walsh symbols detected without training. Walsh orthogonal signaling has the same AWGN performance as MFSK of the same order. The 6–9 dB measured advantage of 3G over 2G ALE comes from soft-decision coding and longer integration replacing hard-decision Golay words with majority voting. It is not evidence against coded MFSK.
4. **Where MFSK was chosen for extreme links, coherent reception was infeasible, and the hypothesis count governed both threshold and cost.** The Mars Exploration Rover entry link used 256-tone MFSK because a carrier loop would have needed about 22 dB-Hz in the carrier alone. Acquisition, not tone detection, set the threshold. Shrinking the Doppler-rate search by tracking gained 2–3 dB and reduced computation.
5. **Under 10–30 Hz simulated spreads, only wide-tone MFSK modes kept copying in an amateur PathSim comparison.** DSSS (Chip64/128), incremental-frequency keying (DominoEX, THOR), PSK and MT63 failed even at +10 dB SNR. A low-power NVIS field study that ranked FSK last used 0.5 ms uncoded FSK symbols at high SNR and does not represent weak-signal MFSK.
6. **No study found an order-of-magnitude computation saving at equal sensitivity for weak signals of uncertain frequency.** The sparse FFT gives about 2× at moderate SNR and degrades near threshold. Hypothesis-space reduction (tracking, prior timing, aiding) is the dominant lever in every domain surveyed. On GPUs, FFTs are bandwidth-bound and tensor-core transforms pay off only when the whole chain is fused.

These findings agree with the calculations in [the fading-limit study](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md). Better implementations may still exist. The survey found no head-to-head 2018–2026 study of MFSK against chirp, OTFS or DSSS on Watterson channels at low SNR, so the strongest statements here rest on theory, fielded precedent and simulation, not on a matched field trial.

## Fielded weak-signal MFSK: Q65 as the benchmark

**Code and metric.** Q65 uses a (65,15) Q-ary repeat-accumulate code over GF(64), punctured to (63,13) with a 12-bit CRC, plus 22 sync symbols, for 85 channel symbols [Taylor, Somerville, Franke and Palermo, *Quick-Start Guide to Q65*, 2021, p. 1, [full]](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/Q65_Quick_Start.pdf). The fast-fading intrinsic metric in `q65_intrinsics_fastfading()` (WSJT-X `lib/qra/q65/q65.c`, read through a third-party port) forms a per-tone weighted sum of bin energies with weights `w_k = g_k·EsNo/(1+g_k·EsNo)/σ²`. The tap profile `g_k` comes from Gaussian or Lorentzian tables indexed by the B90 spread–duration product in logarithmic 9% steps, clamped to about 0.9–210 Hz. The source comment calls the metric heuristically optimal. These weights are the classical estimator-correlator `S/(S+N₀)` restricted to each tone's window. The code assumes an `Es/N₀` degraded by about 8 dB at the maximum spread (240 Hz for QRA64). The weighted sum covers a few to a few dozen bins per tone, which is cheap next to the FFT. [full source, third-party port]

**AWGN thresholds and a priori decoding.** The guide's table gives 50% decoding thresholds from −22.2 dB in 2500 Hz for 15 s sequences to −33.8 dB for 300 s sequences, and down to −37.4 dB with full a priori decoding. The guide states that decoding works with Doppler spreads up to ten times the tone spacing. [full]

**Field comparisons.** QRA64, Q65's predecessor, was reported to give more than 3 dB coding gain without a priori information and 1.9 dB of sync gain over JT65, and about 6 dB over JT4 on 10 GHz EME with the fast-fading metric [Palermo, EME 2016 slides, slides 22–26, [sec]](https://sambuz.com/doc/q-ary-repeat-accumulate-codes-for-weak-signals-ppt-presentation-302291). QRA64 is a QRA(13,64) code with a CRC-6; a decode at −27.8 dB in those slides corresponds to `C/N₀` = 6.2 dB-Hz and `Eb/N₀` of 3.2 dB counting data symbols or 4.5 dB including sync [derived]. A 1000-trial-per-point comparison at 10 GHz found QRA64 ahead of JT4F at 50 Hz spread by 2.8 dB on free text, 3.4 dB against a random station, 0.9 dB on schedules and 1.9 dB on beacons, with the advantage about 1 dB at 20–50 Hz in a spread sweep and falling to zero near 240 Hz [Moncur and Suckling, [full]](https://www.ok2kkw.com/more/comparisons_of_qra64_and_jt4_for_3cm_eme.htm). At 1296 MHz EME, Q65-60A decoded 76% at −27 dB with 5 Hz spread, and 60B–D decoded 98% at −25 dB but 0–10% at −26 dB with 33 Hz spread; the message appears to have been a priori assisted [Atkins, “Q65 – choice of mode for 1296 EME,” [sec]](https://bobatkins.com/radio/q65_mode_selection.html). These are improvements within coded noncoherent MFSK.

**Transfer to Matrix Modem.** Q65 confirms that a spread-weighted tone metric, a modest spread-hypothesis grid and a nonbinary code work in the field on spread channels. Its a priori decoding gains (1.5–3.6 dB) come from shrinking the plausible message set and do not apply to an encrypted payload. Its sync, message length and spread hypothesis search are tuned for minute-scale amateur exchanges, not for the multi-hour weak HF messages in [the time-per-message table](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#how-hard-the-limit-is-in-hf-terms).

## Military HF: orthogonal Walsh signaling

- **2G versus 3G ALE.** The FFI overview describes 2G links as 8-FSK at 125 baud, Golay coded, each word sent three times with hard majority decisions, and calls them "not particularly robust" at low SNR. 3G uses 8-PSK chips with Walsh functions and is described as very robust [Jodalen, Otnes and Solberg, *NATO standards for HF communications: an overview and technical description*, FFI/RAPPORT-2004/02015, Table 3.1 and § 12.1.1, [full]](https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/nato-standards-for-hf-communications-an-overview-and-technical-description). Johnson's analysis reports a measured 3G improvement of 6–9 dB over 2G in AWGN and fading [Johnson, *Analysis of Third-Generation HF ALE Technologies*, § II.A, [full]](http://wireless.nmsu.edu/hf/papers/3g_analysis.pdf). The 2G baseline is a hard-decision, short-symbol design (8 ms symbols, 392 ms per triple-sent word), so this does not compare against a well-coded MFSK receiver. A vendor states 3G linking at −12 dB, 6 dB below 2G, and a secondary wiki puts 2G 50% linking near −1.5 dB in AWGN; the MIL-STD-188-141 tables that would confirm either were not accessible [sec].
- **STANAG 4415.** It uses rate-1/2, K=7 coding with two coded bits per 32-chip Walsh symbol and no known training symbols. The FFI overview reports operation down to −7 dB SNR with Doppler spread up to 40 Hz and multipath approaching 12 ms (§ 7). A secondary primer quotes the requirement as BER below 10⁻³ at −9 dB in 3 kHz on AWGN, which is `C/N₀ = 25.8 dB-Hz` and `Eb/N₀ ≈ 7.0 dB` at 75 bit/s, and BER below 10⁻⁴ at −1 to 0 dB on a 10 ms, 0.5–50 Hz two-path channel, about 15–16 dB `Eb/N₀` [MARS-ALE data modem primer v1.09, [sec]](http://www.n2ckh.com/MARS_ALE_FORUM/MIL-STD_MODEM_PRIMER.pdf). A vendor claims operation about 11 dB below the noise floor, about 5.0 dB `Eb/N₀` [sec], and the FFI overview gives −5 dB for the STANAG 4538 low-data-rate burst waveform BW3 (§ 11.2.3). The AWGN figure is comparable to good coded MFSK. The waveform adds frequency diversity at the cost of a wideband chip stream.

Walsh orthogonal signaling detected noncoherently is orthogonal M-ary signaling in a different basis, with the same AWGN error probability as MFSK of the same order. Its symbols need phase coherence over the Walsh symbol, so they are short (tens of milliseconds), and they resolve multipath. The [tone-versus-chirp calculation](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#tone-versus-chirp-and-other-spread-signals) shows why a signal that resolves multipath pays an extra learning cost at weak signal levels. These waveforms were designed for 75 bit/s links at about 20–30 dB-Hz, not for the −10 to +10 dB-Hz regime.

## Planetary entry links and other extreme MFSK

- **Mars Exploration Rover EDL.** A type-III carrier loop would have needed a 13 Hz loop bandwidth and about 22 dB-Hz in the carrier, roughly the whole available power (only about 19 dB-Hz would have been in the carrier). The project therefore sent one of 256 tones every 10 s (0.8 bit/s). Frequency tracking gave about a 6 dB better threshold than a loop. The open-loop search used 1-s FFT segments at 10 Hz resolution (10,000 frequency cells) over 561 Doppler-rate hypotheses (±700 Hz/s in 2.5 Hz/s steps), 5.61 million hypotheses. Tone detection reached 99% near 13 dB-Hz with 100 noncoherently averaged segments, about 14 dB `Eb/N₀`. Acquisition at 99% needed 20.5–22.5 dB-Hz, about 21.5–23.5 dB `Eb/N₀`, and tracking reduced the threshold by 2–3 dB through a smaller search [Satorius, Estabrook, Wilson and Fort, IPN Progress Report 42-153, 2003, §§ II and V, Fig. 9, [full]](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-153/153A.pdf). A later maximum-likelihood search that folds data-tone power into carrier acquisition gained up to 3 dB in theory and 2–3 dB on MER-B data, or 1 dB with an unknown modulation index [Cattivelli, Estabrook, Satorius and Sayed, “Carrier recovery enhancement for maximum-likelihood Doppler shift estimation in Mars exploration missions,” IEEE JSTSP 2(5):658–669, 2008, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSTSP.2008.2005289).
- **Coherence-limited tone detection.** Oscillator stability limited coherent integration to a few seconds, so spectra were averaged incoherently with drift shifting. Detecting one of four tones at −1 dB-Hz in 1000 s this way corresponds to an `Es/N₀` of 29 dB per 2 bits, about 26 dB of `Eb/N₀` [Lanyi and Kahn, IPN Progress Report 42-129, 1997, [abs]](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-129/129E.html). It illustrates the energy-per-coherence-interval penalty that the [bound tables](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#the-bound-and-what-it-assumes) quantify, here with the coherence limited by equipment rather than by the channel.
- **Other missions.** A 2023 CCSDS proposal for MFSK at very low data rates cites MER and Juno heritage; its slides, and the MSL, Juno and Mars 2020 EDL papers, were not accessible. No MFSK use was found on the Galileo probe, Huygens or Cassini.

MFSK was selected where carrier tracking was infeasible, not because a better alternative was rejected. In these cases the hypothesis count dominated both sensitivity and computation. Matrix Modem has the same structure: timing, frequency, drift and spread hypotheses dominate unless Helper or GPSDO references shrink them. [Received-history drift](received-history-drift.md) and [receiver architecture](receiver-architecture.md#acquisition-and-label-scoring) cover the Matrix search in more detail.

## GNSS weak-signal acquisition as an analog

- **Squaring doubles the value of coherent gain.** Psiaki notes that a pre-squaring SNR gain is worth twice its dB value after squaring. Doubling the coherent interval gave 6 dB in the test statistic, part of which was lost to a 20-hypothesis bit-edge search. Acquisition worked at 20.7 dB-Hz with about 4 s of data [Psiaki, ION GPS 2001, [full]](https://gps.mae.cornell.edu/psiaki_iongps2001b.pdf); the paper's C/N₀ convention is 3 dB above a common one. This is the same quadratic low-SNR behavior as the [bound's quadratic regime](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#the-bound-and-what-it-assumes).
- **Differential combining recovers some of the noncoherent loss.** With 1 ms coherent intervals at a false-alarm rate of 10⁻³, conventional differential combining beat noncoherent combining by about 1 dB and a multi-span generalized form by more than 2 dB. It approached coherent performance only when the residual phase was known. At 23 dB-Hz the multi-span form cut mean acquisition time by 94.5% versus 20 ms coherent integration [Dovis and Ta, “High sensitivity techniques for GNSS signal acquisition,” in *Global Navigation Satellite Systems: Signal, Theory and Applications*, InTech 2012, Fig. 8 and Table 3, [full]](https://doi.org/10.5772/29453). For Matrix Modem this corresponds to exploiting phase memory between adjacent coherent segments, which the weighted periodogram already does when the dwell spans several segments. Psiaki's analysis predicted 18 dB-Hz at a missed-detection probability of 0.12, and FFT shifts across the Doppler grid saved about 2× in transforms. The GNSS lessons for MFSK: make the coherent segment as long as the channel coherence allows, exploit phase continuity between symbols only where the Doppler spread is small against `1/(N·T)`, and expect large computational wins only from pruning the search.
- **Sparse FFT acquisition.** QuickSync aliases `p` code periods before a size-`n/p` transform; the forward FFT is not sparse, and the saving comes from the aliasing. It reduced multiplications by a median 2.2× (1.5–4× per satellite, maximum about 3.7×, 4.8× with known Doppler), costing `O(n·√log n)`, and notes the linear-time variant may not work at very low SNR, where several runs must be summed [Hassanieh, Adib, Katabi and Indyk, MobiCom 2012, [full]](https://www.mit.edu/~fadel/papers/quicksync-paper.pdf).
- **GPU GNSS receivers.** A GPU software receiver (GTX 750 Ti, 9.75 MHz 4-bit samples, ±5 kHz search with `10T + 1` Doppler bins for `T` in ms) gained only about 3.3× on acquisition at 10 ms coherent integration, with host–device transfers cancelling the gain at 1–2 ms, while tracking peaked at about 50× [Xu, Ziedan, Guo and Niu, “NAVSDR: A GPU-based modular GPS software receiver,” probably ION GNSS+ 2015 (venue and year unverified), [full, author copy]](https://maiar.github.io/file/navsdr.pdf). No tensor-core GNSS or DSSS acquisition precedent was found.

## Coded noncoherent MFSK and phase-exploiting variants

- **Turbo-FSK.** Asymptotically 0.29 dB from Shannon by EXIT analysis, and 1.35 dB for 128-byte packets. With coherent reception, ideal sync, AWGN, 1000-bit packets, 10 iterations, M = 128 and λ = 4 (spectral efficiency 0.0117), it reached PER 10⁻³ at `Eb/N₀ = 0.12 dB` (SNR −19.2 dB), 5.5 dB better than a LoRa-like orthogonal plus Hamming scheme; FSK with a turbo code reached BER 10⁻⁵ at 2.91 dB. The authors note synchronization at these SNRs may be arduous [Roth, Doré, Ros and Berg, C. R. Physique 18:178–188, 2017, § 3 and § 4.2, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crhy.2016.11.005). The reviewer's calculation puts the noncoherent 128-FSK symbol-MI requirement at 1.59 dB for the same rate, so Turbo-FSK's advantage over practical noncoherent MFSK is about 3 dB [derived]. It depends on phase coherence across repetitions, which a Doppler-spread HF path removes. No fading or noncoherent Turbo-FSK result was found.
- **Code structure matters inside MFSK.** At rate 1/3, coded-modulation capacity exceeds bit-interleaved capacity by about 0.5 dB for 4-FSK and about 1.9 dB for 64-FSK, as summarized from earlier work in the introduction of [Hong, Cheun, Lim and Cho, “Performance of M-ary turbo coded synchronous FHSS multiple access networks with noncoherent MFSK under Rayleigh fading channels,” JCN 15(6):601–605, 2013, § I, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JCN.2013.000109). This favors GF(M) symbol codes such as Q65's over binary BICM at large alphabets. Iterative demodulation gains up to about 1 dB [Valenti and Cheng, IEEE JSAC 23(9):1739–1747, 2005, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2005.853794), turbo-coded noncoherent CPFSK is reported about 1 dB from its modulation-constrained limit [Torrieri, Cheng and Valenti, IEEE TCOM 56(8), 2008, sec], and optimized LDPC codes for noncoherent MFSK gain up to 1.1 dB in a relay setting [Ferrett and Valenti, arXiv 1801.10370, [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10370).
- **Multiple-symbol CPFSK.** Osborne and Luntz report that noncoherent multi-symbol detection of continuous-phase FSK can outperform coherent PSK [IEEE TCOM 22(8):1023–1036, 1974, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOM.1974.1092333). The gain requires phase constant over the observation, `B_D·N·T ≪ 1`. In Matrix terms it is equivalent to a longer coherent dwell, which the [stable-channel rows of the wideband calculation](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#practical-receivers-against-the-bound) show is valuable only on stable or specular channels. With symbols of 0.1–1 s and spreads of 0.1–10 Hz that condition rarely holds on HF. A NASA contractor report finds a modulation index of 0.715 maximizes the efficiency of binary CPFSK [NTRS 19830014226, full].
- **Ionospheric-scatter OFDM-MFSK.** A blind weighted LLR gained about 1.6 dB at BER 10⁻⁴ [Ding, Niu, Hao, Li and Ren, “A novel weighted LLR algorithm for non-coherent OFDM-MFSK system in ionospheric scatter channel,” IEEE Communications Letters 26(6):1383–1387, 2022, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2022.3162076). Again an improvement within the MFSK family.

| MFSK16 | −14 dB | 31.25 bit/s | 5.8 dB |
| PSK10 | −18 dB | 10 bit/s | 6.8 dB |
| THOR11 | −14 dB | 21.5 bit/s | 7.4 dB |
| DominoEX-4 (no FEC) | −15 dB | 15.6 bit/s | 7.8 dB |

On the F.1487 high-latitude moderate channel (3 ms, 10 Hz) at −8 dB, Olivia, Contestia, MFSK16/31 and RTTYM copied 100%. Chip64/128, DominoEX, THOR, all PSK modes and MT63 gave no copy, and even at +10 dB DominoEX, THOR, PSK and MT63 failed while Chip64 copied 90%. On the high-latitude disturbed channel (7 ms, 30 Hz) at −3 dB, Olivia and Contestia copied 100%, MFSK16 75% and Chip64 10%. This is a single-source, single-message comparison, but its ranking under spread is unambiguous: wide-tone MFSK survives and narrow-tone, differential or chip-rate designs fail. FreeDV's OFDM data modes reach 90% of packets at −4 dB on a 1 Hz, 2 ms channel: DATAC4 at 87 bit/s is about 11.4 dB `Eb/N₀` and DATAC13 at 64 bit/s about 12.7 dB [codec2 repository, `README_data.md`, [full]](https://github.com/drowe67/codec2). That is 6–7 dB worse than FT8's AWGN figure, although the channels differ. A 2020 Winlink simulator study tested VARA, PACTOR and ARDOP only at moderate SNR, so it is not a weak-signal result. No independent ROS, JS8 or SuperFox weak-signal measurements were found.

**Low-power NVIS.** On a 97 km NVIS path with 2–2.75 ms delay spread, 3 kHz bandwidth and 87 ms packets of 162 symbols, uncoded 4/8/16-FSK was the worst of the tested modulations at every `Eb/N₀`; at 8 dB, for example, 8-FSK met BER below 4·10⁻³ 4% of the time against 95% for OFDM. The authors recommend 4-QAM at low power and OFDM in high multipath [Maso et al., *Sensors* 20:6232, 2020, [full]](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20216232). The FSK symbols were about 0.5 ms long, comparable to the delay spread, uncoded and evaluated at high SNR. A companion Antarctic sensing study also favors QAM [Porté et al., Radio Science 2020, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019RS006920). These results are relevant to short-symbol, moderate-SNR sensor links and do not test long-symbol weak-signal MFSK.

## Receiver computation

### Tone metrics

- **The weighted periodogram is the correct long-observation receiver, and it is cheap.** In the long-observation limit the optimum receiver for a Gaussian Doppler-spread signal is a filter-squarer-integrator with power response proportional to `S(f)/(S(f)+N₀)` [Van Trees, *Detection, Estimation and Modulation Theory, Part III*, chs. 4 and 11, [from memory]; Kailath, IEEE TIT 6(3):361–366, 1960, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1960.1057563). A thesis on doubly spread channels found filter-squarer-integrator and sampled-correlator receivers within about 1–2 dB of optimum in several examples [MIT thesis, core.ac.uk 4407019, [abs]](https://core.ac.uk/download/4407019.pdf). The filter-squarer-integrator is therefore not itself a loss mechanism in the long-observation limit; its losses arise when the observation is comparable to the channel correlation time. Kailath's paper interprets the optimum Gaussian-channel receiver as a bank of estimator-correlators (1960; the 1961 date belongs to his thesis), building on Price and Green's Rake receiver [Proc. IRE 46:555–570, 1958, from memory].
- **Implicit diversity has an optimum when the signal is strong enough.** For binary orthogonal signaling with square-law combining over `L` Rayleigh branches, the Chernoff bound is best at a mean SNR of about 3.07 (4.87 dB) per branch, giving an exponent of `0.1488·γ_b` against `0.5·γ_b` for noncoherent orthogonal signaling on AWGN, a 5.26 dB penalty [derived, consistent with Kennedy 1969 from memory]. As the alphabet grows, Kennedy's infinite-bandwidth fading capacity equals AWGN capacity, so the penalty shrinks with large-alphabet FSK, although Telatar and Tse note the limit is approached “impractically slowly”. The optimum per-branch SNR is reachable only when the energy per coherence interval is that large ([distinctions](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#distinctions-to-keep-in-view)).
- **Weight errors are cheap; window errors are not.** In the low-energy-coherence limit, deflection losses of required power are ([`deflection_losses.py`](../../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/coherence-limits/literature-checks/results/deflection_losses.txt), [derived]):

  | Receiver against the actual spectrum | Loss |
  | --- | ---: |
  | Gaussian spectrum, unweighted box of half-width 1.4σ (optimum) | 0.25 dB |
  | same, half-width 1σ, 2σ or 3σ | 0.42, 0.47 or 1.15 dB |
  | Gaussian weights with width wrong by 2^¼, √2, 2 or 4 | 0.03, 0.13, 0.49 or 1.64 dB |
  | flat spectrum, box 1.5, 2 or 4 times too wide | 0.88, 1.5 or 3.0 dB |
  | Jakes spectrum over 8, 16, 32 or 64 bins, unweighted | 0.47, 0.69, 0.90 or 1.09 dB |

  These are deflection figures for many weak bins. They are not detection probability at very low false-alarm rates, they omit the look-elsewhere cost of selecting among spread hypotheses, and at strong signals the weights saturate. The [new mismatch matrix](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#robustness-to-unknown-spread) gives larger numbers because it reports the rate (time) gap, which in this regime is about twice the dB of the power gap. The two agree after that conversion: a 3× spread error costs about 2.2 dB of transmit time, or about 1.1 dB of power.
- **A power-law detector avoids explicit spread hypotheses.** Nuttall's `Σ|X_k|^ν` with `ν ≈ 2.4–2.5` is reported near optimum when the occupied bins are unknown [Nuttall, “Detection performance of power-law processors for random signals of unknown location, structure, extent, and strength,” NUWC-NPT TR 10,751, 1994, DTIC ADA285868, [abs]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA285868.pdf); a companion report treats generalized likelihood-ratio processors [NUWC-NPT TR 10,739, DTIC ADA285589, [abs]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA285589.pdf). No exact dB figure was found, and it is sensitive to interfering tones.

### Drift and frequency-rate search

| Method | Cost | Sensitivity | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brute-force drift trials | `N_f·N_t·N_d` | exact | hyperseti and BLISS documentation [full] |
| Taylor tree (turboSETI) | `N_f·N_t·log₂N_t` | mean loss 1.4 dB at 512 rows, 1.8 dB (worst 2.5 dB) at 4096 rows | [Margot et al., AJ 161:55, 2021, Table 4, [full]](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/abcc77) |
| FDMT | `≤ 2N_f·N_t + N_t·N_Δ·log₂N_f` | brute-force sensitivity for curve families with one curve per point pair | [Zackay and Ofek, ApJ 835:11, 2017, § 4, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373) |
| Fast folding (periodic signals) | `O(b·N·log N)` | 0.93 search efficiency versus 0.70 for FFT plus harmonic summing | [Morello et al., MNRAS 497:4654, 2020, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701) |

GPU turboSETI ran 6.8–10.6× faster than CPU, with dedoppler and hit search taking about 90% of runtime [Cruz, Farah and Elkins, “Search for extraterrestrial intelligence: GPU accelerated TurboSETI,” SciPy 2022, [full]](https://doi.org/10.25080/majora-212e5952-004). GPU Fourier-domain acceleration search spent about 42% in cuFFT, 20% in complex multiply and 35% in power spectra and was memory-bandwidth limited [Dimoudi et al., ApJS 239:28, 2018, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05335). The tree's 1.4–2.5 dB loss is a fixed sensitivity penalty that a GPU can avoid with brute-force or FDMT-style exact search. Prix and Shaltev give a fixed-compute optimization of segment length, mismatch and grid spacing for semi-coherent searches [PRD 85:084010, 2012, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4321), which is the right framework for choosing Matrix coherent segment length against drift-grid density.

Further details that bear on robustness and workspace:

- **The tree fails drastically outside its design range.** Margot et al.'s Table 4 gives mean recovered power of 100% at 4 rows, 81.6% at 64, 72.4% (worst 60.2%) at 512 and 66.0% (worst 56.8%) at 4096. Beyond the unit drift rate (0.15 Hz/s in that search) efficiency falls roughly in inverse proportion to the drift rate, to about 4%, and averages about 16.5% over the ±4 Hz/s Breakthrough Listen range. The in-range loss comes from reusing partial sums along dyadic approximations of straight lines, which deviate by about `log₂(n)/6` pixels [fast discrete Radon transforms, Götz and Druckmüller 1996, Brady 1998, via arXiv 2311.10064, abs].
- **Exact search is memory-bound, not arithmetic-bound.** hyperseti chose brute force because GPUs parallelize across drift trials, and found I/O and peak-finding to be larger bottlenecks than the search; its drift plane needs `N_d × N_chan × 4` bytes, about 4 GB for 2²⁰ channels and 1000 drifts, and a boxcar correction recovers the `√N` smearing loss [hyperseti documentation, full]. BLISS needs 11.6 GB for 2²⁰ channels × 16 steps × 1000 drifts [BLISS documentation, full]. GPU turboSETI processed a 105 GB file in 1 h 45 min on a GTX 1070 Ti against 12 h on an i7-7700K. For Matrix Modem, brute-force cost `N_t·N_f·N_d` is small when there are few spectra per dwell; memory and peak-finding dominate, so drift-plane workspace belongs in the receiver's memory admission.
- **FDMT** applies to any curve family with exactly one curve through any two points, which includes linear and quadratic drift. No published FDMT implementation for drift search was found.
- **Other search kernels.** Fast folding interbinning loses at most 7.4% (about 0.3 dB) of S/N, and the fast folding transform costs about `N·log₂N` per trial [Morello et al. 2020]. A custom shared-memory FFT in the GPU acceleration search ran 1.5–3.2× faster than cuFFT on Maxwell and about 1.5× on P100, and the pipeline 4–6× faster than PRESTO-GPU and OpenMP [Dimoudi et al. 2018]. The 2025 SETI@home front end coherently dechirps over about 123,000 drift rates within ±100 Hz/s [arXiv 2506.14718, abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14718).
- **Semi-coherent scaling.** In the weak-signal Gaussian approximation the threshold scales as `h_th⁻² ∝ T/√N` for `N` incoherent segments over a fixed time `T`, an `N^¼` amplitude penalty; the exact exponent varies from about 1 to 3.5 with the false-alarm probability, and fine-grid and coarse-grid mismatches contribute equally [Prix and Shaltev, eq. 39, Fig. 2]. This is the same quadratic low-SNR behavior as the bound.

### Acquisition grids

Grid losses are implementation losses separate from the channel [derived, standard window and timing arithmetic]:

| Grid | Worst-case loss |
| --- | ---: |
| frequency, rectangular window, no zero-padding | 3.92 dB |
| frequency, 2× zero-padding | 0.91 dB |
| frequency, interbinning (Fourier interpolation) | about 0.3 dB |
| timing, window hop `T/2` (worst offset `T/4`), coherent window | 2.50 dB |
| timing, window hop `T/4` | 1.16 dB |
| timing, window hop `T/8` | 0.56 dB |

For the timing rows the loss is `−20·log₁₀(1 − τ/T)` at worst offset `τ`; with a long incoherent dwell it is about half as many dB. These losses add to every hypothesis-limited threshold, and they are the same order as the waveform differences in [the fading-limit study](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#tone-versus-chirp-and-other-spread-signals), so a comparison between waveforms is only meaningful at equal grid density.

### GPU transforms and tensor cores

- **FFTs are bandwidth-bound.** TurboFFT, which does not use tensor cores, reaches about 90% of A100 memory bandwidth in FP32 [arXiv 2412.05824, [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05824). tcFFT's FP16 tensor-core FFT (radix-16 butterflies as 16×16 fragments, radix 2 and 4 on FP16 CUDA cores) is on average 1.90× faster than cuFFT FP16 for 1D on V100 and 1.24× on A100, up to about 3× for 2D, and 96–99.7% of cuFFT on short bandwidth-bound cases; it wins from a batch of 4 at 131,072 points and from a batch of 2 at 512×256. Its mean relative error against FP64 FFTW is 1.76%, against 1.78% for cuFFT FP16 [Li, Cheng and Lin, arXiv 2104.11471, Table 4, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11471). A mixed-precision FFT that splits FP32 into two scaled FP16 values reached errors of about 2.4·10⁻⁷ for inputs in [−1, 1] [Sorna et al., HiPCW 2018, abs], and tensor cores with warp shuffles gave up to 1.5× for FFTs and 4× for number-theoretic transforms [Durrani et al., PACT 2021, abs]. FlashFFTConv's Monarch decomposition costs `O(N^((p+1)/p))` on FP16/BF16 tensor cores; on H100 its forward convolution ran 4.78×, 6.54×, 2.85× and 1.33–1.82× faster than PyTorch at N = 256, 1K, 32K and 1M–4M, reaching 56–62% FLOP utilization on A100 only by fusing the chain. Sub-matrices smaller than 16 waste tensor cores, and higher orders trade FLOPs for I/O [Fu et al., arXiv 2311.05908, Tables 3 and 6, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05908).
- **High tensor utilization needs large batched products.** The Tensor-Core Beamformer's microbenchmarks reach about 92–99% of FP16 peak on A100, MI210 and MI300X but only 65% on GH200, which the authors attribute to the WMMA interface. Tuned products reached 173 TOPS FP16 on A100 (55% of peak), 335 TOPS on GH200 (34%), 603 TOPS on MI300X (46%) and 93 TOPS on an AD4000 (87%), with 1-bit A100 products at 3080 TOPS (62%). Large matrices reach 50–85% of peak, near-optimal results need a few thousand elements per side, and small matrices are memory-bound. LOFAR beamforming ran up to 20× faster than FP32 [Oostrum et al., IPDPS 2025, Table III, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03269). A packed 4-bit CHIME correlator reached 131% of the maximum unpacked rate, against a cited xGPU baseline of 79% of peak [Klages et al., arXiv 1503.06203, abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.06203); Romein's Tensor-Core Correlator is 5–10× faster than xGPU [A&A 656:A52, 2021, abs]. A tensor-core correlator has also been built for SMA and ngEHT [Yu, Romein et al., Galaxies 11(1):13, 2023, abs], and the DSA beamformer converts 4-bit data to 8-bit for cuBLAS tensor operations [abs]. The 1-bit beamformer results report accuracy only qualitatively (reduced contrast, Doppler processing before sign extraction, a padding-bias correction).
- **Accurate arithmetic can be emulated on low-precision tensor cores.** Error-corrected tensor-core GEMM reached SGEMM accuracy at 51 TFLOPS (FP16, limited exponent range) and 33 TFLOPS (TF32) on A100 [Ootomo and Yokota, arXiv 2203.03341, abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03341). Ozaki scheme II emulates DGEMM with 15–16 INT8 GEMMs, 1.4× native speed on GH200 and 18.5× on an RTX 5080 at n = 8192 [arXiv 2504.08009 and 2508.03984, abs].
- **Four-bit tensor paths are not universal.** Hopper INT4 `mma` compiles to CUDA-core IMAD instructions; legacy `mma` averages 62.9% of peak on H800 while `wgmma` exceeds 95% with zero-initialized data [Luo et al., arXiv 2402.13499, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13499). The RTX Blackwell GB202 appendix (RTX 5090) lists dense rates of 1676 TFLOPS FP4, 838/419 FP8 (FP16/FP32 accumulate), 419/209.5 FP16 (FP16/FP32 accumulate) and 838 TOPS INT8, with 1792 GB/s of memory bandwidth, and has no INT4 row at all, so absence there is not proof that the hardware lacks INT4. An RTX 5080 microbenchmark reports that `tcgen05` is not supported on sm_120 and that FP4 and FP6 require `mma … .kind::f8f6f4` [arXiv 2507.10789, Table IV, full](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10789). No percent-of-peak data were found for tensor-core FFTs on Hopper or Blackwell, for GPU dedoppler or for GPU GNSS acquisition. These agree with the [accelerator review](int4-and-accelerators.md).
- **[derived] Fused GEMM-DFT break-even on RTX 5090 at 8 bytes/point traffic**: a direct DFT stays under memory time to about N = 120, a two-factor Monarch DFT to about 3,000 and a three-factor to about 60,000. Tensor-core DFT pays off only when windowing, transform, power, spread weighting and drift accumulation stay on-chip.

### Quantization

Three different quantization losses must be kept apart: of the input samples, of the transform matrix, and of a strong interferer quantized together with the signal. All [derived] values below are from [`quantization_losses.py`](../../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/coherence-limits/literature-checks/results/quantization_losses.txt), which treats quantization error as white noise; real errors include spurs.

- **Input samples.** At Nyquist sampling of Gaussian input, quantization efficiency is 0.637 (2/π), 0.881, 0.963, 0.988, 0.997 and 0.9999 for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 bits; 2 bits loses about 0.55 dB and 4 bits about 0.05 dB. Radio astronomy sometimes sets thresholds wider than optimal on purpose, for interference headroom [Price, arXiv 1607.03579, Table 1, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03579). The simulation reproduces these: INT4 with the optimal 2.5σ clip gives 0.987, and block-16 absolute-maximum scaling gives 0.991 for NVFP4 and 0.993 for INT4 on pure noise [derived].
- **Transform matrix.** Quantizing a 256-point DFT matrix with block-16 scaling leaves a relative error power of −76.6 dB in FP16, −49.8 dB in INT8, −25.5 dB in INT4 and −20.1 dB in NVFP4 [derived]. FP16 FFTs show about 1.8% mean relative error, roughly −35 dB, which is negligible in noise-dominated bins (tcFFT). A four-bit transform's error floor is high enough to matter whenever a strong component is present.
- **Interferer before isolation.** With a narrowband interferer and block-16 scaling, the losses are:

  | Interference-to-noise ratio | 0 dB | 10 dB | 20 dB | 30 dB | 40 dB |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | NVFP4 | 0.08 dB | 0.47 dB | 3.4 dB | 10.2 dB | 20.0 dB |
  | INT4 | 0.06 dB | 0.21 dB | 1.3 dB | 6.3 dB | 15.0 dB |
  | INT8 | 0.00 dB | 0.00 dB | 0.00 dB | 0.04 dB | 0.40 dB |

This supports the existing rule of qualifying four-bit arithmetic only after Helper/DATA isolation ([precision guide](int4-and-accelerators.md#accumulator-range-and-the-meaning-of-only-int4)). Van Vleck-type corrections for multilevel quantization matter only for strong or highly correlated signals [Benkevitch et al., arXiv 1608.04367, abs; CHIME 4+4-bit bias, arXiv 1803.04296, abs].

**Accumulation.** INT8 × INT8 products accumulated in INT32 cannot overflow for fewer than about 1.3·10⁵ terms in the worst case [derived]. FP16 accumulation runs at twice the FP32-accumulate rate on GeForce parts (419 against 209.5 TFLOPS on GB202) but overflows at 65504. PTX integer `mma` has an optional `.satfinite` clamp [from memory]. Power and drift accumulators should stay in FP32.

### Other waveform receivers

- **Chirp spread spectrum** demodulation is a dechirp and a `2^SF`-point FFT per symbol, the same cost order as MFSK. A chirp couples delay and frequency on one ridge, which can reduce acquisition hypotheses; up- and down-chirp preambles resolve the coupled time and frequency offsets. No complete-receiver measurement showing a lower cost was found. Fractional frequency offsets degrade LoRa sensitivity, and carrier and sampling offsets need compensation [Ghanaatian, Afisiadis, Cotting and Burg, arXiv 1811.04146, abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04146). Static Doppler is tolerated, but LoRa SF12 failed under dynamic Doppler on low-orbit passes, while SF ≤ 11 with at least 31.25 kHz bandwidth worked [Doroshkin et al., IEEE Access 7:75721–75731, 2019, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2919274), an example of long chirps being drift-sensitive, the opposite of what long-dwell weak signals need. The fractional Fourier transform costs `O(N log N)` [Ozaktas et al., IEEE TSP 44(9), 1996, abs]. Hyperbolic FM chirps are Doppler-invariant and are used as underwater-acoustic preambles; no quantitative cost comparison against PN preambles was found.
- **Walsh–Hadamard receivers** need only additions; the IS-95 reverse link detected 64-ary Walsh symbols noncoherently with a fast Hadamard transform, and its sources note that the Walsh symbol must be much shorter than `1/f_D`. On GPUs, tensor-core Hadamard kernels (16×16 blocks, sizes up to 2¹⁵) gain only 1.1–1.4× typically on A100 and 1.0–1.3× on H100 (peaks 3.5–3.6×) over a good CUDA-core implementation [HadaCore, arXiv 2412.08832, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08832). Walsh symbols require phase coherence and are not eigenfunctions of a stationary Doppler-spread channel; complex exponentials are. Doppler spread and frequency offset break their orthogonality.
- **Multiple-symbol differential detection** has fast exact algorithms (Mackenthun's `O(N log N)`, dominated by sorting `N + 1` phase candidates [IEEE TCOM 42(2/3/4), 1994, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOMM.1994.582823); sphere-decoding variants by Lampe, Schober, Pauli and coauthors, whose complexity is low over wide ranges but exponential in `N` in principle [abs]). A combinatorial-geometry version has complexity independent of SNR but an exponent that grows linearly with the fading bandwidth [Pauli, Lampe, Schober and Fukuda, IEEE TCOM 56(10), 2008, abs]. GLRT-optimal noncoherent lattice decoding is polynomial for QAM and PAM [Ryan, Collings and Clarkson, IEEE TSP 55(7), 2007, abs]; noncoherent sequence detection is the related trellis approach [Colavolpe and Raheli 1999; Motedayen-Aval and Anastasopoulos 2003]. All of these pay off only when phase memory spans several symbols. With `B_D·T ≫ 1` there is none, and the spread-weighted energy metric is the right statistic; near zero spread they amount to a longer coherent FFT.
- **No waveform family showed a lower complete-receiver cost.** Wideband-fading theory favors peaky FSK-like signaling on noncoherent channels ([Médard and Gallager 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.992769); [Telatar and Tse 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.850678)). One FFT evaluates every frequency hypothesis at once, DSSS adds a Doppler-bin dimension that grows with coherent time, and chirp and Walsh receivers have no measured complete-receiver advantage.

## What this evidence does and does not establish

It establishes that the receiver form the theory points to, a spread-weighted tone energy with a nonbinary code, is already used successfully in the field, and that robust professional waveforms are orthogonal-signaling cousins of MFSK. It establishes that every large measured gain over a given MFSK system came from coding, soft metrics, longer coherent integration, symbol length matched to the channel, or a smaller hypothesis space, not from abandoning orthogonal signaling. It establishes that tensor-core speedups for transforms are modest unless the processing chain is fused and batched.

It does not establish Matrix Modem's sensitivity, acquisition performance or compute ceiling. Most thresholds above come from different message lengths, success criteria, sync overheads and channel simulators. The Q65 spread comparison uses an approximate branch model. The PathSim ranking is a single-source test. No matched 2018–2026 low-SNR HF study of MFSK against chirp, OTFS or DSSS was found. A coherent or partially coherent scheme could still win on a stable or strongly specular path, as [the specular rows of the bound tables](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#where-the-real-channel-can-be-better) show, and a better receiver than those surveyed may exist.
