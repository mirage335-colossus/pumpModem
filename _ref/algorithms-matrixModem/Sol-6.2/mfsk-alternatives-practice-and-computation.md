# MFSK alternatives: field practice and receiver computation

25 November 2026 · Literature review with cross-checks against new calculations

This note answers the practical half of the question “has anything been shown to be substantially better than MFSK, in receiver computation, sensitivity or both, for weak signals on disturbed channels?” It surveys fielded weak-signal systems, military HF, planetary entry links, GNSS weak-signal acquisition, low-power HF field trials and the computational literature. The theoretical half, and the calculations, are in [the fading-limit study](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md). Earlier comparisons already in this directory are summarized in [Alternatives to MFSK](weak-signal-mfsk-alternatives.md) and are not repeated here.

“Substantially better” is taken to mean more than about 2 dB of sensitivity at matched rate, bandwidth, energy and channel, or an order of magnitude less complete-receiver computation. Access is marked **[full]** (primary text read), **[abs]** (abstract or metadata), **[sec]** (secondary source) or **[derived]** (arithmetic or simulation done for this review). Thresholds are converted with `C/N₀ = SNR + 33.98 dB` for a 2500 Hz reference and `+34.77 dB` for 3000 Hz, and `Eb/N₀ = C/N₀ − 10·log₁₀(R_b)`.

## Findings in brief

1. **No study found shows a gain of more than about 2 dB over well-implemented coded noncoherent MFSK on Doppler-spread or multipath channels at matched conditions.** The one large sensitivity gain found, Turbo-FSK at about 3 dB below practical noncoherent MFSK, requires a phase reference across repeated symbols and ideal synchronization in AWGN. That condition does not hold on Doppler-spread HF.
2. **The strongest fielded weak-signal receiver for spread channels is itself an MFSK estimator-correlator.** Q65 weights each tone's bins by `g·Es/N₀/(1+g·Es/N₀)` from Gaussian or Lorentzian spread tables, tests spread hypotheses in 9% steps, and decodes with a GF(64) repeat-accumulate code. Its published threshold degrades smoothly up to 100 Hz of spread. This is a working precedent for the receiver form that the [new calculations](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#practical-receivers-against-the-bound) find to approach the bound.
3. **The most robust military HF waveforms are coded orthogonal signals.** STANAG 4415 and the 3G ALE burst waveforms use 32-chip Walsh symbols detected without training. Walsh orthogonal signaling has the same AWGN performance as MFSK of the same order. The 6–9 dB measured advantage of 3G over 2G ALE comes from soft-decision coding and longer integration replacing hard-decision Golay words with majority voting. It is not evidence against coded MFSK.
4. **Where MFSK was chosen for extreme links, coherent reception was infeasible, and the hypothesis count governed both threshold and cost.** The Mars Exploration Rover entry link used 256-tone MFSK because a carrier loop would have needed about 22 dB-Hz in the carrier alone. Acquisition, not tone detection, set the threshold. Shrinking the Doppler-rate search by tracking gained 2–3 dB and reduced computation.
5. **Under 10–30 Hz simulated spreads, only wide-tone MFSK modes kept copying in an amateur PathSim comparison.** DSSS (Chip64/128), incremental-frequency keying (DominoEX, THOR), PSK and MT63 failed even at +10 dB SNR. A low-power NVIS field study that ranked FSK last used 0.5 ms uncoded FSK symbols at high SNR and does not represent weak-signal MFSK.
6. **No study found an order-of-magnitude computation saving at equal sensitivity for weak signals of uncertain frequency.** The sparse FFT gives about 2× at moderate SNR and degrades near threshold. Hypothesis-space reduction (tracking, prior timing, aiding) is the dominant lever in every domain surveyed. On GPUs, FFTs are bandwidth-bound and tensor-core transforms pay off only when the whole chain is fused.

These findings agree with the calculations in [the fading-limit study](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md). Better implementations may still exist. The survey found no head-to-head 2018–2026 study of MFSK against chirp, OTFS or DSSS on Watterson channels at low SNR, so the strongest statements here rest on theory, fielded precedent and simulation, not on a matched field trial.

## Fielded weak-signal MFSK: Q65 as the benchmark

**Code and metric.** Q65 uses a (65,15) Q-ary repeat-accumulate code over GF(64), punctured to (63,13) with a 12-bit CRC, plus 22 sync symbols, for 85 channel symbols [Taylor, Somerville, Franke and Palermo, *Quick-Start Guide to Q65*, 2021, p. 1, [full]](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/Q65_Quick_Start.pdf). The fast-fading intrinsic metric in `q65_intrinsics_fastfading()` (WSJT-X `lib/qra/q65/q65.c`, read through a third-party port) forms a per-tone weighted sum of bin energies with weights `w_k = g_k·EsNo/(1+g_k·EsNo)/σ²`. The tap profile `g_k` comes from Gaussian or Lorentzian tables indexed by the B90 spread–duration product in logarithmic 9% steps, clamped to about 0.9–210 Hz. The source comment calls the metric heuristically optimal. These weights are the classical estimator-correlator `S/(S+N₀)` restricted to each tone's window. [full source, third-party port]

**Threshold versus spread.** The guide's Figure 1, digitized from its vector paths (curve-to-legend assignment inferred from markers and the guide's AP table), gives the SNR in 2500 Hz for 50% decoding:

| Doppler spread | Q65-60D, no AP | 60D, maximum AP | Q65-120E, no AP | 120E, maximum AP |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 Hz | −27.1 dB | −29.0 dB | −27.5 dB | −29.6 dB |
| 5 Hz | −25.7 dB | −28.0 dB | −26.8 dB | −28.9 dB |
| 10 Hz | −25.2 dB | −27.4 dB | −26.6 dB | −29.0 dB |
| 20 Hz | −24.4 dB | −26.4 dB | −26.3 dB | −28.7 dB |
| 50 Hz | −22.7 dB | −24.6 dB | −25.4 dB | −27.7 dB |
| 100 Hz | −21.0 dB | −22.7 dB | −23.9 dB | −25.8 dB |

The 60D no-AP row loses 6.1 dB of SNR from 0 to 100 Hz. The 120E submode, with wider tones and a longer transmission, loses 3.6 dB over the same range. On AWGN, Q65-60A decodes at −27.6 dB, which is `C/N₀ = 6.4 dB-Hz` and about 1.3 dB above the symbol-wise mutual-information requirement of 64-FSK at Q65's data-symbol rate (2.87 dB `Es/N₀`, [derived] by the reviewer's Monte Carlo). Including sync symbols the total `Eb/N₀` is about 4.6 dB. [derived]

**How close under spread.** Converting the 60D no-AP row to data-symbol `Es/N₀` gives 4.7, 6.1, 7.4, 9.1 and 10.8 dB at 0, 5, 20, 50 and 100 Hz. A model with `L ≈ 1 + B·T` independent Rayleigh branches per symbol requires about 5.3, 7.4, 9.2 and 10.6 dB for 4, 13, 31 and 61 branches at the same rate. On that approximate mapping, Q65's spread penalty is close to the unavoidable noncoherent-combining loss for its symbol length. [derived; the branch mapping and the WSJT spread definition are approximate, so this is indicative only]

**Field comparisons.** QRA64, Q65's predecessor, was reported to give more than 3 dB coding gain without a priori information and 1.9 dB of sync gain over JT65, and about 6 dB over JT4 on 10 GHz EME with the fast-fading metric [Palermo, EME 2016 slides, slides 22–26, [sec]](https://sambuz.com/doc/q-ary-repeat-accumulate-codes-for-weak-signals-ppt-presentation-302291). A 1000-trial-per-point comparison at 10 GHz found QRA64 ahead of JT4F by 0.9–3.4 dB at 50 Hz spread depending on message type, with the advantage falling to zero near 240 Hz [Moncur and Suckling, [full]](https://www.ok2kkw.com/more/comparisons_of_qra64_and_jt4_for_3cm_eme.htm). These are improvements within coded noncoherent MFSK.

**Transfer to Matrix Modem.** Q65 confirms that a spread-weighted tone metric, a modest spread-hypothesis grid and a nonbinary code work in the field on spread channels. Its a priori decoding gains (1.5–3.6 dB) come from shrinking the plausible message set and do not apply to an encrypted payload. Its sync, message length and spread hypothesis search are tuned for minute-scale amateur exchanges, not for the multi-hour weak HF messages in [the time-per-message table](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#how-hard-the-limit-is-in-hf-terms).

## Military HF: orthogonal Walsh signaling

- **2G versus 3G ALE.** The FFI overview describes 2G links as 8-FSK at 125 baud, Golay coded, each word sent three times with hard majority decisions, and calls them "not particularly robust" at low SNR. 3G uses 8-PSK chips with Walsh functions and is described as very robust [Jodalen, Otnes and Solberg, *NATO standards for HF communications: an overview and technical description*, FFI/RAPPORT-2004/02015, Table 3.1 and § 12.1.1, [full]](https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/nato-standards-for-hf-communications-an-overview-and-technical-description). Johnson's analysis reports a measured 3G improvement of 6–9 dB over 2G in AWGN and fading [Johnson, *Analysis of Third-Generation HF ALE Technologies*, § II.A, [full]](http://wireless.nmsu.edu/hf/papers/3g_analysis.pdf). The 2G baseline is a hard-decision, short-symbol design, so this does not compare against a well-coded MFSK receiver.
- **STANAG 4415.** It uses rate-1/2, K=7 coding with two coded bits per 32-chip Walsh symbol and no known training symbols. The FFI overview reports operation down to −7 dB SNR with Doppler spread up to 40 Hz and multipath approaching 12 ms (§ 7). A secondary primer quotes the requirement as BER below 10⁻³ at −9 dB in 3 kHz on AWGN, which is `C/N₀ = 25.8 dB-Hz` and `Eb/N₀ ≈ 7.0 dB` at 75 bit/s, and BER below 10⁻⁴ at −1 to 0 dB on a 10 ms, 0.5–50 Hz two-path channel, about 15–16 dB `Eb/N₀` [MARS-ALE data modem primer v1.09, [sec]](http://www.n2ckh.com/MARS_ALE_FORUM/MIL-STD_MODEM_PRIMER.pdf). The AWGN figure is comparable to good coded MFSK. The waveform adds frequency diversity at the cost of a wideband chip stream.

Walsh orthogonal signaling detected noncoherently is orthogonal M-ary signaling in a different basis, with the same AWGN error probability as MFSK of the same order. Its symbols need phase coherence over the Walsh symbol, so they are short (tens of milliseconds), and they resolve multipath. The [tone-versus-chirp calculation](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#tone-versus-chirp-and-other-spread-signals) shows why a signal that resolves multipath pays an extra learning cost at weak signal levels. These waveforms were designed for 75 bit/s links at about 20–30 dB-Hz, not for the −10 to +10 dB-Hz regime.

## Planetary entry links and other extreme MFSK

- **Mars Exploration Rover EDL.** A type-III carrier loop would have needed a 13 Hz loop bandwidth and about 22 dB-Hz in the carrier, roughly the whole available power. The project therefore sent one of 256 tones every 10 s (0.8 bit/s). Frequency tracking gave about a 6 dB better threshold than a loop. The open-loop search used 1-s FFT segments at 10 Hz resolution over 561 Doppler-rate hypotheses (±700 Hz/s), 5.61 million hypotheses. Tone detection reached 99% near 13 dB-Hz with 100 noncoherently averaged segments, about 14 dB `Eb/N₀`. Acquisition at 99% needed 20.5–22.5 dB-Hz, and tracking reduced the threshold by 2–3 dB through a smaller search [Satorius, Estabrook, Wilson and Fort, IPN Progress Report 42-153, 2003, §§ II and V, Fig. 9, [full]](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-153/153A.pdf). A later maximum-likelihood search that folds data-tone power into carrier acquisition gained 2–3 dB on MER-B data, or 1 dB with an unknown modulation index [Cattivelli, Estabrook, Satorius and Sayed, “Carrier recovery enhancement for maximum-likelihood Doppler shift estimation in Mars exploration missions,” IEEE JSTSP 2(5):658–669, 2008, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSTSP.2008.2005289).
- **Coherence-limited tone detection.** Detecting one of four tones at −1 dB-Hz in 1000 s with incoherent averaging limited by oscillator stability costs about 26 dB of `Eb/N₀` [Lanyi and Kahn, IPN Progress Report 42-129, 1997, [abs]](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-129/129E.html). It illustrates the energy-per-coherence-interval penalty that the [bound tables](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#the-bound-and-what-it-assumes) quantify.

MFSK was selected where carrier tracking was infeasible, not because a better alternative was rejected. In these cases the hypothesis count dominated both sensitivity and computation. Matrix Modem has the same structure: timing, frequency, drift and spread hypotheses dominate unless Helper or GPSDO references shrink them. [Received-history drift](received-history-drift.md) and [receiver architecture](receiver-architecture.md#acquisition-and-label-scoring) cover the Matrix search in more detail.

## GNSS weak-signal acquisition as an analog

- **Squaring doubles the value of coherent gain.** Psiaki notes that a pre-squaring SNR gain is worth twice its dB value after squaring. Doubling the coherent interval gave 6 dB in the test statistic, part of which was lost to a 20-hypothesis bit-edge search. Acquisition worked at 20.7 dB-Hz with about 4 s of data [Psiaki, ION GPS 2001, [full]](https://gps.mae.cornell.edu/psiaki_iongps2001b.pdf); the paper's C/N₀ convention is 3 dB above a common one. This is the same quadratic low-SNR behavior as the [bound's quadratic regime](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#the-bound-and-what-it-assumes).
- **Differential combining recovers some of the noncoherent loss.** With 1 ms coherent intervals at a false-alarm rate of 10⁻³, conventional differential combining beat noncoherent combining by about 1 dB and a multi-span generalized form by more than 2 dB. It approached coherent performance only when the residual phase was known. At 23 dB-Hz the multi-span form cut mean acquisition time by 94.5% versus 20 ms coherent integration [Dovis and Ta, “High sensitivity techniques for GNSS signal acquisition,” in *Global Navigation Satellite Systems: Signal, Theory and Applications*, InTech 2012, Fig. 8 and Table 3, [full]](https://doi.org/10.5772/29453). For Matrix Modem this corresponds to exploiting phase memory between adjacent coherent segments, which the weighted periodogram already does when the dwell spans several segments.
- **Sparse FFT acquisition.** QuickSync reduced multiplications by a median 2.2× (maximum about 3.7×, 4.8× with known Doppler) and notes the linear-time variant may not work at very low SNR [Hassanieh, Adib, Katabi and Indyk, MobiCom 2012, [full]](https://www.mit.edu/~fadel/papers/quicksync-paper.pdf).
- **GPU GNSS receivers.** A GPU software receiver gained only about 3.3× on acquisition at 10 ms coherent integration, with host–device transfers cancelling the gain at 1–2 ms [Xu, Ziedan, Guo and Niu, “NAVSDR: A GPU-based modular GPS software receiver,” probably ION GNSS+ 2015 (venue and year unverified), [full, author copy]](https://maiar.github.io/file/navsdr.pdf). No tensor-core GNSS or DSSS acquisition precedent was found.

## Coded noncoherent MFSK and phase-exploiting variants

- **Turbo-FSK.** Asymptotically 0.29 dB from Shannon by EXIT analysis, and 1.35 dB for 128-byte packets. With coherent reception, ideal sync, AWGN, 1000-bit packets and M = 128, it reached PER 10⁻³ at `Eb/N₀ = 0.12 dB`, 5.5 dB better than a LoRa-like orthogonal plus Hamming scheme. The authors note synchronization at these SNRs may be arduous [Roth, Doré, Ros and Berg, C. R. Physique 18:178–188, 2017, § 3 and § 4.2, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crhy.2016.11.005). The reviewer's calculation puts the noncoherent 128-FSK symbol-MI requirement at 1.59 dB for the same rate, so Turbo-FSK's advantage over practical noncoherent MFSK is about 3 dB [derived]. It depends on phase coherence across repetitions, which a Doppler-spread HF path removes. No fading or noncoherent Turbo-FSK result was found.
- **Code structure matters inside MFSK.** At rate 1/3, coded-modulation capacity exceeds bit-interleaved capacity by about 0.5 dB for 4-FSK and about 1.9 dB for 64-FSK, as summarized from earlier work in the introduction of [Hong, Cheun, Lim and Cho, “Performance of M-ary turbo coded synchronous FHSS multiple access networks with noncoherent MFSK under Rayleigh fading channels,” JCN 15(6):601–605, 2013, § I, [full]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JCN.2013.000109). This favors GF(M) symbol codes such as Q65's over binary BICM at large alphabets. Iterative demodulation gains up to about 1 dB [Valenti and Cheng, IEEE JSAC 23(9):1739–1747, 2005, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2005.853794), and optimized LDPC codes for noncoherent MFSK gain up to 1.1 dB in a relay setting [Ferrett and Valenti, arXiv 1801.10370, [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10370).
- **Multiple-symbol CPFSK.** Osborne and Luntz report that noncoherent multi-symbol detection of continuous-phase FSK can outperform coherent PSK [IEEE TCOM 22(8):1023–1036, 1974, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOM.1974.1092333). The gain requires phase constant over the observation, `B_D·N·T ≪ 1`. In Matrix terms it is equivalent to a longer coherent dwell, which the [stable-channel rows of the wideband calculation](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#practical-receivers-against-the-bound) show is valuable only on stable or specular channels.
- **Ionospheric-scatter OFDM-MFSK.** A blind weighted LLR gained about 1.6 dB at BER 10⁻⁴ [Ding, Niu, Hao, Li and Ren, “A novel weighted LLR algorithm for non-coherent OFDM-MFSK system in ionospheric scatter channel,” IEEE Communications Letters 26(6):1383–1387, 2022, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2022.3162076). Again an improvement within the MFSK family.

## Amateur HF modes and low-power NVIS field trials

**PathSim comparisons.** A single compiler tabulated AE4JY PathSim tests with a pangram message [K2MO compilation, [full]](https://www.qsl.net/sterling/Activities/20120307_JT65-HF/Band_Info.pdf). Assuming a 3 kHz SNR reference:

| Mode | AWGN error-free threshold | Rate | `Eb/N₀` [derived] |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MFSK16 | −14 dB | 31.25 bit/s | 5.8 dB |
| PSK10 | −18 dB | 10 bit/s | 6.8 dB |
| THOR11 | −14 dB | 21.5 bit/s | 7.4 dB |
| DominoEX-4 (no FEC) | −15 dB | 15.6 bit/s | 7.8 dB |
| Contestia 500/32 | −15 dB | 14.6 bit/s | 8.1 dB |
| PSK31 | −10 dB | 31 bit/s | 9.8 dB |
| Olivia 500/16 | −12 dB | 13.7 bit/s | 11.4 dB |

On the F.1487 high-latitude moderate channel (3 ms, 10 Hz) at −8 dB, Olivia, Contestia, MFSK16/31 and RTTYM copied 100%. Chip64/128, DominoEX, THOR, all PSK modes and MT63 gave no copy, and even at +10 dB DominoEX, THOR, PSK and MT63 failed while Chip64 copied 90%. On the high-latitude disturbed channel (7 ms, 30 Hz) at −3 dB, Olivia and Contestia copied 100%, MFSK16 75% and Chip64 10%. This is a single-source, single-message comparison, but its ranking under spread is unambiguous: wide-tone MFSK survives and narrow-tone, differential or chip-rate designs fail. FreeDV's OFDM data modes reach 90% of packets at −4 dB on a 1 Hz, 2 ms channel, about 11–13 dB `Eb/N₀` [codec2 repository, `README_data.md`, [full]](https://github.com/drowe67/codec2). No independent ROS, JS8 or SuperFox weak-signal measurements were found.

**Low-power NVIS.** On a 97 km NVIS path with 2–2.75 ms delay spread, uncoded 4/8/16-FSK was the worst of the tested modulations at every `Eb/N₀`, and the authors recommend 4-QAM at low power and OFDM in high multipath [Maso et al., *Sensors* 20:6232, 2020, [full]](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20216232). The FSK symbols were about 0.5 ms long, comparable to the delay spread, uncoded and evaluated at high SNR. A companion Antarctic sensing study also favors QAM [Porté et al., Radio Science 2020, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019RS006920). These results are relevant to short-symbol, moderate-SNR sensor links and do not test long-symbol weak-signal MFSK.

## Receiver computation

### Tone metrics

- **The weighted periodogram is the correct long-observation receiver, and it is cheap.** In the long-observation limit the optimum receiver for a Gaussian Doppler-spread signal is a filter-squarer-integrator with power response proportional to `S(f)/(S(f)+N₀)` [Van Trees, *Detection, Estimation and Modulation Theory, Part III*, chs. 4 and 11, [from memory]; Kailath, IEEE TIT 6(3):361–366, 1960, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1960.1057563). A thesis on doubly spread channels found filter-squarer-integrator and sampled-correlator receivers within about 1–2 dB of optimum in several examples [MIT thesis, core.ac.uk 4407019, [abs]](https://core.ac.uk/download/4407019.pdf).
- **Weight errors are cheap; window errors are not.** In the low-energy-coherence limit, an unweighted box of half-width about 1.4σ over a Gaussian spectrum loses about 0.25 dB, and a Gaussian weight whose width is wrong by √2, 2 or 4 loses 0.13, 0.49 or 1.64 dB of required power [derived, deflection]. The [new mismatch matrix](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#robustness-to-unknown-spread) gives larger numbers because it reports the rate (time) gap, which in this regime is about twice the dB of the power gap. The two agree after that conversion: a 3× spread error costs about 2.2 dB of transmit time, or about 1.1 dB of power.
- **A power-law detector avoids explicit spread hypotheses.** Nuttall's `Σ|X_k|^ν` with `ν ≈ 2.4–2.5` is reported near optimum when the occupied bins are unknown [Nuttall, “Detection performance of power-law processors for random signals of unknown location, structure, extent, and strength,” NUWC-NPT TR 10,751, 1994, DTIC ADA285868, [abs]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA285868.pdf); a companion report treats generalized likelihood-ratio processors [NUWC-NPT TR 10,739, DTIC ADA285589, [abs]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA285589.pdf). No exact dB figure was found, and it is sensitive to interfering tones.

### Drift and frequency-rate search

| Method | Cost | Sensitivity | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brute-force drift trials | `N_f·N_t·N_d` | exact | hyperseti and BLISS documentation [full] |
| Taylor tree (turboSETI) | `N_f·N_t·log₂N_t` | mean loss 1.4 dB at 512 rows, 1.8 dB (worst 2.5 dB) at 4096 rows | [Margot et al., AJ 161:55, 2021, Table 4, [full]](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/abcc77) |
| FDMT | `≤ 2N_f·N_t + N_t·N_Δ·log₂N_f` | brute-force sensitivity for curve families with one curve per point pair | [Zackay and Ofek, ApJ 835:11, 2017, § 4, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373) |
| Fast folding (periodic signals) | `O(b·N·log N)` | 0.93 search efficiency versus 0.70 for FFT plus harmonic summing | [Morello et al., MNRAS 497:4654, 2020, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701) |

GPU turboSETI ran 6.8–10.6× faster than CPU, with dedoppler and hit search taking about 90% of runtime [Cruz, Farah and Elkins, “Search for extraterrestrial intelligence: GPU accelerated TurboSETI,” SciPy 2022, [full]](https://doi.org/10.25080/majora-212e5952-004). GPU Fourier-domain acceleration search spent about 42% in cuFFT, 20% in complex multiply and 35% in power spectra and was memory-bandwidth limited [Dimoudi et al., ApJS 239:28, 2018, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05335). The tree's 1.4–2.5 dB loss is a fixed sensitivity penalty that a GPU can avoid with brute-force or FDMT-style exact search. Prix and Shaltev give a fixed-compute optimization of segment length, mismatch and grid spacing for semi-coherent searches [PRD 85:084010, 2012, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4321), which is the right framework for choosing Matrix coherent segment length against drift-grid density.

### GPU transforms and tensor cores

- **FFTs are bandwidth-bound.** TurboFFT reaches about 90% of A100 memory bandwidth in FP32 [arXiv 2412.05824, [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05824). tcFFT's FP16 tensor-core FFT is 1.24–1.90× faster than cuFFT FP16 on average for 1D and up to 3× for 2D, with similar accuracy [Li, Cheng and Lin, arXiv 2104.11471, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11471). FlashFFTConv reaches 56–62% FLOP utilization only by fusing the convolution chain [Fu et al., arXiv 2311.05908, Table 6, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05908).
- **High tensor utilization needs large batched products.** The Tensor-Core Beamformer reaches 50–85% of peak on large matrices and becomes memory-bound for small ones (for example 173 TOPS FP16 on A100, 55% of peak) [Oostrum et al., IPDPS 2025, Table III, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03269).
- **Four-bit tensor paths are not universal.** Hopper INT4 `mma` compiles to CUDA-core IMAD instructions [Luo et al., arXiv 2402.13499, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13499). The RTX Blackwell GB202 appendix lists FP4, FP8, FP16 and INT8 rates but no INT4 row. These agree with the [accelerator review](int4-and-accelerators.md).
- **[derived] Fused GEMM-DFT break-even on RTX 5090 at 8 bytes/point traffic**: a direct DFT stays under memory time to about N = 120, a two-factor Monarch DFT to about 3,000 and a three-factor to about 60,000. Tensor-core DFT pays off only when windowing, transform, power, spread weighting and drift accumulation stay on-chip.

### Quantization

At Nyquist sampling of Gaussian input, quantization efficiency is 0.637, 0.881, 0.963 and 0.988 for 1, 2, 3 and 4 bits; 4 bits loses about 0.05 dB [Price, arXiv 1607.03579, Table 1, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03579). With a strong narrowband interferer before channel isolation and block-16 scaling, the reviewer's model gives NVFP4 losses of 0.08, 0.47, 3.4 and 10.2 dB at interference-to-noise ratios of 0, 10, 20 and 30 dB, INT4 0.06–6.3 dB over the same range, and INT8 0.04 dB at 30 dB [derived]. This supports the existing rule of qualifying four-bit arithmetic only after Helper/DATA isolation ([precision guide](int4-and-accelerators.md#accumulator-range-and-the-meaning-of-only-int4)).

### Other waveform receivers

- **Chirp spread spectrum** demodulation is a dechirp and an FFT, the same cost order as MFSK. Up- and down-chirp preambles resolve the coupled time and frequency offsets. LoRa SF12 failed under dynamic Doppler on low-orbit passes [Doroshkin et al., IEEE Access 7:75721–75731, 2019, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2919274), an example of long chirps being drift-sensitive.
- **Walsh–Hadamard receivers** need only additions, but on GPUs tensor-core Hadamard kernels gain only 1.0–1.4× over a good CUDA-core implementation [HadaCore, arXiv 2412.08832, [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08832). Walsh symbols require phase coherence and are not eigenfunctions of a stationary Doppler-spread channel; complex exponentials are.
- **Multiple-symbol differential detection** has fast exact algorithms (Mackenthun's `O(N log N)` [IEEE TCOM 42(2/3/4), 1994, [abs]](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOMM.1994.582823); sphere-decoding variants by Lampe, Schober, Pauli and coauthors [abs]). They pay off only when phase memory spans several symbols.

## What this evidence does and does not establish

It establishes that the receiver form the theory points to, a spread-weighted tone energy with a nonbinary code, is already used successfully in the field, and that robust professional waveforms are orthogonal-signaling cousins of MFSK. It establishes that every large measured gain over a given MFSK system came from coding, soft metrics, longer coherent integration, symbol length matched to the channel, or a smaller hypothesis space, not from abandoning orthogonal signaling. It establishes that tensor-core speedups for transforms are modest unless the processing chain is fused and batched.

It does not establish Matrix Modem's sensitivity, acquisition performance or compute ceiling. Most thresholds above come from different message lengths, success criteria, sync overheads and channel simulators. The Q65 spread comparison uses an approximate branch model. The PathSim ranking is a single-source test. No matched 2018–2026 low-SNR HF study of MFSK against chirp, OTFS or DSSS was found. A coherent or partially coherent scheme could still win on a stable or strongly specular path, as [the specular rows of the bound tables](fading-limit-and-mfsk-alternatives.md#where-the-real-channel-can-be-better) show, and a better receiver than those surveyed may exist.
