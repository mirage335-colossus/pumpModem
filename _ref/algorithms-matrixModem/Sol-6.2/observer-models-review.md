# Observer models, detectability, and repeated structure


## Decision

The Matrix Modem hypothesis that sparse FSK plus keyed frequency hopping can be difficult to recognize at the intended receiver's noise floor is plausible for some observers and conditions. It is not established by encryption, by a nominally wide hop set, or by measuring only a wideband energy detector. The answer depends on the received waveform distribution under signal-present and signal-absent cases, an observer's antennas and bandwidth, what protocol details it knows, its record length, and the false-alarm rate it must sustain.

Repeated symbols can help the intended receiver combine weak evidence across channel evolution. The same periodicity, tone grid, hop cadence, preamble, quiet-slot timing, or shared helper can help an observer detect presence without recovering the key or plaintext. More candidate tones can dilute energy in individual frequency bins but also enlarge the observer's frequency search and can make a wideband power test more effective. No monotonic relationship between tone count, repetition count, and detectability follows without fixing the observer and total signal budget.

Keep four outcomes distinct in every experiment: decoded content, signal-presence detection, modulation or timing recognition, and interference to a particular service. LPI/LPD is a statistical detection claim about a named observer and observation interval. It is not message confidentiality, and it is not a synonym for inaudibility or harmless interference.

## What the existing Matrix calculation demonstrates

The Opus-5.5 `lpi_observer.cpp` is a useful analysis-only toy study. Its received record has 352 complex dimensions per 160 ms symbol (2200 Hz × 0.16 s). It allocates the energy among `M_obs = 352/G` equal tiles and assumes one Rayleigh-faded tile is occupied per symbol. It compares (1) a total-energy radiometer with known noise level, (2) an LRT that knows signal strength, tile count and channel law, (3) an LRT after per-symbol normalization, and (4) a keyed receiver's presence score over 64 candidate tone dimensions. Required numbers of independent symbols are extrapolated from Monte Carlo statistic means and variances using a normal approximation at nominal `P_FA=10^-3`, `P_D=0.9`. See the [program](../../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/calc/lpi_observer.cpp), [stored output](../../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/calc/results/lpi_observer.md), and the prior [observer chapter](../../../_ref/algorithms-matrixModem/Opus-5.5/06-observer-lpi-and-interference-perception.md).

This establishes detector behavior only inside that model. It usefully illustrates that, under its assumptions, an observer with the correct tile family can collect evidence differently from a radiometer, and that spreading energy over more dimensions can change a particular position-aware statistic. It does not simulate an RF observer, measured HF noise, an actual Matrix waveform, a cryptographic schedule, a signal's full temporal dependence, a field receiver, or GPU execution. Its idealized channel is a fresh independent flat Rayleigh fade each symbol, with matched noise tiles and no carrier or timing search.

Several table details need to travel with any use of the numerical values:

- The radiometer row uses known noise and a Gaussian/CLT-style statistic calculation. It is not a test with a learned noise distribution, nonstationary noise, colored interference, clipping, antenna diversity, or an hourly false-alarm calibration.
- The position-aware observer is especially informed. It is told the tone/tile geometry, `G`, the symbol duration, mean per-symbol signal-to-noise ratio, and Rayleigh observation model. It does not search uncertain offsets or infer those parameters. Its results are not a generic “unkeyed observer.”
- The normalized observer is one chosen normalization and one chosen statistic, not an optimal detector with unknown nuisance parameters. Where its mean-shift estimate is unresolved by the run's 400,000-symbol Monte Carlo, the program emits `inf`. That means the toy estimator did not resolve the shift at its sampling budget. It is not a proof of information-theoretic undetectability.
- The keyed “presence” score and 128-bit message-duration column describe different tasks and search spaces. One uses 64 candidate dimensions while the observer's tile grid can have up to 352 positions. The 128-bit duration uses idealized symbol-wise mutual information for 64-ary noncoherent orthogonal signaling. This ratio is an illustrative comparison, not a measured intercept-to-decode ratio for matched waveform and hardware.
- The observer trials sample one idealized statistical law. The printed normal-approximation crossing has no confidence interval and does not validate tail false alarms at the specified rate. Repeatedly evaluating many times, frequency offsets and detector families would add multiplicity that the toy calculation does not charge.
- The SNR-wall table is a formula applied to assumed bounded noise uncertainty. It does not estimate uncertainty at a real site, and it applies to the specified energy-detection model. A detector can exploit structure that an energy-only test ignores.

These limitations do not invalidate the calculation. They delimit it. Treat its tables as hypothesis-generating references and as candidate regression checks for the same synthetic model. Do not use them as Matrix field LPI claims or as a basis for selecting a waveform without paired, held-out observer experiments.

## Literature that changes the interpretation

The classic square-root law is a useful warning against assuming that cryptography alone gives stealth. In the discrete-time AWGN setting studied by Bash, Goeckel and Towsley, with fixed known channel/noise statistics and a warden testing signal absence, the amount of reliable covert communication scales on the order of the square root of the number of channel uses under their covertness metric. The constant and exact statement depend on the channel, constraints, and metric. This asymptotic theorem is not a detection-time estimate for a particular finite FSK transmission over HF. [Bash, Goeckel & Towsley, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2013.130923), [UMass manuscript](https://web.cs.umass.edu/publication/docs/2012/UM-CS-2012-003.pdf).

Covert-channel results depend strongly on what the warden knows and observes. Noise uncertainty can permit positive rates in some discrete-time models when the uncertainty is genuinely unavailable to the warden. He et al. explicitly distinguish uncertainty assumptions and detector choice. A 2024 multi-antenna result shows a counterexample to treating a radiometer-only benefit as robust: in its AWGN model, a correlation detector using two or more antennas recovers square-root scaling despite noise uncertainty, without knowing channel coefficients or the transmitter codebook. Conversely, these are mathematical models and do not show that any real HF warden has a calibrated multi-antenna receiver or a matching channel. [He et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2016.2647716), [Chen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/LWC.2023.3323764).

Bouette, Luzzi and Wang (2025) bound the square-root scaling constant for general memoryless additive noise under stated integrability conditions, with a tightness result under additional assumptions. The intended receiver and observer share the same channel output in their model. Their companion work treats Gaussian memory and generalized Gaussian noise under specified models. These results caution against casually assuming a non-Gaussian noise floor automatically creates linear covert throughput. Real atmospheric and man-made HF noise is colored, impulsive, nonstationary, and receiver/site dependent, so the memoryless theorem must not be presented as its exact characterization. [IEEE journal article](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2024.3522151), [2023 additive-noise memory paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11697).

Discrete-time noise-uncertainty intuition also does not transfer automatically to long continuous-time observations. Bash, Towsley and Goeckel analyze continuous-time covert communication directly. The later environmental-uncertainty paper reports a detector that restores square-root scaling over a continuous-time channel in its setting, even where discrete-time uncertainty arguments suggest positive-rate transmission. It is an IEEE conference paper whose public record/abstract was inspected for this review. A 2025 paper proves square-root results for a classical-quantum channel, a different observation model. The collective lesson is not that one slogan settles the question, but that the signal-present distribution and the observer's actual observation model are decisive. [Bash, Towsley & Goeckel, *Covert Communication in Continuous-Time Systems*, arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06932), [environmental uncertainty, IEEE record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10476936), [Bullock et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2025.3537970).

Signal interception literature has long explained why energy detection is not the only plausible observer. Gardner's spectral-correlation framework shows how modulation, symbol timing, pulse trains, and repeating structures can regenerate cyclostationary features that distinguish signals from stationary noise, including when noise power changes. It also explains that random modulation can smear visible spectral lines while retaining cyclic features. More recent detector work notes that cyclic-frequency and period knowledge helps performance, and that blindly searching those unknown quantities is additional complexity. This supports both sides of the Matrix question: randomized key schedules can conceal simple fixed patterns, while residual regular timing or a publicly known symbol rate may remain detectable. [Gardner, 1988](https://doi.org/10.1109/26.3769), [author-hosted full text](https://cyclostationarity.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/202210141144.pdf), [Wang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1049/cmu2.12282).

## Implications for FSK, repetition and hopping

**A cryptographic keystream protects meaning only to the extent of its cryptographic design and key handling.** It can make the transmitted symbol sequence unpredictable to an observer without the key. It does not remove total received energy, the symbol clock, occupied bandwidth, a regular preamble, transition statistics, a repeated encrypted sequence, or other features of the waveform distribution. If the same stream-cipher keystream is reused with different plaintexts, ciphertext relationships can also leak information. Nonce/key uniqueness is a cryptographic requirement, separate from RF detectability.

**A sparse tone can be hard for a broad radiometer and easy for a narrow search under different conditions.** For total received power fixed across bandwidth `B`, a calibrated wideband energy detector's signal-to-noise contrast becomes smaller as it integrates more noise dimensions. A channelized observer may instead test many narrow bins or the known tone/hop family, accumulating energy in selected cells. That search must pay a family-wise false-alarm cost over frequencies, timing, offsets, rates, and detector variants. Fixed peak tone power is a different normalization from fixed total power or fixed energy per information bit. Every LPI curve should state which is held fixed.

**Frequency hopping changes occupancy and structure, not conservation of energy.** It may reduce persistence in a single narrow channel, avoid a persistent interferer, and decorrelate observations if the hop separation actually spans independent fading. It can also produce a recognizable dwell cadence or broaden the spectrum available to a wideband detector. A secret schedule obstructs keyless sequence correlation, but it is not needed for a total-energy test. If the schedule or zero-key profile is public, an informed observer should be treated as knowing it.

**Repetition is a receiver-diversity and observer-detection trade.** If `K` repeats share a channel that remains stable enough, a receiver can combine repeated matched-filter or tone-energy evidence. If the channel changes, noncoherent combining may retain evidence without carrier-phase coherence, but a deep correlated fade can still erase all repeats. A receiver-side matched template made from its own recent noisy history is not a free noiseless reference. Its noise is correlated with the matched decision statistic and can bias or raise false alarms. Compare it against known-template noncoherent integration, differential detection, and channel-state tracking at matched total energy and latency.

The observer sees repeated cadence even when each symbol value is secret. When the *same waveform segment* repeats, a delay-autocorrelation or cyclic statistic can reveal the period without knowing the data. When the symbols are independently remasked on each repeat, this particular statistic may disappear, although symbol timing and spectral occupancy can remain. If every repeat carries fresh energy, total energy and airtime grow with repeat count. If total message energy is fixed, energy per repeat falls. These are different comparisons and may yield different receiver and observer operating points. The claim that several repetitions preserve LPI while multiplying receiver work little therefore needs a fixed total transmit-energy, airtime, reliability and observer false-alarm comparison.

**More candidate tones can help a receiver while hurting or helping an observer depending on what is fixed.** At fixed tone amplitude, adding tones or repeats can increase total radiated energy. At fixed total energy, adding choices can lower energy per symbol hypothesis and complicate receiver acquisition, but an observer that knows the tone family can exploit the structured alternatives. A larger publicly fixed grid expands blind search and its false-alarm burden. It does not increase physical entropy if frequencies are correlated across the actual multipath channel, nor does it create frequency diversity when the delay profile leaves the band flat. Treat tone count as a variable in paired ROC and receiver-completion tests rather than asserting that its LPI benefit is monotonic.

## Observer suite and comparison method

The intended experiment should answer a bounded, operational question: for named observer locations and receivers, how long does each detector require to reach a specified detection probability at a specified false-alarm budget, after the candidate waveform traverses the measured/replayed path and the same captured noise episode? Report the intended receiver's complete-message success separately. A receiver-site observer is a valuable common-path condition, but a nearby, better-sited or multi-antenna observer has a different channel and noise floor.

For a staged simulation and capture study, compare at least:

1. Wideband and subband radiometers with calibrated and estimated noise, at several observer bandwidths.
2. Channelized tone/occupancy detectors and the strongest reasonable keyless likelihood or generalized-likelihood search over the published waveform family, timing and frequency uncertainty.
3. Cyclic/timing detectors using symbol rate, hop cadence, repeated block period, preamble, and other publicly known features. Charge the additional frequency/rate search and threshold multiplicity.
4. A schedule-aware matched detector for a public schedule, plus an explicitly informed key-holder-equivalent detector as an upper-observer reference for a secret-key profile. Do not equate a finite brute-force key search with an information-theoretic optimum.
5. For a realistic qualification set, more than one antenna/receiver bandwidth where available, ambient HF/noise recordings at the receive site, and separately selected nearby or alternate-path observer records.

Use paired `H0` and `H1` arms built from the same held-out noise samples. Label whether signal addition occurs digitally, through a validated channel replay, by RF loopback, or over the air. Calibrate thresholds on different time/site/event episodes than those used to estimate the ROC. Use full independent episodes as statistical units, not overlapping windows as independent trials. Report `P_D(T)`, family-wise `P_FA` per hour or per observation interval, confidence intervals, censored runs, receiver operating point, noise uncertainty, received power/energy, exact detector knowledge, search dimensions, and compute budget. Preserve both the detector-level results and nuisance-condition labels. A simulation with Gaussian noise does not estimate a field noise tail.

Include the actual spectrum and transitions after transmitter/audio/RF filtering. A fixed helper or acquisition tone is part of the emission and may become a strong observer feature. Include preambles, pilots, message framing, quiet intervals and retransmissions in energy/time accounting. Evaluate intended receiver sensitivity, signal presence, decoding, and service interference under the same captured episodes but with separate metrics.

No finite detector suite proves no more capable observer exists. The empirical ROC is evidence about those implemented observers on the retained held-out population. The optimal likelihood-ratio test is a theoretical reference only when the distributions are known and its assumptions actually hold. Do not label a result an absolute LPI guarantee.
