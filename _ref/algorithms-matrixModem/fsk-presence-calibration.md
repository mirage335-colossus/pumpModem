# Comparing FSK scores and deciding when a message ends

The FSK draft's proposed false-presence target concerns **one complete public block decision**. A search of 1,024 messages, several window lengths and several alignments still yields one decision. A block carrying the wanted signal can produce a miss, an accepted wrong payload, or a correct payload. All three outcomes matter. A detector can have the requested false-presence rate and almost always miss a class of valid signals. The finite example below shows how a seemingly harmless comparison in the receiver does exactly this. The [original proposal](../../_appraisal/matrixModem-FSK.md), [earlier receiver review](Sol-6.2/receiver-and-evaluation-review.md) and [reproducible calculations](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/presence-calibration/README.md) give the context.

## One calibrated threshold can still disable the per-tone fallback

Consider one fixed message and alignment in complex AWGN. Write the selected tone measurements as independent `z_i ~ CN(μ_i,1)`, so unit-mean exponential power is the null. Over 64 tones compare the whole-window energy `C = |Σ z_i|²` and the per-tone energy `D = Σ |z_i|²`. Both null means are 64. Their shapes differ strongly. Under the null, `C/64` is unit exponential and `D` is unit-scale Gamma with shape 64. Both statistics use the same observations.

For a common raw threshold on `max(C,D)` at family false-alarm probability ≤10⁻⁶, the whole-window tail alone requires a threshold of at least 884.19. Now give successive tones alternating received means +1 and −1, after the known transmit phase has been removed. This is a constructed received phase sequence with unit energy per tone. The coherent sum cancels exactly. The tail of C is unchanged, and D has mean 128. Its moment generating function gives `Pr(D ≥ t) ≤ exp(−t/2 + 64 ln 2 + 64)` at this energy. The resulting bound on detection probability for the raw maximum is **10⁻⁶ + 1.15 × 10⁻¹⁴⁵** (rounded). An accurately estimated common raw threshold would reproduce the failure.

Allocate 5 × 10⁻⁷ to each branch and use its own null quantile. The threshold for D becomes 111.03. Its noncentral Gamma tail, evaluated by a Poisson mixture, gives **at least 89.32% detection** for the union of the same two branches. The sum of the branch budgets bounds the family false-alarm probability for any dependence between them. This comparison establishes a failure mechanism with one template, two unquantized rungs and known noise scale. It gives no operating sensitivity for a searched modem. The [earlier ladder simulation](../../_appraisal/algorithms-matrixModem/sub9-fsk-rate/coherent_block_mc.cpp) already ranks rungs using a Gamma survival probability for their winning message. It measures message selection with signal present and supplies no calibration of the maximum across messages on absent blocks.

For a general ladder, identical independent complex noise and equal windows of length L give `S/L ~ Gamma(number of windows,1)` per fixed template. Shared bins, estimated scale, erased samples, quantization, tapering, unequal lengths and colored residuals change this law. Candidate designs can reserve a fixed share of the block error budget for the per-tone branch and spend the remainder across other attempts. Alternatively, transform each branch to a comparable tail score including its search and freeze a threshold on the resulting joint maximum. Characterize selection among simultaneously accepted, conflicting payloads as well as union detection. The gain of either scheme must be checked on the actual search. A per-tone acceptance can still be displaced by a wrong coherent candidate.

Two other claims in the proposal depended on the type of score. If an additive per-tone score lies in [0,b], replacing one term changes a fixed-template sum by at most b. If each complex bin is bounded in magnitude by b, adding one aligned bin to N−1 aligned bins changes their squared coherent sum by (2N−1)b². Preprocessing that refits common scale or masks can also change other terms. Adding a new observation can only improve the optimum *achievable* risk under a known joint model with the option to ignore it. A prescribed mismatched or equally weighted score has no such guarantee.

## Noise reference and wrong-key calibration

In the idealized single-bin power test, a known mean of one gives threshold `t = −ln(10⁻⁶)`. If the receiver substitutes the median of n nearby independent exponential powers divided by ln 2 for that mean, the threshold itself is random. For odd n and order k = (n+1)/2, the false-alarm probability is the Laplace transform of that order statistic. Independence of exponential order-statistic spacings gives

```text
Pr(test power > t × median / ln 2) = ∏[j=n−k+1…n] j/(j + t/(r ln 2)),
```

where r is the mean test-bin power divided by the mean reference-bin power. Some closed-form examples follow.

| Independent reference bins | Matched power, r = 1 | Test bin 3.01 dB hotter, r = 2 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 31 | 4.297 × 10⁻⁵ | 2.965 × 10⁻³ |
| 63 | 9.798 × 10⁻⁶ | 1.840 × 10⁻³ |

These are single-bin calculations using raw independent references. They establish why an operational calibration must include the actual quantile, placement, guard bins, clipping, dependence and filter state. A local scale estimate is still useful. Calibrating the complete statistic on a matched distribution incorporates its randomness.

Use distinct null controls for physically quiet thermal noise, blank intervals with structured interference, and actual wrong-key/wrong-ordinal transmissions at specified powers. A mismatched position in an ideal P-position example coincides with an independent transmitting position with probability 1/P. If the transmitted energy in its bin is E, the mean wrong-template bin energy before conditioning is 1 + E/P, even when thermal noise has mean one. A strong wrong-key transmission therefore need not produce the thermal null. With multiple messages, maximizing over them also favors accidental matches. Reusing a physical observation with several decoy schedules can probe this conditional behavior at low cost.

If the wanted and D decoy **whole-block scores** are exchangeable under a declared null, a conservative upper-tail rank p-value is `(1 + number of decoys at least as large as wanted)/(D + 1)`. Counting ties matters with discrete scores. The smallest nonrandomized value is 1/(D+1). Resolving 10⁻⁶ by this particular rule requires at least 999,999 decoys. That number is a resolution calculation, not a requirement to run a million decodes per block. An ensemble with a uniformly random address can justify a different statement than a fixed published address on one interference pattern. Shared observations do not by themselves invalidate a rank test, and do not confer exchangeability under each operational null. Hemerik and Goeman give the invariance conditions behind exact random permutation tests. Bates et al. distinguish marginal rank validity from guarantees conditional on calibration data. Gibbs, Cherian and Candès show how stronger conditional coverage for specified groups needs additional structure. Their settings explain the limits of a rank argument here. None supplies a modem-specific null. [Hemerik and Goeman (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11749-017-0571-1) · [Bates et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279) · [Gibbs et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12616).

One affordable route is to derive conservative family bounds for a stated noise model, then exercise a relaxed, measurable false rate on held-out complete receiver episodes through all admission and selection steps. Exact marginal bounds with a union budget do not require independent hypotheses. They do require valid single-attempt tails after every data-dependent operation. Model-based numbers should carry their noise law. For comparison, zero exceedances of a *frozen* threshold in n independent, identically distributed null blocks gives a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound of `1−0.05^(1/n)`. It takes 2,995,731 such blocks to reach 10⁻⁶ by that particular demonstration. Reuse the frozen threshold on independent new blocks. Multiple keys, overlapping alignments and scores from the same received episode do not multiply the number of independent operating episodes. The count explains the value of proving what is tractable and reporting the attainable holdout bound. [Prior episode-level validation guidance](../highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/Fable-5.2/86-statistics-and-calculation-checks.md).

## Two consecutive quiet decisions

Continue the public calendar and decide each observed slot. Confirm completion only after two consecutive physically scored absences. Erasure, missing input and resource exhaustion supply no evidence of physical quiet. Test resumed transmission after an interior fade and continue through the channel and receiver filter tails after the final payload. Score exact payload and completion by a stated horizon. A deep enough fade of a still-transmitting source really can yield receiver observations indistinguishable from quiet. [Physical-completion contract](../../docs/robust-manual-capacity.md#physical-completion-and-security).

For stationary missed active blocks with marginal rate p and conditional persistence `q = Pr(next miss | miss)`, a particular adjacent pair is missed with probability pq. At p = 10⁻³ and q = 0.5 the pair rate is 5 × 10⁻⁴. Independent misses with the same marginal rate give 10⁻⁶. The finite calculation also evaluates a stationary two-state Markov example across 100 continuously active blocks. The probability of any adjacent missed pair is 2.47% for q = 0.5 versus 0.00989% for independent misses. The illustrated q values describe constructed persistence, without a claim of measured ionospheric statistics. Hold out whole channel histories when testing premature completion. Report false presence on genuinely absent slots, failure to detect an active slot, accepted wrong payload, false end during a source message, and delayed or missing end after the source stops.
