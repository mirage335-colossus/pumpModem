# Noise, interference and receiver front ends in high-fidelity weak-signal experiments

Independent research review prepared 13 November 2026. Scope: background noise and interfering signals, measurement antennas, receiver/front-end limits, and discriminating validation for the proposed weak-signal experiments: a top-loaded 200 ft (60.96 m) vertical and loop alternatives below 9 kHz; ground-wave tests near 1 MHz and 30 MHz; HF sky-wave work from 1–30 MHz; and 5.8 GHz Earth–Moon–Earth (EME). This is literature review and experiment design, not a finding about a particular radio, site, antenna installation or modem. No local source or test was changed or run.

## Findings

There is no single “noise floor” that describes these experiments. The observed voltage at the receiver is the combination of the field and polarization selected by the antenna, propagation from each source, antenna and feed losses, local man-made emissions, desired and undesired transmissions, receiver-added noise, and nonlinear or quantization artifacts. The available power of a noisy field is antenna-dependent; a field-strength number and a receiver-input dBm number are not interchangeable without a calibrated antenna factor, polarization/pattern, impedance and bandwidth. For weak-signal work, measurement setup and receiver overload can produce a larger error than the particular stochastic noise law.

The latest in-force ITU-R background-noise recommendation found is **P.372-18, approved 8 September 2026**. It is the appropriate current reference for broadband background noise, but it is a planning model, not a local observation or a complete waveform generator. New empirical work exposes important scope gaps: a 2019 Dutch HF campaign measured city and residential man-made noise above P.372-13 expectations, while an Ofcom campaign completed in 2026 found the indoor RF environment above 400 MHz to depend strongly on nearby active devices, location, and time. These are not contradictory measurements: they cover different bands and populations, and support replacing coarse “rural/city” labels with site, source, band, and time records.

Atmospheric noise is not universally white or Gaussian. Lightning produces impulsive, temporally structured emissions; their contribution depends on source geography, propagation, local time, season, and ionospheric state. A site-specific VLF example, with quantitative findings and limits, is summarized in the [sub-9 kHz antenna chapter](10-sub9khz-antenna.md); it should not be generalized as a global radius or level. Man-made emissions likewise mix persistent spectral lines, switching-related components, bursts, and aggregate backgrounds. A single fitted distribution cannot represent all of these mechanisms across the entire 3–30 MHz band.

For controlled tests, use model families as alternatives to test, not as an oracle: measured empirical amplitude-probability distributions (APDs), time/frequency structure and cross-channel correlation first; Gaussian plus impulsive/compound-Poisson or Middleton-like components where data supports event arrivals; and alpha-stable fits only as a candidate approximation to observed tails. An ideal symmetric alpha-stable law with exponent below two has infinite variance. Actual captured, bandwidth-limited, finite-duration voltages do not have infinite measured energy; do not use unbounded heavy-tail samples as a literal source model or claim a physical infinite-variance radio environment. Tail probabilities, truncation, event rate, pulse shape and bandwidth must be stated and measured for the intended receiver bandwidth. Do not call a p99 value universal: report quantiles with their site, antenna, time interval, bandwidth, estimator and uncertainty.

Strong blockers make a weak-signal front end fail in ways additive noise cannot reproduce. Compression, intermodulation, reciprocal mixing of a blocker with local-oscillator phase noise, ADC clipping and AGC action may raise the in-band floor or remove the desired signal. AGC can stabilize ADC level but cannot restore instantaneous dynamic range already consumed by simultaneous strong and weak signals. Demonstrating a Gaussian AWGN receiver simulation does not rule out these effects. Conversely, in an exceptionally quiet band an inadequate LNA, lossy feed, or quantizer can make the instrument floor dominate, falsely attributing receiver noise to the antenna environment.

At 5.8 GHz, do not carry HF atmospheric/man-made noise assumptions forward. The sky contribution is direction- and beam-dependent; the Sun and a few compact bright sources matter under some pointings, while the 2.7 K cosmic background is a baseline rather than the whole system temperature. C-BASS measurements at 4.76–5 GHz directly detect diffuse Galactic synchrotron and show it has measurable spatial structure. EME also sees a warm lunar disk within the antenna beam and terrestrial/atmospheric spillover; those are useful radiometric calibration scenes, but the lunar brightness and antenna temperature are distinct quantities. A result at another microwave frequency cannot be silently substituted for the 5.8 GHz beam-convolved lunar contribution.

## Domain implications and candidate measurement model

### Below 9 kHz: tall top-loaded vertical versus ground loop

At 9 kHz the free-space wavelength is about 33 km; a 60.96 m radiator is electrically short. The top load changes capacitance and current distribution, but does not make an ideal short monopole. Response depends on top-load geometry, ground/counterpoise, feedpoint, loading network, conductor loss, common-mode feedline current, nearby structures, and electric/magnetic coupling. A loop has an orientation null; mains wiring, lightning, switching supplies and ground currents can couple differently to loop and vertical. A quiet reading on one loop axis is not a globally quiet field.

Antenna comparisons require a common calibration plane, known impedance and bandwidth, and sensitivity to direction and common-mode coupling. The [appraisal procedures](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/experiment-protocol.md#band-specific-measurement-controls) distinguish these causes.

Model the sub-9 kHz record as receiver/thermal floor, observed mains-synchronous tones/harmonics, local periodic or burst interference, and lightning transients/tails. Preserve waveform segments to check pulse timing, cross-polarization and envelope tails. A Gaussian baseline may approximate inter-pulse aggregate, but should not erase pulse timing or imply independent samples. No source justifies a universal p99, pulse rate or alpha-stable exponent for a 3–9 kHz installation.

### 1 MHz and 30 MHz ground wave; 1–30 MHz sky wave

At 1 MHz, nearby infrastructure/local devices can arrive by ground wave while distant lightning noise can arrive by ionospheric refraction. At 30 MHz the propagation mix changes; measure whether local/regional sources dominate rather than assuming a clean transition. HF atmospheric background varies with local time, season, storms, solar illumination and ionospheric absorption. Flares alter desired sky-wave paths and noise arriving by those paths, so apparent noise or reception changes can reflect path state rather than transmitter change.

The applicable noise population depends on site, band, time, antenna and propagation state. The [appraisal procedures](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/experiment-protocol.md#band-specific-measurement-controls) record these conditions and isolate local and distant sources.

Treat additive emissions separately from propagation: (y(t)=h(t)*s(t)+n_a(t)+i(t)+n_r(t)), where (h) is the propagation channel; (n_a) external ambient noise; (i) structured/co-channel or man-made interference; and (n_r) receiver-added noise and artifacts. This decomposition is an experiment bookkeeping model, not a claim that all terms are independent or stationary. Correlation across antenna orientations, bands, time, or a reference receiver can falsify independence. Frequency drift, multipath and fading belong in (h), not in a convenient “noise” term. A source may be both an interfering waveform and an impulsive burst; keep its measured temporal/spectral form rather than replacing it with AWGN.

### 5.8 GHz EME

At 5.8 GHz, budget receiver noise, feed/antenna loss, sky/atmosphere, ground spillover, Sun/bright sources, and Moon beam filling. The 2.7 K cosmic background is not the whole sky: C-BASS detects structured Galactic emission near 5 GHz. A [Sardinia C-band campaign](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24196481) found occupancy through 5.8 GHz; it reported no compression in its survey chain, but does not give blocker levels at an EME input. The Moon's brightness temperature is not its antenna-temperature increment, which depends on beam efficiency, disk solid angle, sidelobes and pointing. Measure the Moon against adjacent sky at unchanged gain; ground view provides another reference, while opacity changes with elevation. Record solar pointing and activity rather than labeling every short burst solar.

Clear-sky noise and the Moon-on-beam increment are different observables; a 3.2 GHz/9.6 cm lunar result is not a direct 5.8 GHz calibration. The [appraisal procedures](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/experiment-protocol.md#band-specific-measurement-controls) distinguish these terms from instrumental drift.

## Front-end model falsifiers

Calibrated bandwidth, impedance, gain and linearity are necessary to interpret the source of apparent noise. The [appraisal protocol](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/experiment-protocol.md#front-end-validation) contains the proposed measurement and control procedures.

Useful falsifiers are predeclared. The proposed source-class model is inadequate if held-out APDs or temporal correlations fall outside its uncertainty envelope; Gaussian-plus-Poisson is inadequate if the observed tail/event process has reproducible structure not captured by the fitted pulse process; a claimed local electronics source is unsupported if switching it off/on does not change the band-limited record beyond calibrated repeatability; a claimed antenna advantage is unsupported if it disappears after feed loss, antenna factor and input impedance are calibrated; and an AWGN-only receiver account is falsified if blocker-level changes cause in-band products or sensitivity shifts. Conversely, failure to observe such effects in one quiet, narrow-band session does not establish their absence at other sites, bandwidths or seasons.
