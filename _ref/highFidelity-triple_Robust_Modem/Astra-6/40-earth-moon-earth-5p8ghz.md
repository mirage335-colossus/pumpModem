# Earth–Moon–Earth at 5.8 GHz: a bounded physical channel model

**Purpose.** This chapter gathers propagation evidence for weak-signal Earth–Moon–Earth (EME) near 5.8 GHz. A terrestrial transmitter illuminates the Moon and a terrestrial receiver detects the scattered return; no active lunar relay is involved. This differs from an Earth–Moon–orbiter link, which has an active spacecraft terminal and different link equations.

The evidence does not support a single universal “lunar echo” impulse response. Center delay, disk support, echo-power weighting, Doppler center/spread, polarization, station separation, elevation, sky background and pointing vary across geometry and environment, with physical dependencies among them. They should be conditioned on the same geometry/state rather than treated as statistically independent by default. For a Robust Modem weak-signal simulation, keep these effects separable and assumptions traceable. The closest-frequency direct surface-radar example inspected here is EME07; classic whole-Moon measurements include 3.5 cm and longer wavelengths. Orbital radar adds useful scattering and roughness context, but its incidence and geometry differ from EME. No field-measured 5.8 GHz EME dataset was identified: any 5.8 GHz channel synthesized from these sources is a scaled model, not a measurement.

## Geometry, scattering, and time variation

### One bulk delay and a much shorter distributed echo

At mean geocentric distance R ~= 384,400 km, the center-to-center round-trip delay is

\[
\tau_c=\frac{2R}{c}\approx 2.564\ \mathrm{s}.
\]

Using the approximately 363,300–405,500 km perigee-to-apogee range gives roughly 2.42–2.71 s. These are geocentric bulk-delay scales, not exact terminal-to-scatterer light times: terminal positions, lunar surface range and motion during propagation must enter the latter. They are distinct from disk delay spread. A spherical Moon of radius a ~= 1,737.4 km has near-limb to subradar-point excess round-trip path about 2a/c ~= 11.59 ms. This is geometric support near-monostatically; for separated stations calculate the isodelay surface from their separate ranges.

The echo’s **effective power-delay profile is usually narrower than that geometric ceiling** because backscatter is nonuniform across delay rings. Near-normal facets contribute a quasi-specular central return; oblique annuli and wavelength-scale rocks add later diffuse power. Keep discrete bulk delay near 2.5 s and model the shorter disk response with a normalized profile. A uniform projected-disk profile is a baseline, not an empirical lunar PDP: it misses central quasi-specular weighting and regional reflectivity differences. The 11.57 ms geometric example at 440.2 MHz informs geometry; its Doppler figures are carrier-specific.

At 5.8 GHz, wavelength is 5.17 cm: near the 4 cm scattering models and 3.5 cm ground radar measurements, but not interchangeable with them. Together with EME07, these support a quasi-specular-plus-diffuse mixture and albedo of several percent, not an exact 5.8 GHz value or universal frequency law. A localized radar footprint is not a disk average; EME09’s S-band orbital data add a different incidence/processing context.

Use a two-component scattering hypothesis as the main physical candidate:

1. **Quasi-specular component:** an incidence-dependent narrow lobe around the specular/subradar region, with the Hagfors law as one established option. Its width is controlled by an effective, wavelength-scale roughness parameter and varies with subradar point/libration.
2. **Diffuse component:** a broader, lower-power return from rocks, rough facets, and possibly subsurface structure. Preserve polarization state or at least co-/cross-circular power fractions when the modem’s polarization is relevant.

Whole-Moon radar return is weak and wavelength-dependent, delay–Doppler maps show structure, and modern polarimetry relies on explicit regolith assumptions. Yet no validated frequency-matched map supports a deterministic 5.8 GHz surface model. A full facet renderer is optional, not the minimum credible channel.

### Bistatic geometry, frequency offset, and spectral spread

For separate stations, path length is transmitter-to-scatterer plus scatterer-to-receiver range; carrier propagation phase is -2*pi*path/wavelength. Define received Doppler as the phase derivative divided by 2*pi, so f_D = -(1/wavelength) d(path)/dt under this convention. Evaluate transmit and receive legs at their retarded epochs. Self-echo monostatic geometry reduces to round-trip Doppler 2*v_r/wavelength when v_r is positive toward the radar; interstation EME sums transmitter- and receiver-leg radial components. Do not use one-way satellite Doppler or a monostatic scalar for an interstation offset.

The **bulk Doppler center** changes with lunar transit, Earth rotation and station geometry, and scales linearly with carrier. At 5.8 GHz, projected surface speed 465 m/s gives about 9.0 kHz on one leg and up to 18 kHz two-way for same-station reflection at that limiting projection. Actual offset depends on ephemeris, elevation, latitude and station pair. Calculate search bounds from geometry; this acquisition offset is distinct from Doppler spread.

The disk has radial-velocity variation from rotation and libration. EME17 defines `w10`/`w50` through full widths of the echo-spectrum autocorrelation at 10%/50% levels; these are neither RMS widths nor exact cumulative-power quantiles. Reproduce the statistic when comparing simulations. For EME07’s unchanged radial-velocity field, frequency-only scaling by 5.8/7.209125 gives about 32/72 Hz. This is a conditional extrapolation: frequency-dependent scattering, footprint, libration and two-leg geometry change the spectrum.

The 2026 broadband EME paper’s 1.296 GHz link simulations emphasize delay spread and relatively slow Doppler, while Taylor’s narrowband EME treatment emphasizes libration-induced spectral broadening and fading. These results are not inherently contradictory: effective impairment depends on occupied bandwidth times delay spread (`B·τ`), Doppler spread times coherent observation/integration time (`T·Δf`), symbol and coding structure, beam weighting, and the chosen channel statistic. Neither the simulated BER plateau or aperture correction reported by the broadband paper nor Taylor’s reference widths should be transferred to a 5.8 GHz modem without matched geometry and waveform evidence.

Libration changes visible terrain and may cause frequency-selective fading through delayed-scatterer interference. Do not assume Rayleigh fading by default; surface statistics, beam weighting, polarization and coherent/diffuse balance matter. Alternatives include a measured complex response, coherent paths plus diffuse scatter, or slow log-amplitude variation; test against measured amplitude and frequency correlation.

### Pointing and aperture

At fixed aperture, gain rises with frequency squared while beamwidth narrows inversely. A 3 m, 60%-efficient dish at 5.8 GHz is about 43 dBi with beamwidth near 1.2°; the Moon is about 0.5° across. The Moon therefore fits within and partly fills the beam; it does not imply TX/RX misalignment. Pointing/tracking error reduces coupling. Compute both patterns and integrate over scattering; scalar pointing loss is an approximation.

Separated stations do not inherently misalign their beams. Each station points toward its own apparent direction of the selected lunar region; parallax, tracking error, and narrow beams can change illuminated/received overlap over the disk. A monostatic equation copying TX gain to RX can overstate return; compute overlap from station coordinates, time and patterns.

## Noise and terrestrial propagation

### Lunar, Galactic, solar, and receiver noise

At microwave frequencies the lunar disc emits thermal radiation. It adds **antenna noise temperature**; it does not destructively cancel the echo. Coupling depends on beam, brightness distribution, pointing and lunar phase/local time. A beam much wider than the 0.5° disc receives only the beam-filling fraction of its brightness. Chang’E-2 measured 3.0/7.8 GHz brightness bracketing 5.8 GHz, but calibration offsets prevent a precise universal target-frequency brightness temperature. Values near 200–230 K are rough scales, not fixed inputs.

Galactic synchrotron falls steeply with frequency; at 5.8 GHz it is generally smaller than at meter wavelengths, but remains direction- and beam-dependent, especially toward the Galactic plane. Include CMB and receiver noise. ITU-R P.372-18 gives noise context; calculate antenna temperature for the actual beam rather than transfer a low-frequency noise factor.

The variable Sun may raise receiver temperature through the main beam or sidelobes when near the Moon or a sidelobe. Bursts can be strong; 2.8 GHz F10.7 is an activity monitor, not 5.8 GHz flux. Use measured solar contribution or a documented avoidance state. Local RFI, feed loss, LNA noise figure and cables are receiver/interference terms, not lunar propagation loss.

### Ionosphere, troposphere, and weather

The ionosphere is dispersive: first-order group delay and Faraday rotation scale roughly as frequency^-2; the equivalent excess path c times group delay also scales as frequency^-2. Carrier propagation phase in radians for a fixed electron column scales as frequency^-1. Effects at 5.8 GHz are much smaller than at 144 MHz. Faraday rotation on both legs affects linear polarization; separate stations see different TEC and magnetic projections. Ideal circular polarization is insensitive to rotation of the linear basis, though lunar depolarization and imperfect antennas can cause loss. Disturbed TEC/scintillation may perturb phase, but low-band outage probabilities cannot transfer directly.

Each endpoint has elevation-dependent gaseous attenuation and emission. At 5.8 GHz this is below strong absorption bands but not zero, especially at low elevation/humidity; ITU-R P.676 supplies the path method. Rain attenuation follows k*R^alpha integrated over path; coefficients depend on frequency/polarization and local rain rate/elevation. Wet feed/radome loss may matter. Treat rain as a site/time-conditioned state, not a universal multi-decibel constant.

## Illustrative 5.8 GHz link calculation (assumptions, not a sensitivity claim)

This calculation is included to make the scale and dependencies reviewable. It is **not** a measured 5.8 GHz EME link, a modem threshold, or a receiver sensitivity. Use the monostatic radar equation as a rough central-beam approximation:

\[
\frac{P_r}{P_t}=\frac{G_tG_r\lambda^2\sigma_{\rm OC}}{(4\pi)^3R_t^2R_r^2},\qquad
L_{\rm iso,dB}=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{(4\pi)^3R_t^2R_r^2}{\lambda^2\sigma_{\rm OC}}\right).
\]

Here gains in the power-ratio equation are linear. The gain-free reference loss gives net loss `L_net,dB = L_iso,dB - G_t,dBi - G_r,dBi`, before endpoint and implementation losses. The selected OC cross section already specifies the received polarization channel; do not subtract that polarization fraction again.

| Input | Illustrative assumption | Provenance/status |
|---|---:|---|
| Carrier / wavelength | 5.8 GHz / 5.17 cm | Design target. |
| Earth–Moon center range | 384,400 km, equal transmit/receive ranges | Mean-range geometry; actual ephemeris varies. |
| Lunar radius | 1,737.4 km | Physical geometry used to get projected area. |
| Assumed polarization-specific disk cross section | sigma_OC = 0.06*pi*a^2 ~= 5.69e11 m^2 | Assumed opposite-circular-polarization (OC) channel; 0.06 uses one localized 7.209 GHz Mare Imbrium observation, not a global 5.8 GHz albedo. |
| Transmit power | 100 W = 50 dBm at antenna feed | Chosen design assumption. |
| TX/RX aperture | 3 m each, efficiency 0.60, gain approximately 43 dBi each | Chosen aperture model; assumes matched central pointing and ignores beam-weighted disk integration. |
| Total noise temperature | 100 K | Chosen total antenna+receiver assumption; requires actual beam filling, lunar/sky temperature, LNA and feed loss validation. |
| Noise bandwidth | 100 Hz | Illustrative analysis bandwidth, not an application-specific detector bandwidth. |
| Clear-sky endpoint loss | 0.5 dB aggregate | Chosen illustrative allowance, not an ITU site calculation. |

These inputs give an isotropic-reference radar loss L_iso ~= 284.549 dB, excluding antenna gains. With 42.999 dBi per dish, the net path loss is about 198.551 dB; 100 W at the feed gives received power about -148.551 dBm. At 100 K, kTB noise in 100 Hz is -158.599 dBm, hence SNR ~= 10.049 dB before the separately assumed 0.5 dB endpoint loss. This arithmetic is not measured. The full-disk, single-region OC RCS assumption, beam weighting, polarization, fade and implementation loss dominate uncertainty. An extra 14 dB stress decrement can be formed by separately choosing 6 dB lower RCS, 3 dB pointing/polarization, 3 dB receiver/implementation and 2 dB weather penalties; this is an unweighted illustrative stress, not an observed joint state or probability.

As an arithmetic cross-check, the 1296 MHz EME thesis reported about 271 dB isotropic two-way path loss. Holding RCS fixed and changing wavelength from 23.1 cm to 5.17 cm adds about 13.0 dB, giving about 284 dB. This is only the radar-equation wavelength term; actual RCS, antenna gain, noise and coupling change too. Do not interpret the result as demodulator sensitivity without measured waveform, receiver bandwidth, channel, coding and detector.

## Proposed channel cases and model choices

These cases are compatible with an application’s separately specified waveform and receiver. They define propagation/noise inputs, not a required communication success probability.

| Case | Physical state | Required components and intended interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate | Mean-range geometry; ephemeris-computed bulk delay/Doppler; several-percent total radar albedo; moderate subradar quasi-specular lobe plus diffuse skirt; nominal beam overlap; measured receiver and quiet-sky noise; clear or average atmosphere. | Keep deterministic geometry, slowly varying phase/amplitude, and finite disk delay/Doppler response distinct. “Moderate” is a constructed state until matched 5.8 GHz observations set its distribution. |
| Adverse weak signal | Apogee-ish range; suboptimal but visible elevation/pointing; smaller effective albedo / less favorable subradar terrain; weaker co-polar fraction; less favorable bistatic beam coupling where station separation, pointing uncertainty or patterns reduce weighted disk illumination/collection; increased receiver+Moon noise; moderate rain/gas/TEC; low-percentile coherent fade. | Attribute dB terms by cause. Retain delayed scattered power and time-varying Doppler; do not convert every effect into independent Gaussian noise. |
| Outage / acquisition failure | Moon near horizon, obstructed site, beam misses/overlap collapses, severe pointing error or local RF interference; solar proximity/burst or deep fading if observed; no valid tracking/ephemeris solution. | Report as a state with its own observability conditions, not as an arbitrarily huge AWGN variance. Separate no usable RF energy, loss of acquisition, and decoder failure. |

### Native implementation candidates

**Candidate A — low-cost geometric baseline.** Add ephemeris bulk delay/Doppler, complex scalar fading and aggregate noise temperature. It cannot reproduce disk support, frequency-selective fades, polarization or beam overlap. Measured material structure within a symbol/frame falsifies it as high-fidelity.

**Candidate B — minimum structured model.** Divide the disk into delay/Doppler annuli or patches. Weight separate TX/RX patterns, a Hagfors-like quasi-specular lobe plus diffuse return, and optional co-/cross-polar power. Sum correlated complex path fields at the waveform output; diffuse power density only defines expected PDP/PSD, not samples to add incoherently to the waveform. Use ephemeris path, derivative, retarded epochs and subradar/libration state. Check patch resolution by convergence of integrated power and delay-Doppler moments.

**Candidate C — measured replay / conditional hybrid.** With paired 5.8 GHz I/Q captures, estimate impulse response, bulk Doppler, coherence and receiver noise; replay held-out sessions through the native receiver. Retain station, beam, range/elevation, time, weather, polarization, power, bandwidth and calibration metadata. Cross-frequency evidence is prior information only.

Alternatives include a Gaussian lobe, empirical delay-Doppler kernel, or discrete reflectors plus diffuse scatter. Simplify components if matched observations show no benefit; falsify scalar gain if delayed energy, non-flat Doppler or within-band coherence loss appears. Keep propagation separate from modem implementation effects.

## Validation evidence needed

Geometry agreement cannot validate lunar scattering, and self-echo observations cannot by themselves validate separated-station EME. Target-frequency captures with calibrated station patterns and noise are the decisive missing evidence. The [appraisal protocol](../../../_appraisal/highFidelity-triple_Robust_Modem/experiment-protocol.md#eme-validation) contains the concrete validation plan and decision gates.

## Unresolved evidence needs

- No verified open 5.8 GHz whole-disk radar cross-section series, EME complex capture, or modem end-to-end field trial was found. The closest-frequency direct surface-radar example inspected here is 7.209 GHz bistatic; the detailed measured communications-system cross-check is at 1296 MHz.
- A target-frequency, geometry-resolved distribution for coherent versus diffuse echo power, delay-power profile, frequency correlation, and fade duration remains the main lunar-channel gap. Existing resolved orbital SAR images answer different incidence/polarization questions than near-backscatter EME.
- A calibrated full-disk lunar brightness-temperature estimate at 5.8 GHz with beam coupling, lunar local time and phase, surface composition, and polarization is not established by the 3/7.8 GHz radiometry; the published calibration issue should remain visible in noise uncertainty.
- Practical station separation, antennas, tracking error, beam overlap, feeder loss, transmit power, desired waveform occupied bandwidth, receiver bandwidth/noise figure, and terminal polarization are application inputs. Without them the worked link budget is intentionally illustrative.
- Local propagation distributions require site and elevation data: ITU P.676 atmospheric profiles, P.838 rain rate/path, TEC/geomagnetic state, and 5.8 GHz solar flux or receiver-temperature measurements. A single universal weather/ionosphere margin is not justified.
- A complete field acquisition record must distinguish no detectable echo from search-range miss, tracking/pointing loss, signal-processing loss, and decoding failure. Such distinctions are needed before any simulated “weak-to-outage” probability can be compared with receiver behavior.
