# Simulation methods, validation practice and few-trial statistics

- **Part of.** [High-fidelity weak-signal channels for the triple Robust Modem](README.md). Evidence grades, access-status words and conventions are defined in [00-method-and-modem-context](00-method-and-modem-context.md).
- **Scope.** Literature review only. Nothing was built or run on the modem, and no repository file outside this directory was changed.
- **Status.** Prepared on 13 November 2026 by one research pass, then checked against its sources by a second, independent pass. Sources that could not be opened are marked by access status; values not read in a source are not presented as verified.
- **Related chapters.** HF channel models [33](33-hf-channel-models-and-simulators.md); noise [12](12-sub9khz-noise.md) and [50](50-noise-and-interference-mf-hf.md); lunar scattering [40](40-eme-lunar-scattering-and-geometry.md); implementation options [70](70-implementation-options.md).

**Summary.**

- Three families of Doppler-shaped fading generators exist: filtered Gaussian noise (low-rate FIR/IIR plus interpolation, or one inverse FFT per block), sums of sinusoids, and autoregressive recursions. None is exact in every respect. A single realisation of an N-sinusoid generator has a time-averaged autocorrelation with variance (1 − J0²)/N [P], so few-sinusoid generators are not ergodic; block generators are periodic and cannot stream; recursions need stationary initialisation.
- Standards say how long a fading test must run. ITU-R F.1487 asks for the longer of 3000/Doppler-spread and 100/(BER × bit rate) [S]. 3GPP asks for 990 wavelengths (990/f_d) and forbids a verdict earlier [S]. For spreads of 0.001–0.1 Hz this is 8 hours to 35 days of simulated time [E]. Harris simulations of HF modems found 4–5 hours necessary to stabilise the measured BER at a 1 Hz spread, 3.5–6 times the F.1487 length, and much longer at 0.1 Hz [G, E].
- The WSJT-X simulators build Watterson and truncated-"Lorentzian" fading with one FFT per file and normalise power per file. The Lorentzian's `fspread` is the full width at −19.6 dB (half-power width 0.30 × fspread); a record with T·fspread ≤ 2 does not fade at all [G, E]. The codec2 generator applies its Gaussian as a filter amplitude response, which makes the generated 2σ about 0.71 of the nominal spread if the ITU convention is intended [G, E].
- Validation precedents: tolerance masks on delay profile and temporal correlation (3GPP TR 37.977 [S]); replay validation in underwater acoustics, where simulated packet error rate stayed within 0.20 of sea-trial values but was slightly optimistic because the sounding aliased delay and Doppler [P]; an HF on-air comparison in which a classic simulator missed "blocky" error patterns [G].
- Memoryless noise samplers (Class A, alpha-stable, mixtures) reproduce an amplitude distribution but neither burst structure nor bandwidth scaling; Markov–Middleton noise adds burst memory while keeping a Class A marginal [P]. The published Vd–bandwidth conversion rests on MF recordings analysed in bandwidths of 6 Hz–100 kHz and is stated to be strictly valid only for MF [T]; ITU-R P.372-18 reproduces it as a figure valid for MF and HF [S]. Neither reaches the modem's sub-hertz detection bandwidths.
- For few expensive trials: exact or Wilson intervals, sequential tests, effective sample size, and importance sampling, whose required sample size grows as exp(KL divergence) [P] and which fails for per-sample noise biasing at these record lengths [E].

Conventions: grades [S] standard, [P] research paper or preprint (peer-review status checked separately), [T] technical report, [B] book/thesis, [G] grey literature (including source code), [E] own derivation (formula and arithmetic shown). Access status is given per source in the References ("read", "abstract", "secondary", or "biblio" = existence and bibliographic data confirmed through the DOI registry but text not read). Statements resting on memory are marked "[recollection, unverified]". Reference keys such as [F1487] resolve in the References.

## Physical mechanisms

This section states what a sampled simulator has to represent and where numerical error originates. Propagation physics is in the other notes.

### 1. From a physical path to a sampled operation

For a path with time-varying delay τ_i(t) and gain a_i(t), the complex-envelope relation is y(t) = Σ_i a_i(t)·x(t − τ_i(t))·exp(−j2π f_c τ_i(t)), with x the complex envelope referred to f_c [textbook form, recollection of the standard result; the consequences below are derived, E]. Two consequences matter for an audio-rate simulator:

- **Translated systems (HF, EME, MF with a radio).** The Doppler shift is −f_c·dτ/dt and is the same for every audio component; the envelope is also time-scaled by (1 − dτ/dt). A fractional delay applied to the *audio* alone produces only −f_audio·dτ/dt. Example [E]: path-length rate 30 m/s gives dτ/dt = 30/c = 1.0×10⁻⁷; at 14 MHz the true shift is 1.4 Hz, the audio-only implementation gives 1.5×10⁻⁴ Hz at 1.5 kHz, a factor f_c/f_audio ≈ 9300 too small. With a two-way path-length rate of 600 m/s (range rate 300 m/s, dτ/dt = 2.0×10⁻⁶) at 5760 MHz the shift is 11.5 kHz and the envelope slips 120 µs per 60 s (illustrative values). A consistent simulator therefore applies the phase term and the delay from the same τ(t).
- **Direct audio radiation (sub-9 kHz).** There is no translation: the channel is pure time-scaling of the real signal, Δf = −f·dτ/dt. At 8 kHz a 1 m/s path-length rate gives 2.7×10⁻⁵ Hz and 19 µs of slip per 5760 s bit [E].

### 2. Doppler-shaped Gaussian tap gains

A diffuse path is modelled as a zero-mean complex Gaussian process with a prescribed Doppler power spectrum. ITU-R F.1487 defines the Gaussian shape exp(−(ν−ν₀)²/2σ²) and calls **2σ** the "frequency spread" [F1487, Annex 1 eq. (2), Annex 3 §1] [S]. From that definition [E]:

- gain autocorrelation r(τ) = exp(−(πB_dτ)²/2), with B_d = 2σ; half-correlation time 0.375/B_d;
- level-crossing rate N_R = √π·B_d·ρ·exp(−ρ²) and average fade duration (exp(ρ²)−1)/(ρ√π·B_d), from Rice's result N_R = √(b₂/(π b₀))·ρ·exp(−ρ²) with b₂/b₀ = (2πσ)² (the same formula returns the familiar √(2π)·f_d·ρ·exp(−ρ²) for the Jakes spectrum, which checks it) [Rice44, biblio; derivation E];
- at −10 dB below rms: 0.51·B_d crossings per second, mean fade 0.19/B_d; at −20 dB: 0.18·B_d and 0.057/B_d [E].

A Lorentzian spectrum (exponential autocorrelation) has an infinite second moment, so its level-crossing rate is unbounded and depends on the sample rate; every practical "Lorentzian" generator therefore truncates or band-limits, and the truncation is part of the model [E].

### 3. Why each generator family has an intrinsic error

- **Filtered noise at a low rate, then interpolation.** Linear interpolation from rate f_low has response sinc²(f/f_low): a component at f₀ suffers droop and leaves an image at f_low − f₀ that is 38 dB down for f₀/f_low = 0.1 and 51 dB down for 0.05 [E].
- **Block inverse-FFT (spectral) synthesis.** The output is periodic with the block length and cannot be continued; frequency resolution is 1/T, so a spectrum narrower than about 2/T is represented by a handful of lines.
- **Sum of sinusoids.** With a finite number of fixed-frequency sinusoids the time average of one realisation does not converge to the ensemble statistics (Section "Measured and reported values", A).
- **Autoregressive recursion.** The method has "unavoidable numerical difficulties", which the authors overcome with "a simple heuristic approach", and needs a start-up procedure to be stationary from the first sample [BB05, abstract]; the abstract does not name the difficulty, and that it is ill-conditioning of the Yule–Walker equations for band-limited spectra is a recollection, unverified.

Further numerical pitfalls [E]:
- *Tail aliasing at the low rate.* With the low-rate Nyquist frequency at 4σ a Gaussian spectrum loses 6×10⁻⁵ of its power to aliasing (2.7×10⁻³ at 3σ). A Lorentzian of half-width f_c keeps 1 − (2/π)·atan(f_N/f_c) outside f_N: 7.9 % at f_N = 8f_c and 1.3 % at 50f_c, so it cannot be generated at a low rate without an explicit truncation.
- *Energy normalisation.* Normalising by the design (ensemble) gain preserves the model's power fluctuation; normalising each record removes it (section D).
- *Tail handling.* A delayed path extends the output beyond the input; circular implementations wrap it to the start unless the record is zero-padded.
- *Time-varying sinusoid frequencies.* Phase must be the running integral of frequency, not frequency × time.

### 4. Very slow fading

Filter memory scales with 1/B_d. For a Gaussian spectrum the tap-gain autocorrelation is Gaussian with σ = 1/(πB_d) (318 s at B_d = 0.001 Hz) and the shaping-filter impulse response has σ_h = 0.225/B_d (225 s), so a ±4σ_h filter spans 1800 s (30 min). A first-order recursion with half-width f_c has time constant 1/(2πf_c) (159 s at 0.001 Hz) and, started from zero, needs 366 s to reach 99 % of its variance; started from a draw of its stationary distribution it needs none. At 12 kHz the pole is at 1 − 5.2×10⁻⁷ for f_c = 0.001 Hz, which single precision (ε ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁷) cannot hold accurately [E]. The statistical counterpart: a record of length T contains only about 1.77·B_d·T independent power states (about 1.0 for B_d·T ≤ 0.1, 2.2 at 1, 18 at 10, 178 at 100) [E, from ∫exp(−(πB_dτ)²)dτ = 0.282/B_d]. Applied to the presets under scrutiny (spread × bit length; this treats each preset's Doppler value as a diffuse 2σ spread, whereas the simulator uses it as a bound on offset and wander, see "Comparison with the modem's presets"): 0.001 Hz × 5760 s = 5.8, about 10 states per bit; 0.1 Hz × 12–26 s = 1.2–2.6, about 2–5 states; 6 Hz and 30 Hz × 12–26 s give 130–1400 states. The first two are slow-fading cases in which a bit does not average the fading [E].

### 5. Time-varying delay

A fractional-delay filter has a gain and phase error that depend on the fractional part of the delay. When the delay drifts, the error is swept and appears as spurious amplitude and phase modulation at the rate (delay rate × sample rate). The Lagrange interpolator is maximally flat at zero frequency and its gain never exceeds one inside its optimal delay range; a truncated-sinc design has a Gibbs error that does not shrink with order; at the Nyquist frequency no real-coefficient filter can have error below |sin(πD)| [Val95, §3.1.4–3.3] [B].

### 6. Frequency-dependent deterministic paths

A single-tuned resonance H = 1/(1 + j2Qδ), δ = (f−f₀)/f₀, has bandwidth f₀/Q, group delay Q/(πf₀) at resonance and phase slope 2Q/f₀ rad per Hz of detuning [E]. At f₀ = 8 kHz: Q = 50 → 160 Hz, 2.0 ms, 0.72°/Hz; Q = 500 → 16 Hz, 19.9 ms, 7.2°/Hz, so a detuning of 0.8 Hz (100 ppm) already rotates the phase by 0.1 rad. A slowly drifting resonance is therefore a phase and amplitude modulator for a 0.06 Hz-wide signal; its group delay is irrelevant at that bandwidth but not for the 225 Hz mode. A sampled implementation must resolve the resonance: the envelope time constant is 1/(π·bandwidth) = 19.9 ms at Q = 500, so an FIR or block-FFT realisation needs about five time constants (0.1 s, 1200 taps at 12 kHz) and a frequency grid finer than about a tenth of the 16 Hz bandwidth [E].

### 7. Scatterer ensembles and speckle

A surface return is a sum over many scatterers with independent phases. With many comparable contributions the sum is complex Gaussian (fully developed speckle), and its Doppler spectrum is the distribution of scatterer power over differential range rate; a few dominant facets give non-Rayleigh statistics and a discrete spectrum. Spectrum shaping (Section 2) and explicit scatterer sums are two routes to the same second-order statistics and differ in higher-order behaviour and in how delay–Doppler coupling is carried [E, generic; lunar specifics in [40](40-eme-lunar-scattering-and-geometry.md)].

### 8. Impulsive noise and bandwidth

Middleton separates interference whose input bandwidth is narrower than the receiver's front end (Class A: the receiver's transient decay is negligible against the emission's duration) from interference broader than it (Class B: the receiver is "shock-excited" and "rings"), and calls their sum Class C [OTR76-86, abstract note and §1.1] [T]. Sferics are broadband against any audio-band receiver, so their statistics in a detection bandwidth depend on the bandwidth: the impulsiveness measure V_d (rms-to-average envelope ratio in dB) falls as bandwidth narrows [NTIA85-173, bandwidth-conversion section, Fig. 94] [T]. Its 1.05 dB Gaussian value is specifically for the Rayleigh envelope of zero-mean complex Gaussian noise; it is not a universal floor (a constant-envelope signal has 0 dB). An alpha-stable law with α < 2 has no finite variance, so an rms-referenced level is undefined for it [SN93, biblio; standard property; sample variance observed to diverge in a numerical check, E].

### 9. Dependent trials

Fading makes successive trials dependent. For a stationary series of n samples with marginal variance σ² and lag correlations ρ_k, the exact finite-n variance of the sample mean is (σ²/n)[1 + 2Σ_{k=1}^{n−1}(1−k/n)ρ_k]. Relative to the iid variance σ²/n, the corresponding variance-equivalent effective sample size is n_eff = n/[1 + 2Σ_{k=1}^{n−1}(1−k/n)ρ_k]. Replacing the finite weights (1−k/n) by 1 is only a large-n approximation when the correlation sum is suitably summable; neither formula is a universal calibration for a KS statistic or another test [BH46, TZ84, biblio; formula standard].

## How the literature models or simulates this

**Frameworks.** Bello's system functions and the WSSUS assumption underlie the tapped-delay-line simulator; Bello also treats wide-sense stationary and uncorrelated-scattering channels separately, shows them to be time–frequency duals, and proposes a "Quasi-WSSUS" model for radio channels [Bello63, abstract] [P]. The Watterson model in ITU-R F.1487 is "a stationary Gaussian scatter" model, "valid for use in 3 kHz channels" and possibly applicable to 12 kHz [F1487 Annex 1 §1] [S]. ITS summarised its limits as bandwidths below 12 kHz, negligible delay dispersion, a single skywave path and stationarity in delay and frequency [NTIA98-348 §1] [T]. The older F.520-2 gives no test length but lists what a simulator may need beyond two Rayleigh paths: selective fades 0.1–1.2 kHz wide moving at 0.5–2 kHz/s, frequency drifts, AGC, synthesiser phase jumps [F520 Annex 3] [S]. The ITS wideband model (Vogler and Hoffmeyer) adds a delay shape factor, Doppler shift that varies with delay, and a choice of Gaussian or Lorentzian Doppler shape; its validation was a visual comparison of simulated and measured scattering functions for three auroral near-vertical cases, and the authors state that quantitative validation was left for later work [NTIA92-284 §3] [T]. The software report says "true validation … must wait for" the hardware simulator [NTIA98-348 §4] [T]. For non-WSSUS channels Matz defines a local scattering function and a channel correlation function and a "doubly underspread" class [Matz05, abstract] [P]. Pätzold's group fitted a non-stationary, time-continuous simulation model to measured Doppler power spectra of shallow underwater channels and assessed it only on the average Doppler shift and the Doppler spread [Nad17, abstract] [P]. The underwater community warns that WSSUS "was developed for narrowband systems" and that tap correlation appears when fading statistics depend on frequency [vW13] [P]; an earlier simulator paper found the uncorrelated-scattering assumption broken by phase correlation between taps [vW08, abstract] [P].

**Fading generators in software.** MathWorks documents two techniques: filtered Gaussian noise brought to the signal rate by polyphase and then linear interpolation, and a sum-of-sinusoids method (GMEDS1 of Pätzold et al. [Pat09]); its channel object keeps state so that the output does not depend on how the input is split into blocks, and it offers Jakes, flat, Gaussian, bi-Gaussian, rounded, bell and restricted or asymmetric Jakes shapes [MW-fading] [G]. Hardware emulators use a low-rate IIR shaping filter and staged interpolation [Alim11] [P]; Komninakis's software simulator uses one fixed IIR filter and a variable polyphase interpolator [Kom03, abstract] [P]. Open amateur tools are described in section D above. The spectral-representation literature in structural engineering covers random-phase synthesis of the kind WSJT-X uses [SJ72, SD91, biblio; not read, and the statement that amplitude-deterministic random-phase sums are only asymptotically Gaussian is from recollection, unverified].

**Standardised time-varying test channels.** 3GPP defines a two-tap "moving" channel with one sinusoidally sweeping delay, a two-tap "birth–death" channel with paths jumping every 191 ms, and a deterministic Doppler trajectory for high-speed trains [TS25141 Annex D] [S]. These are stress patterns with fixed minimum durations, not statistical models.

**Scatter and scintillation simulators.** Nunes et al. sort scintillation simulators into physics-based (split-step; accurate but "complicated to use"), statistical (the Cornell model: easy, but the amplitude–phase relation "is not justified on physical grounds") and phase-screen models; they also note that real records are non-stationary beyond a few minutes whereas synthetic ones can be made arbitrarily long and stationary [Nunes22 §1 and the text at Fig. 19] [P]. The Cornell toolkit takes two inputs, S4 and τ0 [Cornell-tools] [G]; its internal filter structure is not quoted here because the paper was not obtained [Hum09, abstract]. Radar-clutter work generates correlated non-Gaussian processes by spherically invariant models or memoryless non-linear transforms of Gaussian processes [Rang95, TW99, Watts12, WTW13 — biblio only; descriptions from recollection, unverified].

**Noise.** Middleton's Class A/B models [OTR76-86, Mid77, SM77, Mid99], stable laws with the Chambers–Mallows–Stuck generator [SN93, CMS76, Weron96], Markov–Middleton bursts [Ndo13, Chen24], clustering-Poisson sferic arrival models [CFS03, Gere23], periodic and cyclostationary power-line models [ZD02, Kat06], and empirical APD families indexed by V_d [NTIA85-173, P372] are the main published options (OTR76-86, Chen24, Gere23, NTIA85-173 and P372 read; the others at biblio or abstract level here). ITU-R SM.1753 splits measured noise into white Gaussian, impulsive and single-carrier parts and reports pulse level, length and period distributions [SM1753 §3–4] [S]. Methods that impose a marginal distribution and an autocovariance together exist [LM82, CN96, biblio], but they control second-order structure only [recollection, unverified].

**Validation practice.** Otnes et al. adopt the definition of validation as showing that a model "within its domain of applicability possesses a satisfactory range of accuracy consistent with the intended application", and add that validation should also locate the borders of that domain [Otnes13 §I] [P]. They order observables as scalar (delay spread, Doppler spread, BER, SINR; suited to large data sets), one-dimensional (delay profile, Doppler spectrum) and two-dimensional (spreading function; qualitative), and recommend including communication signals of different kinds because a coherent and an incoherent scheme respond to different channel properties [Otnes13 §III–IV]. A sounding with probe period T can measure delay spread up to T and Doppler spread up to 1/T, so the two trade against each other and whatever exceeds them aliases [Otnes13 §II; symbols were lost in text extraction, the relation is the standard one]. 3GPP validates an emulator, not the model: delay profile, temporal correlation, spatial correlation and cross-polarisation against masks [TR37977 §8.3] [S]. Scattering-function estimators and their bias are treated by Kay and Doyle and by Artés et al. [KD03, AMH04, biblio; not read]. Spectral estimation references: [Welch67, Thomson82, biblio]. Two standard facts bound what such a comparison can show [E]: an unaveraged spectral estimate has a standard error equal to its mean, falling as 1/√K with K independent segments; and Doppler resolution is the reciprocal of the segment length. Resolving a spectrum into ten cells with 25 % standard error therefore needs 160/B_d seconds: 27 minutes at 0.1 Hz, 44 hours at 0.001 Hz. A one-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) test calibrated for independent observations is not made generally valid for autocorrelated envelope samples by substituting an effective sample size: that adjustment is statistic-, dependence- and null-specific. Use independent channel episodes as inferential units, a block procedure justified for the process and statistic, or a null simulation that preserves within-episode dependence. Keep each episode intact when resampling or simulating the null.

**Documented simulator-versus-field differences.** (i) Underwater replay: slightly optimistic, cause identified (section E). (ii) HF: in a 2014 mid-latitude trial (five-minute runs, daytime) simulated SNR ranged over roughly 10–15 dB against 15–20 dB on air, errors on air came in blocks that the simulator did not produce, and the best data-unit size was 1024 bytes on air against about 200 bytes predicted from simulation [Isode14] [G]. (iii) F.520-2 nevertheless records that "some administrations have reported good correlation" between simulator and on-air tests [F520 considering b)] [S]. (iv) High-latitude measurements were used to characterise modems outside the standard parameter sets [Ang98, biblio; content not read]. (v) Harris states that the Watterson model represents the signal perturbations over periods "of up to a few seconds" but not the SNR variation over "a few seconds to tens of minutes", and built an intermediate-duration variation model, fitted to recorded long-haul and NVIS SNR series, into a Watterson simulator, proposing it for F.1487 [FK09, abstract] [P].

**Statistics.** Sources located: interval estimation [CP34, AC98, BCD01, BCD02, JL97, Han83]; sequential analysis [Wald45]; dependent samples [BH46, TZ84, Geyer92, Kunsch89]; importance sampling and rare events [SB90, GW97, Hest95, Owen, CD18, PSIS, Bucklew04, RT09]; common random numbers [GY92]; paired binary outcomes [McN47]; model uncertainty [KOH01, TK07, DKD09]; verification and validation [Sar13, OR10]; extremes [Coles01]; tolerance limits [Wilks41]. Apart from those marked "read" in the References, these were confirmed bibliographically and are summarised from general knowledge.

**Learned and GPU simulators.** See section H. Sionna provides GPU-batched 3GPP stochastic models and, since 2023, differentiable ray tracing aimed at "digital twins" [Sionna22, SionnaRT, abstract]; digital-twin validations published since then find delay spreads under-predicted (section H). Nothing located addresses narrowband hour-long signals.

## What a high-fidelity simulation would need

These are options with their limits. None is put forward as the design, and better approaches may exist.

**Inputs.** (1) Per path: delay law τ(t), mean gain, specular fraction, Doppler spectrum shape with its width convention stated (2σ, half-power, or full width at a named level — the `fspread` of the WSJT-X Lorentzian generators is about three times their 2σ, that of its Watterson generator equals 2σ, and codec2's nominal spread is about √2 times its generated 2σ). (2) A statement of stationarity time. (3) Deterministic responses (antenna, filters). (4) Noise: level statistic (rms, median, F_a), reference bandwidth, impulsiveness measure, temporal description. (5) The carrier frequency for every translated case, since Doppler and delay must be consistent.

**Fading-process options.**
- *Low-rate shaped Gaussian process with band-limited interpolation.* Streams; any spectrum that a filter can approximate. Limits: interpolation images (−38 dB for linear interpolation at 10× oversampling); memory of 30 min at 0.001 Hz; needs stationary start-up.
- *One block for the whole test at a low rate.* For slow fading the entire record is small, so periodicity is harmless and the spectrum is exact on the frequency grid. Limits: test length fixed in advance; random-phase-only variants are not Gaussian with few lines.
- *Sum of sinusoids.* No run-in, trivially chunk-invariant, exact time shifts. Limits: non-ergodic per realisation; needs large N or re-randomisation; arbitrary spectra need frequency sampling from the spectrum.
- *First-order or higher recursions.* Exact for exponential autocorrelation; cheap. Limits: Gaussian and band-limited shapes are only approximated; conditioning; precision at 12 kHz.
- *Replay of measured gain records.* Reproduces whatever was measured, including non-stationarity. Limits: finite independent content, sounding aliasing, noise in the measurement, and no extrapolation.

**Doubly selective structure options.** Independent taps (WSSUS); delay–Doppler-coupled functions (ITS wideband model); explicit scatterer sums; direct replay. The first cannot represent drifting delays or correlated taps; the last cannot produce independent realisations.

**Delay and transfer-function options.** Whole-sample delays for static paths; windowed-sinc or Farrow filters for drifting ones; whole-signal resampling when time dilation is the dominant effect. Frequency-domain block filtering gives exact static responses but needs overlap handling and cannot follow fast parameter changes; recursive filters follow changes but create transients.

**Noise options.** Gaussian; memoryless non-Gaussian; Markov-modulated; filtered Poisson with a pulse library and clustered arrivals; replay; hybrids. A calibration loop that the literature supports: fix the rms level (F_a), tune to V_d and the APD shape in 200 Hz, then check that V_d follows the measured trend with bandwidth inside the analysed 6 Hz–100 kHz [NTIA85-173 eq. (55)], and compare pulse length and period distributions [SM1753]. Because the modem's detection bandwidths lie below the analysed range, the wideband waveform, not the narrowband statistic, has to carry the model.

**Observables to validate against.** Envelope distribution; Doppler spectrum and temporal correlation with tolerance masks; level-crossing rate and fade duration; delay profile; scattering function estimated exactly as in the source; phase-drift statistics; noise APD in more than one bandwidth, V_d, pulse inter-arrival and duration distributions, spectrum; and end-to-end error statistics for at least two modulation types with different sensitivities. Each published observable should be regenerated with the source's own estimator and record length, since estimator bias is part of the published number.

**Selected initial statistical plan.** Start with a fixed batch of 20 independent, identically distributed complete channel episodes per predeclared scenario stratum, sampled independently from that stratum's specified episode distribution. Predeclare the scenario distribution, random-seed generator and outcome rules; retain sampled seeds for reproduction. Fixed seeds alone define a reproducible finite set, not an iid sample for Clopper–Pearson inference. An episode is the unit: its fading, noise, bursts, mode changes and receiver outcomes may be dependent within it. Preserve the configured-calendar/keyed-ordinal scope and score physical completion only after two scored quiet slots. For binary completion, report a two-sided exact Clopper–Pearson interval; its nominal 95% coverage is marginal for one predeclared stratum/outcome under the iid Bernoulli episode assumption. For a family-wide claim across K predeclared stratum/outcome decisions, use Bonferroni intervals at per-decision error α=0.05/K (or another predeclared familywise-valid adjustment); do not select favorable strata or endpoints post hoc and call their unadjusted intervals 95%. Declare pass or fail only if the full applicable interval is on the corresponding side of the predeclared requirement; otherwise call it inconclusive. Even 20/20 completions yield a marginal two-sided 95% lower bound of only 0.832, so this is a bounded screen, not a high-reliability demonstration. For continuous episode summaries, report every episode value and the fixed-n Student-t interval for the mean only when the independent episode-level sampling distribution is plausibly normal; otherwise show the data and state that this interval is not justified. For distribution comparisons, retain each episode's samples together and calibrate the chosen statistic by a predeclared null simulation over independent episodes with the same within-episode generator/dependence, or use an episode-level distance with uncertainty across episodes. Do not apply an iid KS p-value to pooled samples or an unvalidated effective-N substitution.

Keep this initial batch fixed. A later confirmation batch is a new analysis stage with its own predeclared error-budget allocation; do not keep adding episodes and reuse an ordinary fixed-n interval after looking. If unrestricted interim stopping is operationally necessary, use a confidence sequence whose assumptions hold for iid episode-level outcomes; Howard et al. give nonasymptotic confidence sequences with coverage uniform over time [Howard21]. This does not make dependent samples inside an episode independent. Define the estimand's timeout and resource-exhaustion policy in advance: non-completion by the service deadline, or exhaustion of a resource limit included in the service contract, is a completion failure. An unrelated external interruption may be marked invalid and replaced only under objective, predeclared criteria that do not depend on the outcome; retain its reason and count. Report any censoring, and do not treat outcomes censored by receiver failure as successful or omit them from continuous summaries without addressing informative censoring. These are proposed procedures, not empirical validation.

**Reproducibility.** Counter-based random numbers indexed by source and sample; double-precision phase accumulation; a regression test that the output is identical for different chunk sizes.

The presets (section 5 of [00](00-method-and-modem-context.md)) are a declared synthetic stress (kind A in its section 6). This chapter bears on them through what their numbers mean in published generators, how long a run has to be, and which numerical risks the simulator's generators carry. The test lengths below apply the F.1487 rule as if each preset's Doppler value were a diffuse 2σ spread, which in the simulator it is not.

The simulator's generators, set against the method guidance above:

- *Two-path family whose direct path never fades.* This is closer to F.1487's specular-plus-diffuse construction (a non-fading tap, Annex 1 §3) than to its pair of equal Rayleigh paths, so a preset's Doppler value cannot be compared with an F.1487 2σ or a WSJT-X `fspread` without stating the convention. In the GUI's changing-channel realisation the number of independent channel states in a run is set by the knot spacing, which [00] describes as synthetic; the duration rules of section B and the trial statistics of section G apply with that spacing in place of 1/B_d.
- *Ornstein–Uhlenbeck and Gaussian-spectrum ray gains.* The OU option is a first-order recursion, so the points of section 4 apply: a stationary initial state, a pole very close to one at audio sample rates for sub-0.01 Hz widths, and an explicit truncation of the Lorentzian tail. The Gaussian-spectrum option needs the filter memory or block length of section 4 (30 min at 0.001 Hz). [00] does not say how either is initialised or in what precision, so this is not assessed here.
- *Sinusoidal and Gaussian-erf selective coefficients.* These are deterministic bounded laws on three branches. In the Watterson framework selectivity arises from path delays, and F.520-2 lists selective fades 0.1–1.2 kHz wide moving at 0.5–2 kHz/s [S]; no source in this chapter validates a branch-wise multiplicative law.
- *White Gaussian noise of fixed variance.* This is the moderate column of the noise rows. The −164 dBm/Hz default is an rms density: for Class A noise with A = Γ′ = 0.01 the rms lies 21.6 dB above the median envelope, and for alpha-stable noise it is undefined [E].
- *Single-sample impulses at a Bernoulli rate.* These are broadband against every receiver bandwidth, so after the receiver filter they ring as Class B interference does [T]. They have no burst memory (unlike Markov–Middleton noise) and no clustered arrivals (unlike measured sferics), and their filtered amplitude distribution is not tied to a measured APD or V_d [E].
- *One constant level, with one ramped attenuation interval.* The intermediate-term variation documented on air and in Harris's model (seconds to tens of minutes; about 4 dB standard deviation according to the papers Isode cites) is absent, as it is from the classic Watterson simulator [Isode14, FK09].

## Contradictions, open questions, and things not found

**Disagreements.**
- *How long is long enough?* F.1487 says 3000/spread; 3GPP's 990/f_d is about half that in equivalent units; the Harris simulations found 4–5 hours necessary at 1 Hz, 3.5–6 times the F.1487 length, and call the ITU figure too optimistic. None of these rules was derived for channels that are themselves non-stationary.
- *Are classic HF simulators realistic?* F.520-2 reports good correlation with on-air tests; the 2014 on-air comparison, Harris [FK09] and the ITS reports say the stationary two-path model misses minute-scale variation and wideband or disturbed behaviour. The answer depends on the observable: averaged BER versus error clustering.
- *Doppler shape.* Gaussian in the Watterson model; exponential-autocorrelation (Lorentzian) preferred by ITS for auroral spread-F; a truncated Lorentzian in WSJT-X. The shapes have very different crossing-rate behaviour and no source compares them on fade statistics.
- *"Eight sinusoids are enough."* True for ensemble statistics over many trials [ZX03]; false for the time average of one run [XZB06].
- *Scintillation.* Statistical two-parameter models are easy and validated on receiver outcomes, but are criticised as physically unjustified in their amplitude–phase relation [Nunes22].
- *Noise distribution.* Hall versus alpha-stable depends on site [CF00]; the CCIR and Herman–DeAngelis bandwidth conversions disagree, and neither is supported by data below 6 Hz or at VLF. NTIA 85-173 calls the Herman–DeAngelis curves strictly valid only for MF, whereas ITU-R P.372-18 says MF and HF.

**Open questions.** No located source validates a simulator for Doppler spreads of 0.001–0.1 Hz or for coherent integration over an hour. The effect of per-record power normalisation on published weak-signal sensitivity figures is not documented (the inference in section D is made here). The codec2 width factor (section D) is likewise inferred from the code and was not checked against the PathSim guide that the code cites as its reference. No tolerance has been tied to modem performance: 3GPP states its masks reflect measurability only.

**Not found or not done.** Not searched: MIL-STD-188-110 simulator-verification text; CTIA emulator test plans. Searched briefly, with the results integrated above: generative and digital-twin channel models after 2023 and critiques of their validation (no survey was read); GPU channel simulation for narrowband long signals (none found); HF modem on-air versus simulator comparisons (Furman–Koski 2009 and Weston–Koski 2015 added); Komninakis's IIR simulator and a Pätzold-group non-stationary model (abstracts only); filtered-Poisson and clustered sferic models (the clustering-Poisson model and two hardware generators, abstracts only; by its abstract, Fieve et al. 2007 predicts noise statistics from measurements and lightning data and is not a waveform simulator); ITU-R P.372 on V_d (edition 18 read). Not obtainable: full texts of [YB00], [BB05], [Kom03], [PB01], [Bello63], [Matz05], [Laakso96], [Hum09], [Ndo13], [CFS03], [Nad17], Jeruchim (1984), and the Furman–Nieto paper "Understanding HF channel simulator requirements in order to reduce HF modem performance measurement variability" (an uploaded copy exists on a document-sharing site whose text is not visible without its reader; venue and year not confirmed). Suggested leads that could not be confirmed online and are therefore not cited as sources: Jakes (1974); Tranter et al.; Hall (1966); Field and Lewinstein (1978); the Furman–Nieto paper's venue. The "10/p" rule of thumb attributed to Jeruchim could not be checked; the intervals in section G are computed instead.
