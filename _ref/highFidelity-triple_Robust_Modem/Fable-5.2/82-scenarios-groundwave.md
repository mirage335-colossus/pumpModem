# Illustrative groundwave scenario specifications

This is a candidate research input specification for the 1 MHz and 30 MHz
branches in [the completion plan](80-completion-plan.md) §§6.3–6.4. Scenarios
are conditional physical states for controlled comparisons. They have no assigned
occurrence probabilities. A combination of their extremes must not be presented
as an observed natural joint episode without supporting measurements. Explicitly
adversarial combinations may be separate stress cases with no natural occurrence
claim.

These descriptions do not qualify modem performance. Simulated output should be
compared with field observables in the stated domain before a branch is called
validated. Keep the complete waveform and channel continuous through Helper,
DATA and quiet-slot completion.

## Shared source, power and measurement contract

- Use the ITU-R P.368 reference radiator as the propagation normalization:
  1 kW e.m.r.p., short vertical over perfectly conducting ground, 300 mV/m at
  1 km. Store calculated (L_b), field, and terminal received power as distinct
  values. Preserve P.368's basic-transmission-loss convention; record antenna
  realized gain, mismatch and efficiency separately. Scale field with the square
  root of radiated power. The 1 kW value is a reference, not a claim that the
  modem transmitter radiates that power.
- For a modem comparison, use a declared 4 W delivered transmitter reference
  only after measuring or explicitly assigning antenna loss and feed loss. If
  using the GUI's 4 W / 180 dB / −164 dBm/Hz row as the target, the corresponding
  ideal-antenna condition is `Lb + Fa = 193.5 dB` under [00 §3.1](00-method-and-modem-context.md).
  Measure a local external-noise factor (F_a) and receiver noise floor; do not
  infer a groundwave range from that GUI number alone.
- A source's direct and terrain-reflected/diffracted components may be summed
  coherently only when their relative phase, delay and geometry are specified.
  Ionospheric returns from the wanted transmitter are coherent alternatives
  with their own path delay, phase and state. An unrelated transmitter is an
  independent co-channel source; add its waveform and carrier state separately.
- Record at minimum: absolute calibrated received power or field, complex
  transfer/phase, arrival delays, carrier offset/drift and short-term spectrum,
  receiver noise PSD/APD, environmental inputs, and uncensored outage periods.
  Hold transmitter EIRP/reference planes, antennas, route and receiver constant
  for baseline/corrected comparisons.

|---|---|---|---|
| **30M-A Low-antenna sea reference** | 30 MHz; 100 km sea route; vertical antennas 1–3 m above mean water; seawater σ=4–5 S/m and εr=70–80; no unobstructed direct line of sight, with horizon profile surveyed. Reference atmosphere k=4/3; record tide and coastline offsets. | P.368-10 smooth-sea surface-wave reference (within its frequency ceiling), compared with a declared rough-sea alternative and direct/space-wave geometry check. Normalize at 1 kW e.m.r.p.; also store actual realized radiated power and receive antenna pattern. The rough-impedance calculation is not a universal measured correction and is near its perturbation limit at 30 MHz in rough weather. |
| **30M-B Rough-sea episode** | Same route, hardware and EIRP as A; specify measured wave spectrum/direction, wind and tide for a single observation window. Keep the same route wet/shore geometry. | Pair smooth-water baseline with Barrick effective-impedance prediction using (a) measured directional spectrum and (b) wind-only spectrum. Report their model disagreement; do not tune spectrum to the received level and then call agreement independent. Coherent wanted component remains one surface-wave path unless measurements resolve additional paths. | Compare time-aligned received power/phase with environmental series. Hold out a separate episode for prediction check. Kirincich & Emery's 16.188 MHz one-way 45 km below-horizon record motivates this model comparison but does not validate 30 MHz; their 7 km high, visible station is a different space-wave/mixed-propagation branch. |
| **30M-C Terrain, clutter and raised receiver** | 30 MHz land route with surveyed terrain profile, land εr/σ, vegetation/buildings and both antenna heights. Include one low, below-horizon case and one elevated/horizon-visible case as separate geometry records. | Compare P.368 smooth-earth baseline, terrain diffraction/integral-equation path, and direct plus ground-reflected space-wave calculation using exact heights and phase. If using a building/forest penetration model, identify its measured frequency/domain and do not relabel it groundwave. Never combine mutually inconsistent direct-ray visibility and low-height groundwave assumptions. | Measure complex transfer along a spatially sampled route, calibrated path loss, antenna patterns, profile, and delay/ripple versus location. Dagefu et al.'s full-text 40 MHz results cover 0–200 m near-ground clutter/indoor paths and may inform a short-range control only; they do not validate this long route. |
| **30M-D Competing skywave or scatter** | Retain one wanted route from A or C. Add one physically declared competing mode only: same-source sporadic-E/F reflection or scatter, meteor, aircraft, or tropospheric scatter. For aircraft or meteor scatter of the wanted transmitter, propagate the same waveform as a delayed, phase-coherent, Doppler-shifted path when its coherence is supported. Only an unrelated co-channel transmitter has a separate independent waveform and carrier state. Run these as alternatives/paired controlled cases, not as a single sum of each mechanism's maximum. State desired-to-competitor ratio as a sweep when no matched observations exist. | Observe wanted/competitor power ratio, delay/Doppler spectrum, event timing and receiver acquisition/tracking. Existing aircraft and backscatter budgets are derived; meteor evidence is for meteor-burst links and does not prove a continuous competing arrival on a given 30 MHz link. No false joint occurrence probability is implied. |
| **30M-E No viable wanted surface path / outage** | 30 MHz, 100 km low land route using 3 mS/m and very-dry 0.1 mS/m sensitivity endpoints, vertical ground-level antennas; state terrain and local antenna losses. For sea, use a distinct 300 km branch with antenna/horizon/ducting state declared. | Use smooth-earth calculation as a nominal reference only, then add a measured terrain/antenna/noise record if available. The surface-path outage is a measured below-sensitivity interval or a synthetic zero of that surface component; non-detection alone does not prove zero transfer. Test total wanted-signal outage separately. | Record calibrated sensitivity/noise, time below acquisition threshold and post-outage recovery. 180 dB is not a universal outage threshold; it depends on transmitter radiated power, receiver noise, antenna and interference. No observed example currently establishes that a wanted 30 MHz surface path at 180 dB is dominated by the smooth-earth surface wave. |

## Validation gates

1. **Reference physics:** reproduce P.368/LFMF loss with pinned reference vectors; compare to an independent complex-field model and validate its phase/group-delay convention separately; verify the 6 dB loss-convention and reference-radiator conversions.
2. **Matched field evidence:** measure transmitter radiated power, both antenna patterns/heights, route/environment and calibrated receiver level/phase on the same interval. Compare held-out routes or dates; do not fit conductivity, sea spectrum or atmosphere to the validation samples.
3. **Mode discrimination:** collect enough frequency/space/time-resolved complex data to distinguish a surface path, direct/reflected space wave, terrain return and ionospheric/scatter arrival. Report unresolved mixture when the observables cannot separate them.
4. **Receiver relevance:** only after channel validation, replay the same continuous field/noise history through the native modem and preserve Helper, DATA, quiet-tail completion and explicit outages. Keep decode success, link budget agreement and propagation validation as separate results.

The closest decisive missing evidence is a calibrated one-way 30 MHz sea record with both a low below-horizon surface-wave geometry and a raised line-of-sight receiver, synchronized sea-state/refractivity inputs and complex phase. At 1 MHz it is a held-out calibrated mixed land/sea level-and-phase route with surveyed ground parameters, plus a separately recorded night-skywave interval. These gaps prevent a measured cross-frequency joint scenario claim; they do not prevent the conditional scenarios above from being implemented as hypotheses.

For completion controls, keep a hidden truth timeline of actual source activity and include both an interior fade with later DATA and two quiet slots after real transmission end. Report observed premature completion, erasure, ordered delivery, codec integrity and reacquisition/reset behavior; a deep fade covering two quiet-score apertures can be indistinguishable from actual silence.

## Bounded complex transfer control

For 1M-A, obtain magnitude from a pinned NTIA LFMF/P.368-10 calculation at each frequency of interest using the declared 1 MHz homogeneous-ground inputs. As a fully specified simple phase control, set h(f)=10^(−Lb(f)/20) exp[−j2π(f−f0)d/c] with f0=1 MHz and d=100 km, choosing zero phase at f0. Lb is basic transmission loss in dB; account for transmitter and receiver gains and external/receiver noise at their own reference planes once. This vacuum-slope phase is a synthetic nondispersive control, not LFMF phase or a measurement. For a physically richer comparator, use the complex homogeneous-earth Fock/Bremmer/Wait residue-series prescription and time convention in [90 §1.1](90-cross-check-calculations.md), independently reproduce its complex phase with a pinned implementation and phase-sign tests, then use the phase formulation of Millington–Pressey described in [20](20-groundwave-1mhz.md) for mixed ground. P.368/LFMF agreement alone checks magnitude implementation, not independent phase physics. Current documentation specifies the formulas and validation path; no new solver has been implemented or run.
