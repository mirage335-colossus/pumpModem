# Skywave dispersion at high and low latitudes: the measured extremes

- **Part of.** [High-fidelity weak-signal channels for the triple Robust Modem](README.md). Evidence grades, access-status words and conventions are defined in [00-method-and-modem-context](00-method-and-modem-context.md).
- **Scope.** Literature review and bounded public-data analysis. Nothing was built or run on the modem, and no repository file outside this directory was changed.
- **Status.** Prepared on 13 November 2026 by one research pass, then checked against its sources by a second, independent pass. Sources that could not be opened are marked by access status; values not read in a source are not presented as verified.
- **Related chapters.** signal level and absorption [30](30-skywave-signal-level-and-outages.md); mid-latitude dispersion [31](31-skywave-dispersion-midlatitude-nvis-longhaul.md); channel models [33](33-hf-channel-models-and-simulators.md).

**Summary.** "30 Hz Doppler spread, 7 ms delay" is exactly the ITU-R F.1487 high-latitude disturbed test point: two equal-power, independently Rayleigh-fading paths 7 ms apart, each with a Gaussian Doppler spectrum of 2σ = 30 Hz. F.1487 itself says 25 Hz and 5 ms are each exceeded about 5 % of the time at high latitudes. Against measurements it is a high-percentile test condition, not a bound. DAMSON (1995, four Scandinavian paths, 80 %-power composite estimators, SNR −5 to +5 dB in 3 kHz) found 5 %-exceeded composite Doppler spreads of 3–16 Hz (27 Hz in one band) and multipath spreads of 3.5–4.6 ms (5.7 ms in one band) on three 1,000–2,000 km paths, but 49.5–57 Hz (73 Hz above 14 MHz) and 7.4–11.4 ms on a 194 km path whose returns probably arrive off the great circle from the auroral zone; those maxima sit at the instrument limits (±40 Hz, 12.5 ms). The journal abstract's range of 2–55 Hz matches no table of the thesis, which reaches 73 Hz. The NRL prober on a 1,294 km trans-auroral path (1992) measured 2σ spreads of 5–30 Hz and 0.5–1 ms regularly at night at Kp ≤ 2, with scatter signals 30–55 dB weaker than specular ones and a mean Doppler shift that reached 25 Hz and moved by about 9 Hz in 90 s (one event); in the same campaign a MITRE probe on a 3,100 km path measured a median 2σ Doppler spread of 5 Hz, a 90th percentile of 13 Hz and a maximum of 17 Hz. Leicester sounders give 95th-percentile 80 % widths of up to 15.7 Hz as first published and 13.3 Hz after reprocessing (trough path, solar-maximum winter nights), and up to 13 Hz (polar cap). At low latitudes, post-sunset spread F produced Doppler widths above 6 Hz at −10 dB (15 Hz at −30 dB) on half of the evenings of a solar-maximum campaign, delay spreads near 1 ms and coherence bandwidths below 1.5 kHz (on 75 % of evenings). A VHF/UHF Recommendation gives evening-type trans-equatorial propagation fading rates up to about 15 Hz and Doppler spreads sometimes above 40 Hz, without stating the frequency; at HF, post-sunset plumes reached about ±60 Hz on a 4,200 km Hawaii–Samoa link (conference slides), while a 12,760 km Antarctica–Spain link had median 80 % widths of 0.5 Hz or less inside its ±2.5 Hz analysis window. No measured low-latitude differential delay above about 3.5 ms was found. Real severe channels also differ in structure from the two-ray preset: a dominant mode plus weak diffuse scatter, delay–Doppler slant, heavier-than-Gaussian tails, non-stationary shifts. The percentile statistics are conditioned on detection and on SNR of −5 to +5 dB (DAMSON) or at least 4–6 dB (Leicester) in 3 kHz, i.e. C/N0 of about 30–41 dB-Hz; only the Antarctica–Spain set (at least 16 dB-Hz) reaches the 20 dB-Hz simulated in the modem's HF rows, and it excludes spreads beyond ±2.5 Hz. MIL-STD-188-110C tests its 75 bit/s mode at 2 dB SNR on two equal Rayleigh paths 5 ms apart with 5 Hz (2σ) fading; the STANAG 4415 envelope of 10 ms and 50 Hz at 0 dB is known here only from a secondary source.

The completed public-data work below adds all-member QAN–ALE and Ottawa–Alert qualification, an unsuccessful published-statistic reproduction, retrospective seasonal tail validation and a selected pulse/riometer event join. These processed records extend the observational evidence but do not supply calibrated coherent channel responses or an endpoint-operation ledger.

## Physical mechanisms

### 1. What the published numbers mean (estimators)

The same channel yields very different "spread" numbers depending on the estimator. The conversions below are for a Gaussian Doppler spectrum with standard deviation σ unless stated [E].

| Estimator | Used by | Relation to 2σ (Gaussian) |
|---|---|---|
| 2σ of each path's Gaussian spectrum ("frequency spread") | ITU-R F.1487, F.520, MIL-STD-188-110C; NRL and MITRE (2σ of the whole measured delay or Doppler power profile); FFI/Jodalen DAMSON analysis | 1 |
| Central (or narrowest) width holding 80 % of power | DAMSON composite Doppler spread (Angling); Leicester | 2 × 1.2816σ = 1.28 × (2σ); i.e. 2σ = 0.78 × W80 |
| Central width holding 95 % of power | DAMSON 1995 conference paper (all modes pooled) | 2 × 1.96σ = 1.96 × (2σ) |
| FWHM (−3 dB) | SuperDARN-type Lorentzian width; Angling's simulator tests; Alaskan HF beacons (FWHM of a fitted Gaussian) | 2.355σ = 1.18 × (2σ) |
| Full width at −10 / −20 / −30 dB of peak | Los Alamos equatorial work | 2σ × √(2 ln 10) = 2.15; 2σ × 3.03; 2σ × 3.72 |
| Composite multipath spread | DAMSON, Leicester | leading edge of first mode to trailing edge of last mode; ignores relative mode power |
| Effective / equivalent multipath spread | Angling; Leicester | mode separation weighted by the ratio of mode powers |

- F.1487 defines the differential delay as the delay between two modes and the frequency spread as the 2σ value of the Gaussian tap-gain spectrum — [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 3 §1 and Annex 1 eq. (2)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].
- DAMSON composite Doppler spread: each mode is replaced by a Gaussian with its measured mean and standard deviation, the Gaussians are power-weighted and summed, and the central 80 % power region of the sum is quoted. Composite multipath spread runs from the leading edge of the 80 % power interval of the first mode to the trailing edge of that of the last. The 80 % figure converts to 2σ by multiplying by 0.78 — [Angling 2001 thesis, §5.4.6 and p. 3-12](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- [E] Because each mode's Gaussian is built from its measured standard deviation, the factor 0.78 returns the measured 2σ exactly when only one mode is present, whatever the spectral shape; that σ is a second moment inside the analysis box, so it depends on the box and the noise threshold. For the Leicester estimator (narrowest 80 % of the measured spectrum) the factor holds only for a Gaussian spectrum.
- The Norwegian analysis of the same DAMSON data instead sums the modes on the frequency axis, assumes a Gaussian and quotes 2σ; multipath is first-to-last mode arrival — [Jodalen 2000, FFI/RAPPORT-2000/02687, §2.3](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader) [T; read, Norwegian].
- Leicester: Doppler spread is the narrowest spectral width containing 80 % of received power, noise-corrected; composite multipath excludes modes more than 12 dB below the strongest — [Stocker et al. 2013, ¶7–9](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005264) [P; read].
- NRL: delay and Doppler spread are twice the standard deviation of the measured profile about its mean — [Wagner et al. 1993, NRL/FR/5554-93-9575, §5](https://archive.org/download/DTIC_ADA273363/DTIC_ADA273363.pdf) [T; scan and Table 1 visually checked].
- [E] Consequences: the ITU 30 Hz (2σ) equals 38 Hz as an 80 % width, 35 Hz FWHM and 64 Hz at −10 dB. For a Lorentzian of FWHM Γ the central 80 % width is Γ·tan(0.4π) = 3.08 Γ (against 1.09 Γ for a Gaussian), so an 80 % estimator applied to a heavy-tailed spectrum reads about three times the half-power width.

### 2. High-latitude mechanisms

- Field-aligned F-region irregularities (about 10 km down to 1 m) are produced by convective instabilities on the gradients of large structures: convecting polar-cap patches, auroral boundary blobs, the poleward wall of the night-side trough, the poleward boundary of the auroral region and precipitation regions — [Wagner et al. 1993, §2, summarising Tsunoda's review](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read].
- Because scatter from field-aligned irregularities needs near-perpendicular incidence and field lines are near-vertical, higher frequencies must travel further before scattering: "slant-F" echoes whose delay increases with frequency. They appear at delays longer than the one-hop path and at frequencies above the great-circle MUF — [Wagner et al. 1993, §2 and §6](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read]; [Wagner et al. 1995, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/94RS03177) [P; abstract].
- Doppler spread arises from bulk drift of irregularity regions plus their internal random motion — [Wagner et al. 1993, §1](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read]. A fitted drift for one noontime scatter region was 1,200 m/s parallel to the path — [Wagner et al. 1995, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/94RS03177) [P; abstract].
- [E] Scale of the Doppler shift: for a scatterer of velocity v the shift is (k_s − k_i)·v/2π, at most 2v/λ (backscatter) and 2v·sin(θ/2)/λ for a scattering angle θ. At 10 MHz (λ = 30 m), v = 1,000 m/s gives up to 67 Hz for backscatter-like side-scatter and about 17–23 Hz for a long forward link (θ ≈ 30–40°). Short paths whose signals return from the oval well off the great circle can therefore see the largest spreads, consistent with DAMSON (below).
- The trough removes great-circle support at night in winter and equinox; signals then arrive by refraction in the poleward wall or by scatter from auroral irregularities, tens of degrees off the great circle, with longer delays and larger Doppler spread — [Warrington & Stocker 2003, ¶2–4, ¶15](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002RS002815) [P; read]; [Siddle et al. 2004, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004RS003049) [P; abstract].
- In the polar cap, patches (southward IMF, high activity, antisunward drift) and arcs (northward IMF, low activity, duskward drift) give arrival directions up to 100° off the great circle and delay spreads well beyond great-circle values — [Stocker et al. 2013, ¶4](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005264) [P; read].
- Sporadic E is almost always present under the poleward trough edge (90 % of occasions before midnight, 80 % after) — [Warrington & Stocker 2003, ¶4, citing Rodger et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002RS002815) [P; secondary].
- At VHF, reflections from field-aligned auroral ionisation occur typically when the local K index reaches 5 or more, last a few hours (often afternoon to midnight) and have a characteristic rasping sound caused by multiple Doppler shifts — [ITU-R P.844-1, Table 1 and §1.7](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.844-1-199408-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].

**Storm-time route availability is a separate evidence axis from scattering spread.** Continuous-chirp vertical and oblique sounding on Asian paths during 10–13 May 2024 records a shifting trough/auroral precipitation region, loss of regular F2 modes, intermittent F1/Es returns, diffuse returns, lower mode maximum-observed frequencies, stronger absorption, and long blackout intervals. The source supports a nonstationary mode/availability trajectory for the observed paths; it does not report a communication fading spectrum, a modem outage probability, or a joint high-latitude delay–Doppler sample. Do not label those blackouts as measured high-latitude Doppler-spread extrema [P; read: Ponomarchuk et al. 2025, full paper].

### 3. Equatorial and low-latitude mechanisms

- Equatorial spread F: structure from about 100 km down to 0.1 m at the reflection level turns specular reflection into strong scatter over a wide area, while the plasma drifts eastward at about 125 m/s; scatter from moving structure produces the Doppler spread. Bottomside spread F, not only the large plumes, does most of the damage — [Fitzgerald, Argo & Carlos 1993, LA-UR-93-2140](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/7368684) [T; read].
- Off-great-circle reflectors on a Japan–Australia path drifted eastward at mostly 100–300 m/s (median 200 m/s); events appeared abruptly after sunset to the west and peaked at the equinoxes, notably March — [Maruyama & Kawamura 2006, abstract and §4](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/24/1401/2006/) [P; read].
- A post-sunset rise of the layer at an average 38 m/s accompanied spread-F onset over Peru — [Fitzgerald 1994, LA-UR-94-1811, §3.1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/10156838) [T; read]. [E] For a 1,600 km path reflecting near 350 km (flat-Earth mirror geometry, incidence angle φ ≈ 66°, cos φ ≈ 0.40) at 13.37 MHz the shift is 2·f·v·cos φ/c = 2 × 13.37e6 × 38 × 0.40 / 3e8 ≈ 1.4 Hz (about 1.5 Hz with Earth curvature, φ ≈ 63°), the same order as the −0.5 Hz (one-hop) and −1.5 Hz (two-hop) shifts reported at the start of the record; the 38 m/s is an average over the following hour.
- Daytime equatorial sporadic E (electrojet irregularities) is a scatter mode that is semi-transparent and geometry dependent; Doppler spreads from it are expected to be largest on east–west paths — [Angling 2001 thesis, §7.3, citing Davies 1990](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read; the east–west expectation is the author's inference, not a measurement].
- Trans-equatorial propagation: by day the equatorial anomaly supports long-range signals that are narrowly spread and coherent, with maximum frequencies up to 60 MHz; after sunset irregularities give scatter-like propagation that distorts frequencies throughout HF and VHF — [Nielson & Crochet 1974, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/RG012i004p00688) [P; abstract].

### 4. Why severe dispersion and weak signals go together

- The widest spreads belong to volume scatter, whose peak level is about 30 dB (strongest) to 55 dB (weakest detectable) below a specular return on the same path — [Wagner et al. 1993, §6](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read].
- When a signal is highly Doppler-spread its components may fall below the noise floor and go undetected, which biases spread statistics downward — [Stocker et al. 2013, ¶24](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005264) [P; read; offered by the authors as a possible explanation].
- Low SNR, not dispersion, was the main cause of robust-modem failure on DAMSON paths — [Bergsvik 1998, FFI report, abstract](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/evaluering-av-hf-modemer-og-flerfrekvenssystemer-basert-pa-kanalmalinger-pa-hoye-breddegrader) [T; abstract].

## Measured and reported values

### A. DAMSON (Doppler and Multipath Sounding Network), northern Scandinavia

Instrument and data set — [Angling 2001 thesis, §5.1–5.2, §6.1, Tables 6-1 to 6-4](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read]:

SNR depends on the DAMSON power and antennas and does not transfer to other systems (thesis §6.4).
- H–K multipath is above 7 ms in every band and time block; the author attributes it to rays travelling north to the auroral zone and returning from a tilted layer (off-great-circle), noting that without direction finding the cause is uncertain — [Angling 2001 thesis, §6.3](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- Published summary: 5 %-exceeded Doppler spreads of 2–55 Hz and multipath spreads of 1–11 ms; availabilities of 64–100 % at 0 dB SNR when there is mode support — [Angling et al. 1998, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/97RS02206) [P; abstract]. The thesis tables reach 73 Hz and its chapter 6 conclusion says 2–73 Hz, while its chapter 11 repeats 2–55 Hz (discrepancy noted below). No entry of 55 Hz appears in thesis Tables 6-5 and 6-6: the H–K entries nearest below are 49.5–52.5 Hz and the nearest above 57.0 Hz, so the abstract probably rests on a data selection or processing version other than the thesis tables [inference]. The body of the paper was not obtained (the publisher refused automated access), and no source read states its selection. Its availability range is that of thesis Table 6-7, and a 1999 CRC paper cites it for Doppler spreading "greater than 50 Hz" — [Jorgenson & Moreland 1999, §3](https://www.n5dux.com/ham/files/pdf/HF%20Serial-Tone%20Waveform%20Design.pdf) [T; read].
- [E] In 2σ terms (×0.78; for single-mode measurements this is the measured 2σ, see §1) the all-frequency 5 % points are about 8.6, 12.5, 2.3 and 38.6 Hz; on H–K the 6.8–11.2 MHz band is about 27 Hz (00–24 UT) and 39 Hz (19–01 UT), and the 14.4–21.9 MHz band about 53–57 Hz.
- [E] Instrument ceiling: a spectrum that fills the ±40 Hz window uniformly has σ = 80/√12 = 23.1 Hz, which the composite (Gaussian-reconstruction) estimator reports as 2.563 × 23.1 = 59 Hz; values above that (64.5–73 Hz) need power concentrated towards the window edges or several modes with separated mean shifts (one mode with all its power at ±40 Hz would read 2.563 × 40 = 103 Hz). H–K values of 49.5–73 Hz, and multipath of 11.2–11.4 ms against a 12.5 ms window, are therefore at the measurement limit; true extents may be larger and aliasing may contribute.
- Availability of a MIL-STD-188-110A 75 bit/s modem (BER 10⁻³, performance surface measured at 0 dB SNR), given mode support: 93.8–97.0 % on the three longer paths (all frequencies, 00–24 UT) and 78.4 % on H–K, falling to 64.3–64.7 % on H–K at 6.7–11.2 MHz — [Angling 2001 thesis, Table 6-7](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- Robust modems are needed up to 90 % of the time on the four auroral paths; they should operate when Doppler spread exceeds 40 Hz and multipath spread exceeds 11.5 ms; multiple modes are common between the lowest and maximum usable frequencies; simulations for robust modems should include channel models with dominant modes — [Willink et al. 1999, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1049/ip-map:19990575) [P; abstract].
- By 1999 DAMSON had observed multipath lasting more than 10 ms and Doppler spreading above 50 Hz at high latitude, against 1–4 ms and 1 Hz or less on more typical mid-latitude channels — [Jorgenson & Moreland 1999, RTO-MP-26 paper 33, §3](https://www.n5dux.com/ham/files/pdf/HF%20Serial-Tone%20Waveform%20Design.pdf) [T; read].
- Two 75 bit/s modems (STANAG 4415, STANAG 4285) had 60–75 % higher availability than a 2,400 bit/s STANAG 4285 modem, which alone degraded during a geomagnetic disturbance; the 75 bit/s modems needed one frequency in quiet summer and winter conditions and four when disturbed — [Jodalen et al. 2001, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000RS002547) [P; abstract; the same statements are in the English abstract of the FFI report, read]. Periods analysed: winter 1997/98 (local K 0–1, with five moderately disturbed days at K 3–4; smoothed sunspot number 39–44), summer 1998 (K 2–3, 60–65), disturbed 26 August–3 September 1998 (K 5, 65–70); in that analysis the measurable limits were 12.5 ms and 80 Hz — [Jodalen 2000, FFI report, Table 1 and §2.2](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader) [T; read].
- Long delays (several ms) are regularly seen at midday on frequencies above the predicted MUF, possibly ground scatter; Doppler spreads of tens of Hz occur during substorms when the reflection point is within the auroral oval — [Jacobsen et al., ESA Space Weather Workshop poster abstract](https://swe.ssa.esa.int/TECEES/spweather/workshops/proceedings_w2/doc/poster_jacobsen.pdf) [G; read].
- A Swedish-Norwegian DAMSON study of 16–22 October 1995, when the geomagnetic Q index reached 7 for several hours, found Doppler spread and shift positively correlated with disturbance level — [Hervás et al. 2020, §3.3, citing Bröms et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20092486) [P; secondary].
- Earliest DAMSON results (Svalbard–Tuentangen, 2,017 km, 19–25 April 1995; Doppler spread as the central width holding 95 % of the boxed power, all modes pooled): spreads up to about 20 Hz at 9.04 MHz among 841 analysable measurements; widely spread components 12–16 dB below the peak of the mode; consistently larger spreads in the second half of the week, when the Kiruna Q index averaged about 4 instead of about 1, with medians above 10 Hz before and after midnight at the lower frequencies — [Angling et al. 1995, AGARD-CP-574 paper 17](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA310824) [T; read as OCR text]. [E] For a Gaussian the 95 % width is 1.96 × 2σ, so 20 Hz is about 10 Hz as 2σ and 10 Hz about 5 Hz.
- A ten-day DAMSON campaign at Kiruna in March 1998 with a six-element direction-finding array found most signals narrow in delay and Doppler, some cases with Doppler spread beyond a stated modem limit but none beyond the delay limit, an east-to-west trend of arrival direction with Doppler offset, and that spatial filtering with two or three elements reduced large Doppler spreads — [Jackson 2003, PhD thesis, abstract](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Combined_channel_sounding_and_direction_finding_studies_of_HF_radio_propagation_effects_observed_over_high_latitude_communication_paths/10127792) [T; abstract].

**Independent DAMSON processing check.** Willink's [CRC97-001 official report](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2020/isde-ised/Co24/Co24-3-7-97-001-eng.pdf), §1.2, Table 3.2 and §6.3/Table 6.3, is now inspected. Its April–October 1995 four-path measurements cycle ten 2–22-MHz frequencies, one minute each; six 1.6-s records give 0.62-Hz resolution and ±40-Hz Doppler coverage. Doppler width contains 80% of mode power; multipath spans first-to-last detected modes. Manual GOOD/DIFFICULT/BAD classes and a separate robust-modem subset change the denominator; BAD includes interference and no propagation. CW SNR and delay–Doppler measurements occupy different parts of a minute. Some upper percentiles exceed reliable instrumental width. The robust subset's Harstad–Kiruna 95th-percentile widths include 41 Hz at 11.2 MHz and 48.8 Hz at 17.5 MHz. These are conditional tails, not all-time occurrence probabilities or per-tap Gaussian 2σ. This report does not resolve Angling's 55/73-Hz summary discrepancy; neither figure is selected as a universal upper bound.

### B. NRL wideband channel prober (Wagner, Goldstein and co-workers)

- Trans-auroral, solar minimum: 2,300 km, Frobisher Bay to Rome (New York), quarterly October 1985–July 1986. In quiet conditions with the midpoint below the oval the channel resembled a mid-latitude or specular-multipath channel with small Doppler spread; at night with the midpoint inside the oval the return was diffuse and the Doppler spread about ten times larger — [Wagner, Goldstein & Meyers 1988, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/RS023i004p00555) [P; abstract]. (The roughly 1,300 km trans-auroral path is the 1992 campaign, not this paper.)
- "Northern Exposure 92": Sondrestrom–Keflavik, 1,294 km (about 1,300 km in the report), midpoint at 72° corrected geomagnetic latitude (both from the 1995 abstract), 13 March–2 April 1992, mean sunspot number 108, 97 experiments; the 1995 paper adds a noontime scatter event at Kp = 3. Extended-Doppler format: 61.27 Hz sampling, window ±30.64 Hz — [Wagner et al. 1993, executive summary, §4–5](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read]; [Wagner et al. 1995, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/94RS03177) [P; abstract].
- Three signal classes: strong specular reflection (quiet daytime; negligible Doppler spread), strong specular multipath from horizontal gradients (mostly night), and weak scatter with the widest spreads (night) — [Wagner et al. 1993, abstract](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read].
- At night (22–02 UT) 2σ Doppler spreads of 5–30 Hz and 2σ delay spreads of 0.5–1 ms were observed regularly; lower values when one strong feature dominates. Quiet daytime near the MUF with multipath: about 1–2 Hz and 50–200 µs. Midday unspread: 0.3 Hz and 30 µs, at the measurement resolution — [Wagner et al. 1993, §6](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read].

### C. SRI probe and earlier measurements

- One-hop F-layer transmissions in polar-cap, auroral and equatorial experiments were distorted by amounts of order hundreds of microseconds in delay and tens of hertz in Doppler; 20 kHz pseudo-noise probe — [Basler et al. 1988, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/RS023i004p00569) [P; abstract]. (Its title is "Ionospheric distortion of HF signals"; it is not confined to very high latitudes.)
- Average Doppler spreads of up to 14 Hz have been reported for polar-cap propagation; an example scattering function is from Narssarssuaq–Thule, 10.57 MHz, 20 March 1985; these early data sets were small and no cumulative distributions were published — [Angling 2001 thesis, §4.3, citing Basler et al. and Wagner et al.](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; secondary].
- Frequency spread measured over one-minute intervals exceeded about 1 Hz on a mid-latitude path and about 9 Hz on a trans-auroral path (Thule–Palo Alto) for 5 % of samples — [Angling 2001 thesis, §4.3.2, citing Shepherd & Lomax 1967](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; secondary].

**MITRE's resolved single-mode covariance evidence.** [Nissen and Bello2003](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2002RS002746), §§2–5/Figs.3–5, supplies a full primary body for the 3,100-km Sondrestrom–Bedford auroral campaign,14 March–1 April 1992. The 1-MHz probe samples delay at 0.488µs across 2 ms. Approximately 100 records remain after rejecting multimode returns. Their labelled 2σ Doppler-width percentiles 10/50/90 are 1.75/5/13 Hz; corresponding delay values are 17/~100/~400µs. These are selected **within-mode** statistics. The paper's longest 512-response duration is printed 30.768 s, although 512/15.625=32.768 s; retain the discrepancy.

For the illustrated 15.5-MHz record, §4 estimates mean-removed complex-response correlation over 200 delay samples and averages equal-separation entries. Magnitude falls to small values within a few microseconds, at the equipment-filter scale; several examples behave similarly. This supports uncorrelated scattering at resolved delays for this measured population, rather than proving it for every HF regime. It measures neither inter-mode covariance nor simultaneous widely separated carriers. No Gaussian/Lorentzian goodness-of-fit or held-out nonstationary generator validation is supplied. This source narrows the older statement that no within-mode evidence exists; its finite window and mode selection remain binding.

### E. SuperDARN spectral width as a proxy

- Spectral-width histograms of F-region backscatter (Northern Hemisphere radars, winters 1994 and 1996–97): at low latitude in the afternoon sector, equatorward of the high-width oval, the distribution peaks at 100 m/s (mean 192 m/s, under 5 % above 300 m/s); in the night-side region of enhanced width it peaks at 200 m/s (mean 250 m/s, about 22 % above 300 m/s); in the cusp it peaks at 250 m/s (mean 349 m/s, nearly 50 % above 300 m/s) — [Villain et al. 2002, §2.5](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/20/1769/2002/) [P; read]. These widths come from the fitting software of the time, which the next item finds biased upward.
- The standard fitting software overestimates width: subtracting the fluctuation level adds about 30 % (about 20 % at normal averaging), zero-lag noise about another 30 %, and the two together up to 100 %; in a 2-hour test set the corrected widths were 20–40 % smaller and essentially confined below 200–250 m/s. In their worked example a correlation time of 20 ms corresponds to about 200 m/s. Low-width echoes have Gaussian autocorrelation (random-velocity dominated); high-width echoes decay exponentially, i.e. a Lorentzian spectrum — [Ponomarenko & Waters 2006, §2–4](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/24/115/2006/) [P; read].
- Width distributions depend on radar frequency and range — [Vallières, Villain & André 2003, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2001RS002550) [P; abstract].
- [E] Conversion: half-power width in Hz = 2·f·W/c for backscatter. At 12 MHz, W = 100, 200, 250, 350 m/s gives 8, 16, 20, 28 Hz, i.e. correlation times of 40, 20, 16, 11 ms. A Lorentzian of 16–24 Hz FWHM has an 80 % power width of 49–74 Hz, numerically the range DAMSON reported on its 194 km path. The match is suggestive only: DAMSON's window truncates the tails and a one-way link scales by sin(θ/2).

### F. Geomagnetic control, onset, decay and duration

- No source read gives Doppler- or delay-spread percentiles binned by Kp. The nearest are the split of one DAMSON week into periods of mean Q about 1 and about 4, with consistently larger spreads in the second (§A), and the MITRE finding that the largest spreads coincided with solar flares and disturbed days (§B). What is documented is that severity follows the position of the reflection or scatter region relative to the oval and trough, more than Kp itself (items above).
- Diurnal window: 22–02 UT (about 21–01 magnetic local time) on the Greenland–Iceland path; 21:30–05:30 UT in the Leicester example; DAMSON used 19–01 UT as the expected worst block — [Wagner et al. 1993, §6](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf); [Warrington & Stocker 2003, ¶15](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002RS002815); [Angling 2001 thesis, §6.2](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [read].
- Minutes scale: a steep rise of the 2σ spread from 15 to about 30 Hz at 700 s and a mean shift moving at about 0.1 Hz/s (NRL single event, above); on a trans-equatorial link, spread-F plumes grew within a few minutes (§H).
- Hours scale: a six-hour Harstad–Kiruna record (17 November 1995) had about 1.7 h of high composite Doppler and multipath, then about 1.2 h of low spreads and high SNR, then rising multipath and falling SNR — [Angling 2001 thesis, §10.2](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- Run lengths of "usable channel" (20-minute sampling): for STANAG 4415 runs longer than 320 minutes dominate on both paths, shortening to 80–320 minutes in the disturbed period; for 2,400 bit/s runs are 40–320 minutes on the 2,019 km path, under 80 minutes when disturbed, and under 40 minutes on the 194 km path — [Jodalen 2000, FFI report, §9](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader) [T; read]. SNR and Doppler spread change considerably from one 10-minute interval to the next (same report, citing Willink & Landry 1997; secondary).
- Recent polar and auroral measurements (2015–2026) found here report occurrence and single events rather than a general communications spread distribution. Cameron et al. used a 577 km Qaanaaq–Alert HF link (4.6–18.4 MHz scheduled; about 2.5 years, with a long operational gap). Signals were 2 s Barker-coded transmissions, recorded at 50 kHz and filtered to 3 kHz; the analyzed parameter records were detection-conditioned and used an SNR >0 dB gate. Among detected 11.1 MHz signals in the April 2013–April 2014 year, >30° off-great-circle deflections were reported for 65.6% after weighting the changed transmission rates. This percentage is not the fraction of all attempted transmissions, a probability of modem outage, or a joint DATA/Helper fading distribution. In a separate operational-interval statistic, the median deflection exceeded 30° in 36.1% of the intervals at 11.1 MHz. Deflected signals had increased time of flight and Doppler spread and lower SNR — [Cameron et al. 2024, §§2, 4.1 and 4.3, Figs. 4–5, 7–10](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023RS007897) [P; full text inspected on 2026-11-13]. The scheduled frequencies were not simultaneous coherent samples, so this paper does not establish complex response covariance at triple-Robust carrier spacing. An Alaskan HF beacon network (2.9 and 3.4 MHz, 62–68° N, 1-minute cadence) showed on one evening (8 November 2023, F10.7 = 143) Doppler widths (FWHM of a fitted Gaussian) mostly of 0.1–0.5 Hz and at most about 1.9 Hz, and range widths up to about 18 km of equivalent monostatic range (about 0.12 ms of group delay [E: × 2/c]); spreading increased as westward convection strengthened to above 1,000 m/s and was largest on the paths nearest the poleward trough boundary — [Aricoche, Hysell & Morton 2026, §4 and Fig. 10](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspas.2026.1848834) [P; read; values read from the figure].

### G. Equatorial spread F on HF links

- Kwajalein sector, August 1990 (solar maximum): 700 km path Maloelap–Bikini reflecting near the magnetic equator, CW at 10.2 MHz plus a 60 kHz probe, about two hours each evening for two weeks. - Same campaign, conference version: almost every night was degraded; Doppler spreads above 20 Hz were reported as not uncommon; Doppler broadening grew from a few hundred mHz to nearly 20 Hz; coherence bandwidth fell from above 70 kHz to below 1 kHz and spatial coherence from above 0.5 km to below 50 m; the probe indicated delay spreads of about 1 ms. In the worked evening (10 August 1990, from 18:40 local) discrete multi-hop lines gave way to a smeared spectrum about 20 Hz wide roughly 50 minutes into the record, which then persisted — [Fitzgerald, Argo & Carlos 1993, LA-UR-93-2140](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/7368684) [T; read]. The level at which "20 Hz" is measured is not stated.

Gaussian-equivalent 2σ is 2.8 Hz from the −10 dB figure and 4.0 Hz from the −30 dB figure. These conversions assume the abstract's spreads are full widths, which it does not state, and the two medians need not come from the same spectra.
- Peru, January 1994: 1,600 km path Piura–Arequipa (midpoint 125 km from Jicamarca), five evenings; spread F strong on two, intermediate on one, weak on two. On 14 January the one-hop mode began to broaden about 32 minutes into the record (00:14 UT) and kept broadening, the fading rate increased, delay spread appeared on all frequencies of the 4–20 MHz oblique ionogram within the hour, and coherence between antennas 38 m apart dropped. The probe's Doppler window was only ±5 Hz — [Fitzgerald 1994, LA-UR-94-1811, §2–3](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/10156838) [T; read; qualitative].
- Thailand, DAMSON, 26 August–22 September 1997 (low solar activity: the smoothed sunspot number was 39–44 three months later, [Jodalen 2000, Table 1](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader)), about 600 km north–south path, 4.1–10.1 MHz: propagation repeated closely from day to day. At night medians stayed below 2 Hz although the 5 % level rose; Doppler spread plotted against mode delay peaked near 4 ms (2F2) and 6 ms (3F2). These are times of flight of individual modes, not differential delays (the daytime 1E mode arrived at about 2.3 ms). The link between Doppler spread and delay was inconclusive — [Angling 2001 thesis, §7.2–7.4](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read; chapter 7 does not restate which per-mode spread estimator is plotted]. Spread F is not reported as a feature of this campaign. [E] On a 600 km path a 1F mode reflected near 300–350 km arrives after about 2.8–3.1 ms, so 3F2 at about 6 ms lies 3–3.5 ms behind 1F and about 1.5–2 ms behind 2F2, when those modes coexist.
- India, Visakhapatnam (17.7° N, dip 20°), 5.5 MHz vertical-incidence Doppler radar: spread F came in two to five episodes per night at intervals of 1–2 hours; line-of-sight velocities swung between +70 and −60 m/s, and the width of the Doppler velocity spectrum was at least 50 m/s for spread F against 25 m/s without — [Reddi, Sarma & Niranjan 2009, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007RS003777) [P; abstract]. [E] At 5.5 MHz, monostatic, 50 m/s corresponds to 2·f·W/c ≈ 1.8 Hz and 70 m/s to a shift of about 2.6 Hz; the abstract does not define the width.

### H. Trans-equatorial propagation (TEP)

**Peru transition evidence, source-body qualification.** The full official [LA-UR-94-1811 report](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/10156838), 27 PDF pages, was re-examined, including instrument description and Figs.11–12. January 1994 Piura–Arequipa observations use a 1,600-km path; the 10-kHz chirp probe has 0.1-ms delay resolution, a 2.5-ms delay window and ±5-Hz Doppler coverage. The detailed January 14/15 transition shows Doppler/delay broadening and reduced 38-m spatial coherence after spread-F onset. The separate 6,700-km AVA link already exceeds the 2.5-ms delay window: this is censoring, not a measured full spread. Crucially, a+10-Hz peak in the CW analysis is identified as an alias artifact, so it cannot support a physical extreme. Although 26 h of probe observations include 15 h of spread F, the illustrated approximately one-hour transition is explicitly not representative. It supports a coupled transition mechanism, not percentile distributions or a unique S6-H covariance law. This primary report does not upgrade the still-unread 1999 journal article to full-text status.

- Afternoon type: peak 17–19 h local mean time at the magnetic-equator crossing; strong steady signals, low fading rate, Doppler spread about ±2–4 Hz; paths 6,000–9,000 km or more. Evening type: peak 20–23 h; high signal strength with deep, rapid fading at rates up to about 15 Hz and a large Doppler spread that sometimes exceeds 40 Hz; paths 3,000–6,000 km; supports higher frequencies — [ITU-R P.844-1, §1.4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.844-1-199408-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read]. This Recommendation concerns 30 MHz–3 GHz and does not state the frequency of those figures.
- [E] If the 40 Hz applies near 50 MHz and spread scales with frequency (same geometry and velocities), the equivalent is about 8 Hz at 10 MHz, 11 Hz at 14 MHz and 22 Hz at 28 MHz. Both assumptions are unverified, and the one HF trans-equatorial measurement of plume extent found (below) is larger.
- Low-latitude F-region scatter interferes at 1,000–4,000 km and a separate trans-equatorial category at 4,000–9,000 km, evening to midnight, around the equinoxes, with strong signals — [ITU-R P.844-1, Table 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.844-1-199408-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].
- HF broadcast signals over 8,000 km (Shepparton–Oarai): single great-circle path by day; after sunset, off-great-circle traces appear abruptly to the west and return gradually, often several in sequence; frequent at equinox (March strongest); attributed to plasma-bubble onset — [Maruyama & Kawamura 2006, abstract, §3–5](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/24/1401/2006/) [P; read]. No delay or Doppler spreads are given.
- A 12,760 km trans-equatorial Antarctica–Spain link was sounded from 2003 to 2014 (2–30 MHz, 10 kHz wideband, austral-summer campaigns) — [Hervás et al. 2020, §3.5](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20092486) [P; read]; [Vilella, Miralles & Pijoan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007RS003812); [Ads et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012RS005074) [P; abstracts]. Four campaigns of 2011–2014 analysed only signals with spreads inside ±3.5 ms and ±2.5 Hz and with SNR of at least 6 dB in 10 Hz. Composite (80 %) Doppler spreads had means of about 0.1 Hz (2010–11, mean sunspot number 42) to 0.5 Hz (2013–14, 123), medians of 0.25 Hz or less (about 0.5 Hz in 2013–14) and three quartiles below 0.5 Hz (0.75 Hz in 2013–14); night-time (20–07 UT) means reached about 0.6–0.8 Hz. Composite delay spreads averaged about 1–2 ms and reached 3–3.5 ms from 20 UT at 10–12 MHz — [Alsina-Pagès et al. 2020, §3–4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12172750) [P; read]. The window excludes larger spreads by construction.
- Hawaii (WWVH) to American Samoa, about 4,200 km across the magnetic equator, 10 and 15 MHz, Doppler and arrival angle observed for more than a year (most data at 15 MHz): post-sunset spread-F plumes in the Doppler spectra extended to about ±60 Hz and grew within a few minutes, with phase-path rates of about 600 m/s when depletions first formed; 10 MHz behaved better than 15 MHz — [Miller 2017, conference slides](https://ies2017.bc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/200/2017/05/10A1-Miller_IES_2017-Slides.pdf) [G; read]. The slides do not state the frequency or the level of the ±60 Hz extent. [E] A phase-path rate of 600 m/s is a shift of 600/λ ≈ 30 Hz at 15 MHz (20 Hz at 10 MHz).
- An Australian space-weather service note describes evening trans-equatorial propagation as deeply and rapidly fading and reports a Doppler spread of up to 2 kHz once observed on a CW signal, without frequency, path or estimator; its discussion of evening TEP concerns VHF and UHF — [Kennewell & Wilkinson, IPS educational note](https://www.sws.bom.gov.au/Category/Educational/Other%20Topics/Radio%20Communication/Transequatorial.pdf) [G; read].

### I. Joint statistics, scattering-function shape, spectra, amplitude statistics, SNR

- Joint occurrence: DAMSON published joint exceedance contours P(multipath > x, Doppler > y) per SNR band. The 194 km path is extreme in both dimensions; on the 2,019 km path Doppler spread is highest in the lowest band where multipath is also highest (multi-hop paths with control points deeper in the auroral zone) — [Angling 2001 thesis, §6.2–6.3](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read]. The tabulated numbers are axis intercepts of the 5 % contour, not joint extremes.
- In NRL data the widest Doppler spreads (20–30 Hz) coincide with 2σ delay spreads of only 0.5–1 ms, though low-level scatter extends over about 2.4 ms; spread factors (2σ delay × 2σ Doppler) are largest when no single feature dominates — [Wagner et al. 1993, §5–6](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA273363.pdf) [T; read]. [E] 30 Hz × 1 ms = 0.03, against 0.21 for the F.1487 point if 7 ms is read as a spread.
- On the Leicester polar-cap path Doppler spreads are larger but delay spreads smaller than on the trough path — [Stocker et al. 2013, ¶26](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005264) [P; read]. So "largest Doppler with largest delay" is path-specific, not general.
- Delay–Doppler coupling: the ITS model includes a Doppler shift that varies with delay to reproduce the slant that its authors say is frequently seen in measured scattering functions — [Vogler & Hoffmeyer 1992, §2, citing Wagner et al. 1989](https://www.its.ntia.gov/publications/download/92-284.pdf) [T; read].
- Multi-component functions: two scatter regions at ±8 Hz (NRL); a trough-wall return split into two parts with different Doppler shifts and azimuths — [Zaalov et al. 2007, ¶7](https://doi.org/10.1029/2006RS003611) [P; read].
- Spectral shape: 2σ measured directly on two DAMSON paths was consistently higher than 2σ inferred from 68 % and 80 % power widths, typically by 10 %, suggesting non-Gaussian components; a Voigt profile was proposed — [Angling 2001 thesis, §9.2, citing Moreland et al. 1995](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; secondary]. A Lorentzian spectrum roughly tripled the BER of a serial-tone modem relative to a Gaussian of equal FWHM at 2–8 Hz (same thesis, §9.2; simulation).
- Amplitude statistics: no measured amplitude distributions for these paths were found. Indirect evidence against pure Rayleigh: specular plus scatter mixtures (NRL classes), the recommendation to simulate dominant modes (Willink et al.), and F.1487 §3, which allows a non-fading specular tap.
- SNR: the DAMSON Table 6-6 statistics are for −5 to +5 dB in 3 kHz (Table 6-5 takes all detected SNR); Leicester for ≥ 4–6 dB; the Antarctica–Spain link for ≥ 6 dB in 10 Hz. [E] C/N0 = SNR(3 kHz) + 10·log10(3000) = SNR + 34.8 dB-Hz, so these correspond to about 30–40 and ≥ 39–41 dB-Hz, and 6 dB in 10 Hz to ≥ 16 dB-Hz. Only the Antarctica–Spain statistics, which exclude spreads beyond ±2.5 Hz, extend to the 20 dB-Hz simulated in the modem's HF rows ([00](00-method-and-modem-context.md), §4.1); no auroral or spread-F dispersion statistics were found at such levels.

### J. What the standards assume versus what was measured

- F.1487 Annex 3 (two independently fading paths, equal mean attenuation and spread, no shift): low latitude quiet 0.5 ms/0.5 Hz, moderate 2 ms/1.5 Hz, disturbed 6 ms/10 Hz; mid-latitude quiet 0.5 ms/0.1 Hz, moderate 1 ms/0.5 Hz, disturbed 2 ms/1 Hz, disturbed near-vertical 7 ms/1 Hz; high latitude quiet 1 ms/0.5 Hz, moderate 3 ms/10 Hz, disturbed 7 ms/30 Hz. Occurrence: low latitude, 4 ms and 3 Hz each exceeded about 5 % of the time; high latitude, 5 ms and 25 Hz. No origin is given for any of these numbers except notes tying five conditions (low-latitude moderate; mid-latitude quiet, moderate and disturbed; high-latitude quiet) to former F.520 — [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 3 §2–5](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].
- F.1487 describes its model as a stationary Gaussian-scatter model, valid for 3 kHz channels; its comparative test grid runs to 20 ms and 40 Hz — [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 1 §1, Annex 2 Table 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].
- Former F.520-2: good 0.5 ms/0.1 Hz, moderate 1 ms/0.5 Hz, poor 2 ms/1 Hz, and an optional flutter-fading condition of 0.5 ms/10 Hz; comparative tests to 50 Hz spread; simulators should cover frequency drifts of 0–7 Hz. No origin is stated for the flutter condition — [ITU-R F.520-2, Annexes 1–3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.520-2-199203-W!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read].
- STANAG 4415 is described as designed to tolerate, as minimum required performance, delay and Doppler spreads up to 10 ms and 50 Hz at 0 dB SNR — [Stocker et al. 2013, ¶25](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013RS005264) [P; secondary; the STANAG itself, a NATO document, was not obtained]. MIL-STD-188-110C requires its optional robust 75 bit/s serial-tone mode to meet the STANAG 4415 performance requirements without restating them; its own Table XVI requires the 75 bit/s mode, at the maximum interleaver, to reach a coded BER of 10⁻⁵ at 2 dB SNR (signal and noise in 3 kHz) on two independent, equal-power Rayleigh paths 5 ms apart with a 2σ fading bandwidth of 5 Hz, on a Watterson simulator (2 dB more is allowed for frequency hopping and 1 dB more for embedded modems) — [MIL-STD-188-110C with Change 1 (2012), §5.3.2.5, Table XVI and §5.3.4](https://www.sigidwiki.com/images/c/c8/MIL-STD-188_110C_CHG_NOTICE-1.pdf) [S; read]. DAMSON characterisations were used to define the requirements of STANAG 4415 and its descendants — [Hervás et al. 2020, §3.3](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20092486) [P; read].
- A typical MIL-STD-188-110A 75 bit/s modem measured at 0 dB SNR kept BER below 10⁻⁵ for Doppler spreads under about 20 Hz (80 % width) and multipath under about 4 ms; beyond 20 Hz its BER rose progressively towards 10⁻³, and near 4 ms of multipath it rose abruptly — [Angling 2001 thesis, §3, Fig. 3-7](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read]. This is the surface behind the availabilities of Table 6-7.
- Approximate tolerances of one manufacturer's modem: FSK 75 bit/s 5 ms/40 Hz; STANAG 4285 75 bit/s 6 ms/28–32 Hz, 2,400 bit/s 5 ms/4 Hz; MIL-STD-188-110B 3,200–9,600 bit/s 3.5–5 ms/0.5–3.5 Hz — [Warrington & Stocker 2003, Table 7, from Brown 2001](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002RS002815) [P; secondary]. Performance surfaces behind the DAMSON availability work were measured over 0–40 ms and 0–40 Hz — [Jodalen 2000, FFI report, §3](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader) [T; read].
- Exceedance of "30 Hz (2σ), 7 ms":
  - Not reached at the 95th percentile on the Leicester trough and polar-cap paths (≤ 15.7 Hz as 80 % width, i.e. ≤ 12 Hz as 2σ; ≤ 5.8 ms) nor on three of four DAMSON paths (80 % widths ≤ 16 Hz for all frequencies combined and ≤ 27 Hz in any single band; ≤ 5.7 ms; no-guard-band tables; with the guard band, which drops the least-spread measurements, Harstad–Tuentangen reaches 7.8 ms at 6.8–11.2 MHz in 19–01 UT), nor at the 90th percentile on the MITRE 3,100 km path (13 Hz as 2σ; maximum 17 Hz).
  - Exceeded more than 5 % of the time on DAMSON Harstad–Kiruna in each dimension separately (39–44 Hz 2σ-equivalent for all frequencies combined, 27–39 Hz at 6.8–11.2 MHz and 53–57 Hz at 14.4–21.9 MHz, but only about 5–7 Hz below 4.7 MHz; 7.4–11.4 ms), with the instrument at its limits.
  - Reached at the instrument limit in NRL night scatter at Kp ≤ 2.
  - Design guidance derived from DAMSON asks for more: above 40 Hz and 11.5 ms (Willink), 50 Hz and 10 ms (STANAG 4415 as reported).
- Low latitude "10 Hz, 6 ms": the Doppler figure equals the F.520 flutter value. The 6 ms is a differential delay, whereas the 6 ms seen at night in Thailand is the time of flight of a 3F2 mode, about 3–3.5 ms behind a 1F mode [E, §G]; no measured low-latitude differential delay near 6 ms was found (the largest are about 3–3.5 ms on the Thailand and Antarctica–Spain paths, and about 1 ms of diffuse spread in spread F). Measured spread-F spectra of about 20 Hz overall width are roughly 6–7 Hz as Gaussian 2σ if that width is near −20 dB [E: 20/3.03], so 10 Hz is not clearly exceeded in 2σ terms on the Kwajalein path, although plumes of about ±60 Hz on a trans-equatorial HF link (§H) would exceed it. F.1487 does not say what time base its 5 % (3 Hz) refers to: within the post-sunset hours of a solar-maximum spread-F season about half the evenings exceeded 3–4 Hz equivalent, yet averaged over all hours that could still be of order 5 % [E: 0.5 × (2 to 4 h)/24 h = 4–8 %].

## How the literature models or simulates this

- **Watterson / F.1487 Gaussian-scatter tapped delay line.** Rayleigh taps, Gaussian spectra, no delay spread within a mode, stationary; validated for 3 kHz; specular components may be added as non-fading taps — [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read]. Its validation rested on 36 minutes of quiet mid-latitude data on one 1,300 km path in November 1967, and the thesis judges it unsatisfactory for high and equatorial latitudes — [Angling 2001 thesis, §8.1](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- **Performance-surface method.** Modem SNR requirement mapped over Doppler spread × multipath on a Watterson simulator, then compared point by point with sounder data — [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 2](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) [S; read]; [Jodalen 2000, FFI report, §3–4](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/ytelsen-av-standard-hf-modemer-pa-norske-breddegrader) [T; read]. Known failure: composite multipath treats weak and strong modes alike and the method performs poorly when Doppler spread is present; an equivalent multipath spread weighted by mode power repaired agreement — [Angling 2001 thesis, §10.4–10.6](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- **ITS wideband model (Vogler & Hoffmeyer).** Each mode has a delay power profile (total and partial delay spread), a Gaussian or Lorentzian Doppler shape with half-width, and a Doppler shift that varies linearly across delay (slant). Fitted to NRL auroral near-vertical scattering functions from quiet to intense spread F; described by its authors as showing capability, not quantitative validation — [Vogler & Hoffmeyer 1992, §2–4](https://www.its.ntia.gov/publications/download/92-284.pdf) [T; read].
- **Replay simulation (SUEDE).** DAMSON parameters at 10-minute intervals drive the ITS model. Replay that includes the low-power delay wings of each mode, which fall outside the modem's equaliser span, degraded the predicted MIL-STD-188-110A BER by many orders of magnitude relative to a Watterson simulation — [Angling 2001 thesis, chapters 8–11](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/thesis/Measured_and_simulated_scattering_functions_and_their_impact_on_high_frequency_HF_systems/10162685) [T; read].
- **Leicester directional channel simulator and ray tracing.** Seven representative cases built from Svalbard–Kiruna and Kirkenes–Kiruna measurements (March 2004–May 2005; these dates were not confirmed on re-check) — [Warrington, Stocker & Siddle 2006, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005RS003294) [P; abstract]; [Hervás et al. 2020, §3.3](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20092486) [P; read]. Ray tracing through model troughs, patches and arcs reproduces off-great-circle delays and azimuths; a nowcasting model for polar air routes assimilates ionosonde, TEC and absorption inputs — [Warrington et al. 2016, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015RS005880) [P; abstract]. VOACAP-type predictions omit off-great-circle and poorly predict high-order modes — [Stocker & Warrington 2011, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010RS004482) [P; read].
- **Physics-based wideband simulators.** Gherm, Zernov & Strangeways (2005) full paper was read: its complex-phase/Rytov solution models a 3-D fluctuating ionosphere and demonstrates E and low/high-angle F modes on a modeled 1,000-km path. This is a synthetic example, not a measured channel fit; weak/moderate-scatter assumptions and example inputs are summarized in [33](33-hf-channel-models-and-simulators.md#4-physics-based-stochastic-simulators) [P; full paper, §§2, 4.1, 5]. HiCIRF: numerical ray tracing plus stochastic signal-structure computation, element-to-element, one-way and radar geometries, disturbed or undisturbed — [Nickisch et al. 2012, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011RS004928) [P; abstract]. Geometric-optics phase power spectrum for Doppler and angle spreading of auroral clutter, compared with measured radar data cubes — [Ravan, Riddolls & Adve 2012, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011RS004944) [P; abstract]. The latter two sources' validated domains and failures could not be assessed from abstracts.
- **SuperDARN autocorrelation models.** Gaussian decay when random velocity dominates, exponential when irregularity lifetime or diffusion dominates — [Ponomarenko & Waters 2006, §4](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/24/115/2006/) [P; read].
- **Recent high-latitude modelling** concentrates on coverage, MUF and mode structure (auroral E raising the MUF from 8.5 to 26 MHz in a ray-traced radar case; sporadic E in the Arctic), not on dispersion statistics — [Ruck & Themens 2021, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021SW002901); [Cameron et al. 2022, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jastp.2022.105826) [P; abstracts].

## What a high-fidelity simulation would need

Options are listed with their limits; none is established as best, and other structures may serve better.

**Inputs (per scenario)**
- Geometry class: long oblique auroral or polar-cap path; trough-tangent path; short path equatorward of the oval (side-scatter); equatorial one-hop; long trans-equatorial.
- Regime and its schedule: specular; specular multipath; scatter only (above MUF); a time line with regime changes over minutes to hours, tied to local time, season and solar-cycle phase.
- Per component: delay and delay extent, power, mean Doppler shift and its drift, spectral width with a stated estimator, spectral shape, amplitude law.
- Level: component powers relative to the specular level (to −30…−55 dB) and the resulting C/N0. Noise structure is covered by other notes.

**Model-structure options**
1. *Extended tapped delay line.* More than two taps, unequal powers, per-tap shift and width, optional non-fading tap, selectable Gaussian/Lorentzian/Voigt shape. Simple and standard-compatible. Limits: no diffuse delay continuum unless many taps; stationarity; needs measured power ratios, which are sparse.
2. *Scattering-function synthesis per mode (ITS type).* Delay power profile, shape, slant. Captures diffuse scatter and delay–Doppler coupling. Limits: parameters come from a handful of fitted cases; tap correlation across delay is unknown (the thesis notes this needs wideband data).
3. *Replay or parameter replay.* Preserves real time evolution and joint statistics. Limits: sounder windows clip the worst cases (±30–40 Hz, 12.5 ms); 10–20 minute sampling hides minute-scale changes; raw data sets were not found to be public.
4. *Physics-based.* Ray tracing through structured ionospheres plus irregularity spectra with drift (HiCIRF or complex-phase type). Gives geometry-consistent delay, Doppler and angle. Limits: needs irregularity strength, spectrum and velocity fields that are poorly constrained; validation against communications-band measurements is thin.
5. *Regime-switching wrapper.* A Markov or scripted schedule that moves among option-1/2 states (quiet → trough-wall mode appears 2–7 ms late → scatter only → recovery; or pre-sunset specular → spread-F onset within tens of minutes → hours of spread). Limits: transition statistics are barely documented.

**Ranges the simulator should be able to span (not all at once)**
- Doppler width to at least 60–80 Hz as an 80 % measure (about 47–62 Hz 2σ), since measurements were clipped near those values; trans-equatorial plume extents of about ±60 Hz point the same way.
- Mean shift to ±25 Hz with drifts of order 0.1 Hz/s and bimodal spectra (±8 Hz components).
- Delay extents to 12 ms and beyond, as discrete late modes and as a 1–3 ms diffuse continuum.
- Component powers down to −30 dB and lower relative to a specular mode.

**What it implies for this modem [E]**
- Cells (fine chips) of the HF rows are 16.7 ms. At 2σ = 30 Hz the 0.5-correlation time is 0.375/30 = 12.5 ms, shorter than a cell; at 57 Hz it is 6.6 ms. Energy from one keyed carrier spreads over tens of hertz, comparable with the carrier spacing of 75 Hz ([00](00-method-and-modem-context.md), §4.1).
- A 7 ms echo overlaps 42 % of the next cell and puts spectral notches every 143 Hz; 11.5 ms gives 69 % and 87 Hz. A flat-fading assumption fails even for a 225 Hz signal.
- A 25 Hz shift that wanders at 0.1 Hz/s is a tracking problem distinct from spread.

**Observables to validate against**
- Percentile tables by path class, band, season, time block (DAMSON Tables 6-5/6-6; Leicester Tables 1–6 and Table 2).
- NRL night values (2σ 5–30 Hz, 0.5–1 ms; scatter level −30 to −55 dB) and the exp. 43 time series; MITRE 2σ percentiles for the same campaign (Doppler median 5 Hz, 90th percentile 13 Hz).
- Equatorial: −10 dB and −30 dB widths (6 and 15 Hz medians), coherence bandwidth below 1.5 kHz, onset time scale.
- Always compute the same estimator, within the same Doppler and delay window and SNR selection, as the measurement being matched.

## Comparison with the modem's presets

The presets of [00](00-method-and-modem-context.md) §5 that bear on this chapter are the three HF and two Aurora presets; None, the Sub9 presets and EME focused do not apply. Four of the five carry ITU-R F.1487 numbers, which are the 2σ widths of two equal Rayleigh paths and their differential delay; DAMSON numbers are 80 % composite widths and first-to-last-mode spreads.

| Preset (Doppler, delay) | What the evidence of this chapter supports |
|---|---|
| HF best (0.1 Hz, 0.5 ms) | F.1487 mid-latitude quiet. Below every high- and low-latitude value found, even quiet ones: about 0.3 Hz 2σ and 30 µs, at the resolution limit, at quiet midday (NRL); median 0.5 Hz as 80 % width (Leicester trough path); quiet floor about 2 Hz 2σ on a trans-auroral path (MITRE). |
| HF moderate (10 Hz, 6 ms) | F.1487 low-latitude disturbed. The Doppler value lies within the measured post-sunset spread-F range (Gaussian-equivalent 2σ of 2.8–4 Hz on half of the Kwajalein evenings, about 6–7 Hz for the 20 Hz-wide smeared spectra, plume extents of about ±60 Hz on one trans-equatorial HF link). | HF worst (30 Hz, 7 ms) | F.1487 high-latitude disturbed. Exceeded at 5 % only on DAMSON's 194 km Harstad–Kiruna path, in each dimension separately, and reached at the window limit by NRL night scatter; not reached at the 95th percentile on the Leicester trough and polar-cap paths, at 5 % on the three longer DAMSON paths or at the 90th percentile of the MITRE path (13 Hz). Close to the worst Leicester 95th percentiles (13 Hz as 80 % width, about 10 Hz as 2σ, which the authors equate to this condition) and to the 5 % values of the three longer DAMSON paths (2.3–12.5 Hz 2σ-equivalent, 3.5–4.6 ms); far above high-latitude quiet conditions (F.1487: 1 ms, 0.5 Hz). Two differences of kind apply to all five. Each preset's Doppler value bounds the offset and wander of discrete rays whose direct path never fades ([00](00-method-and-modem-context.md) §5), whereas every measured value above is the spectral width of fading scatter, often a weak diffuse component 12–55 dB below the strongest one, or scatter alone; the simulator's lower-level Gaussian-spectrum option (2σ width) is the nearer analogue, subject to the Lorentzian and multi-component shapes noted in §I. And the measured statistics are conditioned on about 30–41 dB-Hz (DAMSON, Leicester), 10–20 dB above the 20 dB-Hz simulated in the HF rows at the default −164 dBm/Hz; the one set that reaches 16 dB-Hz (Antarctica–Spain) excludes spreads beyond ±2.5 Hz. For the 11.6 s bits of rows 4, 5 and 7, the diffuse spread of about 0.1 Hz that costs whole-bit coherence about 3 dB ([00](00-method-and-modem-context.md) §4.1) is exceeded by factors of about 30 to 700 in the adverse and worst conditions above.

## Contradictions, open questions, and things not found

**Contradictions and qualifications**
- DAMSON 5 % Doppler range: "2 to 55 Hz" in the journal abstract and thesis chapter 11; "2 to 73 Hz" in thesis chapter 6, whose tables contain 64.5–73 Hz and no value of 55 Hz. The paper's body was not obtained; the 1995 conference version uses another estimator (95 % power) on one path; the abstract's availability range matches the thesis. Probably a different data selection or processing version [inference]; unresolved.
- Four estimators are applied to DAMSON-type data (80 % of a Gaussian-sum composite; 2σ of the composite; 95 % of the pooled boxed power; narrowest 80 % width in the Leicester work). The 0.78 factor converts the DAMSON composite back to the measured 2σ exactly for single-mode measurements, but converts a Leicester 80 % width to 2σ only if the spectrum is Gaussian; for a Lorentzian the 80 % width is about three times the half-power width (§1).
- STANAG 4415 envelope: "10 ms and 50 Hz at 0 dB" (Stocker et al. 2013) versus the design recommendation "> 40 Hz and > 11.5 ms" (Willink et al. 1999). The standard's own table was not obtained; MIL-STD-188-110C refers to it without restating it, and its own 75 bit/s test condition (5 ms, 5 Hz, 2 dB) is far milder.
- Leicester reprocessing: the 2001 winter-night 95th percentiles of 12.8 and 15.7 Hz (2003) became 12.0 and 13.3 Hz after reprocessing (2011). The later papers say their ≥ 6 dB SNR selection is for consistency with Angling et al. (1998) and Warrington & Stocker (2003), but the 2003 paper states ≥ 4 dB.
- Thailand delays: the 4 and 6 ms of the thesis are times of flight of 2F2 and 3F2 modes, not differential delays (§G), so they do not match F.1487's low-latitude disturbed differential delay of 6 ms.
- F.1487 annotates its low-latitude moderate condition (2 ms, 1.5 Hz) as corresponding to the former F.520 "poor" channel, which is 2 ms, 1 Hz.
- F.1487 occurrence percentages have no stated time base (hours, season, cycle phase). The low-latitude "3 Hz at 5 %" is far too low for post-sunset hours in a solar-maximum spread-F season (about half of evenings above 3–4 Hz equivalent), plausible as an all-hours average, and compatible with the Thailand data taken at low solar activity.
- Leicester authors equate their worst 95th percentile (13 Hz) with the F.1487 high-latitude *moderate* condition; DAMSON's short path exceeds the *disturbed* condition at 5 %. Path geometry, not latitude alone, sets severity.
- Kp: severity does not rise monotonically with Kp. NRL's widest spreads were at Kp ≤ 2; the Leicester trough path shows smaller azimuth deviation at higher Kp; the 75 bit/s modems were unaffected in a K = 5 period, although Doppler spread and shift correlated with disturbance in one October 1995 storm, and one April 1995 week showed larger spreads at higher Q.
- Solar cycle: the trough-path winter-night 95th percentile at 11.12 MHz steps from under 2 Hz to about 11 Hz between sunspot numbers 65 and 105 (no data between; 13–16 Hz in the 2001 tables). DAMSON's 1995 statistics were taken at low solar activity [inference: between the cycle-22 maximum of 1989 (NRL report) and a smoothed sunspot number of 39–44 in December 1997–January 1998 (Jodalen 2000); no 1995 value was found in the sources read] and NRL's 1992 data shortly after maximum, so the classic DAMSON percentiles may understate solar-maximum conditions on some paths [inference]. Conversely one polar-cap frequency showed Doppler spread *falling* with sunspot number, which the authors attribute to spread signals dropping below the noise.
- SuperDARN spectral widths in the older literature are overestimated by about 20–100 % (Ponomarenko & Waters 2006): one case where earlier statistics overstate severity.
- Selection bias: every statistic is conditional on detection above an SNR threshold, and sounder windows clip the largest values. Both effects push published maxima down.

**Inferences and conjectures (not documented)**
- The F.1487 high-latitude occurrence figures (5 ms, 25 Hz) and low-latitude figures (4 ms, 3 Hz) resemble the DAMSON Scandinavian and Thailand results, and the DAMSON thesis evaluated a preliminary draft ITU-R recommendation on channel simulation (thesis §9); no source read or found states this origin.
- The F.1487 low-latitude disturbed Doppler value (10 Hz) may descend from the F.520 flutter condition; not documented.

## Additional geographic and radar evidence

The adjacent Astra chapter exposed three useful omitted leads; the primary bodies were then inspected directly rather than treating that chapter as new independent evidence.

**Nigeria daytime spread F.** [Olugbon et al.2021](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020SW002676), §§2–4, studies the Abuja–Lagos 6.957-MHz Doppler link and Ilorin ionograms for 23 July and 30 September 2019. Fifteen-minute ionograms required manual correction where automatic scaling selected a second hop. The September Doppler oscillations/broadening persisted approximately 14:54–17:50 UTC, longer than the ionogram disturbance; the July case included blanketing and structured sporadic E. A feature **centered near 1 Hz is not a 1-Hz spread**. These low-activity, daytime events contradict a universal postsunset-only scenario, while differing reflection points prevent direct identification of identical irregularities. They support a separate daytime S6 mechanism hypothesis; no complete CIR, amplitude covariance or occurrence distribution is inferred.

**Calibrated radar climatology.** [Ponomarenko & McWilliams2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023RS007657), §§2–5, uses 2008–2019 Rankin Inlet beams 7–8, alternating 10–11/12–13 MHz bands, FITACF3.0 and daily interferometer-delay calibration. Monthly hourly 8° phase histograms retain the modal bin only when it contains at least 10 points. This deliberately loses overlapping modes. The study separates seasonal Pedersen/low-angle behavior and warns that low-velocity ionospheric scatter can be misclassified as ground scatter. Its propagation-model derivation is deferred. Thus these data can constrain mode geometry, virtual height and skip range; they do not directly supply a one-way complex gain, modem Doppler width, or a joint outage law. Historical estimator/classification assumptions require version-specific treatment.

### Java NVIS: public received I/Q, with bounded usability checks

[Dear et al.2024](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023RS007877), §§3–4, describes the 87-km Pameungpeuk–Bandung path, transmitter/receiver coordinates (7.65°S,107.69°E)/(6.89°S,107.59°E), and 3.453/7.102-MHz sounding. ## Full QAN–ALE archive analysis and operational limits

The original six-file reconnaissance is superseded by an all-member inspection. On 13 November 2026 the documented [public version-download route](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/dataset/:persistentId/versions/1.0?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/RGDNR1) returned a 220,925,515-byte outer ZIP, SHA256 `d501984614873278746db8ba3a1aa9349c9a972c1cb432d2b1807edcafabf518`. **Schema and decoding.** All 5,588 MATLAB files decode into the same 28 named numeric variables and 1,597,429 received records. The scalar delay-spread fields each have 1,803 entries equal to 1000; `clock_oddity` is nonzero for 832 records. Flag them explicitly rather than winsorizing them into natural extremes. Stored `snr` spans 0.00494–7104.91, which is inconsistent with interpreting the stored values directly as the article's plotted dB quantity. A linear-power-ratio interpretation would give −23.06…38.52 dB, but no inspected dictionary certifies the exact conversion or the distinction from `snr_dd`. `sigpow` 28.17–200.16 has no verified dBm/terminal reference. Absolute delay also needs a processing-reference calibration: the later deposit's Ottawa–Alert example stores 2–3.2 in `toflight`, below the 13.86-ms vacuum transit time if interpreted directly as milliseconds. **Do not infer an absolute arrival-time or C/N0 scale from these arrays.** Relative stored values, source-plotted physical quantities and calibrated receiver inputs are separate evidence.

**Schedule and denominator check.** The source describes 30/hour on each original frequency, then 4/8/24/24/24/8 per hour after 15 December 2013. Timestamp differences independently show 120-s modal spacing in November 2013; in January 2014 they show 900/450/150/150/150 s for 4.6374/6.9544/8.0081/11.1184/14.3644 MHz. December 15 still contains 698 records at 4.6374 MHz, with 23 on December 16, so midnight on the stated change date is not a verified operational boundary. For 2016, [Cameron et al.2026, Table 1](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2025RS008395) supplies a later change to 8/hour at 4.6374 MHz; March 2016 timestamps confirm 450-s modal spacing. The 2024 article's simple “after” rate must not be extrapolated through 2016.

## Ottawa–Alert pulse archive and an absorption-event check

The separate [Gillies et al. data deposit, DOI 10.7910/DVN/TLAHPX](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/TLAHPX), version 1, was downloaded using the documented public Dataverse version-download API. Its ZIP is 70,660,699 bytes, SHA256 `cdb4807237ed0e79211d4764a5375dbe98ee1a9970ed3b7d2eb142a6e79104f9`; ZIP CRC validation passed. All 4,223 MATLAB files were parsed: 581,803 received records on 780 distinct dates, 19 December 2013–20 October 2019, in 2013/2014/2016/2017/2019. All have the same 28-field schema as the QAN archive; no producer dictionary, operation ledger or complex samples are included. This is a distinct path/population, not an independent replication of the QAN measurements. The newer KZAO3 G Ottawa subset is not the full Gillies population either.

**Baseline reconstruction and denominator sensitivity.** Excluding July–August 2016, as the article does, removes 105 files and 362 records, leaving 581,441 received pulses on 734 dates with any data. Modal timestamp spacing supports rates of `(4,8,24,4,24,24)` per hour in 2013–2014 and `(8,8,24,8,24,24)` in the later observed years, for `(5.3820,6.9285,8.0995,10.4220,11.1070,14.3644)` MHz. No SNR threshold was added. Two explicit reconstructions give:

| Assumed operating population | Expected pulses | Received | Unreceived fraction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Each frequency operates for 24 h only on dates with its own MAT file | 1,545,504 | 581,441 | 62.3786% |
| All six frequencies operate for 24 h on every date with any MAT file | 1,632,576 | 581,441 | 64.3851% |

The first reproduces the paper's approximately 62% pooled baseline after rounding, but this agreement does **not** identify its exact operating mask. The 2.0065 percentage-point difference is denominator sensitivity, not sampling uncertainty. Neither assumption establishes whether a missing pulse was caused by propagation, interference, equipment, thresholding or unavailable operation. Reproduction requires the exact active-time and coincident-riometer masks; missing files are not automatically ionospheric failures.

The paper's absorption-bin quantity is **normalized excess loss**, `P_rel=100(P−P_baseline)/(100−P_baseline)`, with probabilities expressed in percent; it is not a percentage-point increase in total loss. Its reported weighted values above 0.5 dB are 26–32% for most frequencies, with exceptions of 19% at 14 MHz and about 40% at 10 MHz (§4). These remain source-specific associations with four remote riometers, 900–1,700 km from modeled D-region pierce points. No universal riometer-to-communications attenuation or outage conversion follows.

**Actual selected-event join.** The illustrated 5 August 2017 event was checked against the deposited Ottawa records and the public [GO-Canada GO-Rio daily archive](https://data.phys.ucalgary.ca/sort_by_project/GO-Canada/GO-Rio/txt/2017/08/05/). Files `norstar_k2_rio-{chur,gill,rabb,rank}_20170805_v03.txt` give UTC, 30-MHz absorption in dB, raw voltage and baseline parameters. Their headers explicitly say the summary data have not been checked for errors. Five-second sampling supplies 17,244/17,280/17,280/17,240 samples respectively; short gaps are retained. A minute is usable only with all 12 finite samples, each with absolute absorption below 100 dB; no interpolation or clipping of negative absorption was used. This mechanical filter is not an independent instrument-quality certification.

At 5.3820 MHz all 35 received records that day have zero clock flags and timestamps congruent to 126 s modulo 450 s after UTC midnight. Under the article's continuous-operation assumption, use `126+450k` s as the scheduled phase. The interval 00:00–08:00 UTC then contains 64 expected pulses and 33 records; hourly received counts are `7,6,8,5,1,2,2,2` out of eight.
