# Public waveform alphabet research

This is a retained research note, not an operating or qualification guide. The
[current Robust architecture](robust-current.md) and [abbreviated testing
policy](triad-performance.md) govern new work. The old single-carrier receiver,
planner and standalone campaign tools have been removed; their results do not
qualify the current three-carrier stages.

The old public binary alphabet used repeatable amplitude/phase codewords. Its
construction is retired. The relevant lessons apply when comparing any current
three-carrier alternatives:

- A public deterministic waveform can be regenerated by an observer. Noise-like
  appearance is not secrecy, and data encryption does not make a public
  acquisition pattern secret.
- Candidate separation must be evaluated after nuisance phase, gain, timing,
  filtering and channel response are allowed. Different coefficient arrays can
  become difficult to distinguish under those transformations.
- An amplitude difference does not prove that an amplitude-only receiver has
  useful sensitivity. Conversely, discarding phase can discard discriminating
  evidence that remains useful to a complex receiver.
- Finite pulse support, overlap, carrier summation and clipping change sample
  amplitudes and sidelobes. A coefficient histogram is not the final ADC signal's
  distribution or occupied spectrum.

Current [independent APSK patterns](triad-patterns.md) use one native source.
Compare alternate receiver statistics on small matched observations from that
source; do not preserve or rebuild the old alphabet as a second implementation.
Keep public detector inputs distinct from evaluator truth and state the searched
hypotheses when reporting a probability or negative result.
