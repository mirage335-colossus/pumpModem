## Purpose and findings

The isolated fixed-bank study compared FP32 reference frequency contraction with explicit TF32, TF32x3, BF16, scaled-FP16 and split-BF16 tensor products, including optional fused first-advection. It retained all states and candidates. In 33 complete resident screen jobs, plain TF32, BF16 and scaled FP16 exceeded the predeclared likelihood-error/bias gate, while TF32x3 and split BF16 passed that development gate. A separate 216-case microdiagnostic covered arithmetic shapes and posterior patterns; it was not the resident policy screen. The passing modes are experimental options, not selected receiver defaults. Tensor contraction and advection were a small share of measured GPU work, so fusion's theoretical headroom was limited. The study did not qualify a speedup in operationally useful reception.

Fresh conditional-noise results and 128 sampled physical paths are summarized in the main report. Sensitivity-loss budgets of 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 dB remained unresolved. An overloaded capture was missed by every policy, and a signal-absent interference control crossed both gates under all compared policies; this demonstrates an admission-model limitation, not a tensor-specific error. GPU performance counters were unavailable, so utilization and achieved memory traffic were not measured.
